\section{Cross-Lens Study: Protocol, Controls, and Per-Family Results}
\label{app:cross-lens}

This appendix documents the corpus-conditionality study of
\S\ref{subsec:cross-lens}.

\paragraph{Lenses.}
We use the reference implementation of the Jacobian lens
\citep{gurnee2026workspace}, which reads an intermediate hidden state by
transporting it through an averaged Jacobian into the space consumed by
the unembedding and decoding through the LM head. On Qwen3.5-9B we fit
two lenses that differ \emph{only} in fitting corpus: 100 seeded English
C4 prompts versus 100 seeded Chinese C4 prompts, with identical seeds,
positions, and procedure. Transport is evaluated at layers
\(\{2,5,7,10,12,14,17,19,21,24,26,29\}\) of 31 at the final prompt
position; the \emph{mid-band} refers to the middle layers of this set,
where the two lenses' token readings can diverge (both lenses converge
and agree at the final band). Composition is validated numerically:
transported and normalized states reproduce the lens's own output scores
to within 0.2\%.

\paragraph{SRP basis and comparison operation.}
Because the implemented lens feeds the unembedding directly, the score
each lens reports is a selected readout score on the transported state,
and SRP decomposes it in the shared weight-only basis
(\S\ref{sec:method-comparing-accounts}). The dictionary is an
independently trained \(k{=}128\) (seed 0) readout factorizer for
Qwen3.5-9B---a different operating point from the main-table
\(16\times\)/\(k{=}256\) configuration, disclosed here per the
cross-recipe rule of Appendix~\ref{app:basis-stability}. No translation
pairs, language labels, or lens-fitting corpora enter the basis: it is
fitted to LM-head rows only. For each prompt, each lens's reported score
is decomposed at every mid-band layer, and the two lenses \emph{agree}
when the same feature id is dominant under both on a majority of the
mid-band layers.

\paragraph{Prompts.}
Eighty evaluation prompts span seven families---Chinese antonym (14),
English cloze (10), Chinese cloze (18), Chinese exemplar (8), Chinese
factual recall (10), and translation in both directions (10 each)---plus
12 control prompts (6 digit, 6 proper-noun) whose expected surface form
is language-invariant. Two null comparisons price in loose matching:
(a) \emph{within-lens unrelated token}: the dominant feature of each
reading is compared against the decomposition of an unrelated token on
the same state (159 comparisons); (b) \emph{shuffled pairing}: the two
lenses' readings are re-paired across different prompts
(240 comparisons).

\paragraph{Results.}
The same SRP feature is dominant under both lenses on 77 of 80
cross-lens prompts (96\%, CI [0.90, 0.99]), uniformly across families
(\Cref{tab:app-cross-lens-families}). Within a single lens, one feature
carries both surface forms (e.g.\ the English and Chinese realizations
of the same concept) on 61/80 (English lens) and 62/80 (Chinese lens)
prompts. Both null floors are empty: 0/159 within-lens unrelated-token
matches and 0/240 shuffled-pairing matches, so the headline agreement is
not an artifact of a permissive matching criterion. The lens-only
column shows the surface picture the decomposition corrects: on
factual-recall prompts the two lenses report different scripts on 9/10
prompts while the dominant feature agrees on 9/10. Worked example: on
the antonym prompt asking for the opposite of
\begin{CJK*}{UTF8}{gbsn}小\end{CJK*}, the English-fitted lens reports
\texttt{large}/\texttt{big} across the upper mid-band while the
Chinese-fitted lens reports
\begin{CJK*}{UTF8}{gbsn}大的/大\end{CJK*}; both readings are carried by
the same readout feature, whose top unembedding rows are the
\begin{CJK*}{UTF8}{gbsn}大\end{CJK*}~family.

\paragraph{Extension: a second language pair.}
The English--German bank removes the script confound: both languages are
Latin-script, so agreement cannot come from a script-level cue. Ninety
cross-lens prompts in seven families mirror the English--Chinese bank
(German antonym, cloze in both languages, German exemplar and factual
recall, translation in both directions), plus the same 12 controls.
Every decomposed surface is validated single-token under the Qwen
tokenizer, cognate pairs are excluded at normalized edit distance
\(\le 2\) after diacritic folding (30 of 132 candidates excluded; the
exclusion report ships with the bank), and surface-language calls are
lexical rather than script-based. English- and German-fitted Jacobian
lenses (identical recipe, seeds, and 100-prompt fitting budget) diverge
in their top-1 token readings on 73/90 prompts while the same SRP
feature stays dominant on 84/90 (0.93, CI [0.86, 0.97]); floors are
1/204 (within-lens unrelated token) and 0/270 (shuffled pairing).
Per family: German antonym 7/9, German cloze 14/14, English cloze
15/15, exemplar 7/10, factual recall 8/8, translation DE\(\to\)EN
17/17 and EN\(\to\)DE 16/17; controls 5/6 (digits) and 6/6 (proper
nouns). Within-lens cross-form carrying is 62/90 (English lens) and
53/90 (German lens), weaker than in the English--Chinese study and
reported as measured.

\paragraph{Extension: a second lens family.}
To test whether corpus conditionality is specific to the Jacobian
construction, we fit a second corpus-conditioned family: per-layer
bias-free ridge translators \citep{hoerl1970ridge} into the final-layer
residual basis, in the tuned-lens style \citep{belrose2023tuned} but
solved in closed form in state space, on exactly the same 100-prompt
English and Chinese corpora and layer set as the Jacobian lenses.
Ridge strength is selected per layer on a held-out 10-prompt tail;
holdout \(R^2\) rises monotonically with depth (0.46--0.92 English,
0.46--0.86 Chinese), and transported states match the model's top-1
token on 61--75\% of positions at the two deepest fitted layers. Under
this family the corpus-conditionality pattern reproduces (e.g.\
EN\(\to\)ZH translation prompts read Latin under the English-fitted and
CJK under the Chinese-fitted translator on 9/10), while the same SRP
feature stays dominant on 67/80 prompts (floors 3/159 and 1/240).
Finally, holding the fitting corpus fixed (English) and varying only
the construction, the Jacobian and ridge lenses read the same dominant
feature on 76/80 prompts with both floors empty (0/159, 0/240): the
feature account is transport-invariant across the two families.

\paragraph{Caveats and scope.}
The Chinese and German C4 fitting corpora contain incidental English
text, which biases the lens pairs toward each other and makes the
token-level divergence conservative. The study covers one model at one
fitting scale, now across two language pairs and two fitted-lens
constructions; the ridge translator is our minimal tuned-lens-style
instantiation, not the exact construction of
\citet{belrose2023tuned}. The released per-case records
include every prompt, layer, dominant-feature id, and top-token reading.
The conclusion licensed is the narrow one stated in the main text: in
this controlled setting the reported surface language follows the
fitting corpus even when both readings share a dominant LM-head feature,
so a surface-language reading alone does not identify the language of an
intermediate computation.
