\section{Beyond Token Identity}
\label{sec:beyond-token}
\label{sec:readout-analyses}

The validated feature space now pays for itself on the two ambiguities
that token identity cannot express: one token supported by different
feature structure in different contexts
(\S\ref{subsec:same-token-analysis}), and different surface tokens---here,
the outputs of differently fitted vocabulary lenses---supported by the
same feature structure (\S\ref{subsec:cross-lens}). The first is
presented as worked, audited case analyses; the second is a controlled
quantitative study.

\subsection{Same Token, Different Feature Structure}
\label{subsec:same-token-analysis}
\label{subsec:feature-competition}
\label{subsec:lens-to-sparse-account}

These analyses use Qwen3.5-2B, whose readout clears the interpretation
checks by a wide margin; they are illustrative worked analyses rather
than a systematic sense-disambiguation evaluation, and every label-level
reading is backed by the audit of \S\ref{subsec:label-audit}. In
\Cref{fig:main-lens-prism-comparison}, SRP holds the
\texttt{bug}-minus-\texttt{insect} score fixed and decomposes it into
signed readout terms plus an explicit residual, in logit units.
\Cref{fig:selected-readout-prism-examples}
(Appendix~\ref{app:additional-margin-case-studies}) sets it beside its
insect-context counterpart, where the competitor becomes the
software-sense anchor (\texttt{bug}-vs-\texttt{error}), and two further
polysemous-token differences (\texttt{bark}-vs-\texttt{dog},
\texttt{bass}-vs-\texttt{trout}), where a faithful account must reassign
signed terms across senses. In the insect context, \texttt{bug} draws
positive contributions from mosquito-, bee-, and beetle-labeled rows and
negative contributions from error-token rows (\(\tilde\rho=0.185\)); in
the software context, positive contributions shift to defect, crash, and
debug rows (\(\tilde\rho=0.003\)); \texttt{bark} and \texttt{bass} show
the same reassignment (\(\tilde\rho=0.126\) and \(0.097\); all four
within the reading gate).

Holding the token fixed isolates the context effect: the row-side
coefficients \(z_{t,i}\) are frozen by the token, and only the
projections \(p_i(h)\) vary across prompts
(\Cref{sec:method-local-score}).\label{subsec:context-reweighting} In
\Cref{fig:same-token-polysemy}, prompt context reweights \texttt{bug},
\texttt{ring}, and \texttt{bridge} toward features whose row labels match
the setting (each token score, after subtracting the shared offset, has
\(\tilde\rho<0.05\)): \texttt{bug} shifts from mosquito/bee to
defect/crash/debug labels, \texttt{bridge} from river/crossing to
networking labels. Context redistributes mass across a fixed token-row
decomposition without changing its row-side coefficients---a
reorganization the scalar token score cannot display. Additional views
are in Appendix~\ref{app:additional-qwen-display-examples}.

\subsection{Different Surface Tokens, Shared Feature Structure}
\label{subsec:cross-lens}

Fitted vocabulary lenses read intermediate hidden states by learning a
map into the space the LM head consumes and reporting the resulting
tokens; the fit uses a text corpus. The Jacobian lens is a recent,
contemporaneous instance \citep{gurnee2026workspace}, transporting states
through an averaged Jacobian before decoding through the unembedding.
Because the lens output is a selected readout score on the transported
state, SRP decomposes it in the shared weight-only basis
(\S\ref{sec:method-comparing-accounts})---and can therefore ask whether
two lenses that report \emph{different} tokens on the \emph{same} state
are reading different structure or the same structure.

\paragraph{Design.}
On Qwen3.5-9B we fit two Jacobian lenses that differ only in fitting
corpus: 100 English against 100 Chinese C4 prompts, same seeds, layers,
and procedure. Both lenses then read the same frozen hidden states from
80 evaluation prompts in seven families (Chinese antonym, English and
Chinese cloze, Chinese exemplar and factual recall, and translation in
both directions), plus 12 control prompts (digits and proper nouns) whose
surface form should not depend on language. For each prompt we compare,
across the mid-band layers, the dominant SRP feature of the score each
lens reports. The SRP basis is fitted to LM-head rows only: no
translation pairs, language labels, or lens-fitting corpora enter it.
Full protocol, per-family tables, and per-case results are in
Appendix~\ref{app:cross-lens}.

\paragraph{Result.}
The token readings diverge exactly as a corpus-following account
predicts: on factual-recall prompts, for example, the English-fitted lens
reports Latin-script top-1 tokens while the Chinese-fitted lens reports
CJK top-1 tokens on 9 of 10 prompts---a lens-only analysis stops here,
with two lenses apparently disagreeing about the model's intermediate
language. The decomposition says otherwise: the same SRP feature is
dominant under both lenses' readings on 77 of 80 prompts (96\%, 95\% CI
[0.90, 0.99]). On the Chinese antonym prompt asking for the opposite of
\begin{CJK*}{UTF8}{gbsn}小\end{CJK*}~(``small''), the English lens
reports \texttt{large}/\texttt{big} at the upper mid-band while the
Chinese lens reports
\begin{CJK*}{UTF8}{gbsn}大的/大\end{CJK*}; both readings ride one
readout feature whose top rows are the
\begin{CJK*}{UTF8}{gbsn}大\end{CJK*}-family. Within a single lens, one
feature carries both surface forms on 61/80 (English lens) and 62/80
(Chinese lens) prompts. The agreement is not an artifact of loose
matching: the dominant feature matches an unrelated token's decomposition
in 0/159 within-lens comparisons, and re-pairing lenses across shuffled
prompts matches in 0/240. The controls behave: digit and proper-noun
prompts, where no language flip is expected, agree at 5/6 and 5/6.

\paragraph{Reading.}
In this controlled setting, the reported surface language follows the
lens's fitting corpus even when both readings are supported by the same
dominant LM-head feature. An English surface reading from a fitted lens
is therefore not, by itself, evidence of an English intermediate
computation---a distinction that matters wherever lens token readings
feed working-language audits or monitoring. We do not claim that models
never privilege one language, nor that the shared feature is a
language-neutral causal representation; the claim is scoped to the tested
lens construction, model, and language pair, and the lens-only baseline
is reported beside the decomposition throughout
(Appendix~\ref{app:cross-lens}).
