\section{Corpus Conditionality: A Fitted Lens Reports the Language of Its Corpus}
\label{sec:beyond-token}
\label{sec:readout-analyses}
\label{subsec:cross-lens}

Fitted vocabulary lenses transport an intermediate hidden state into the
space the LM head reads and report the resulting tokens. Those tokens
are read both as predictions of what a model will say and as
evidence about the state itself: which distinction it carries, and which
language the model is operating in. The second reading has a dependency
the token report does not expose. The transport is fitted on a corpus,
so the reported surface form is a property of the instrument as much as
of the state, and a token ranking cannot separate the two. We call that
possibility \emph{corpus conditionality}, and this section tests it.

Two dependencies are worth separating. A fitted lens inherits its
fitting corpus through \(T_\ell\)
(\S\ref{sec:method-comparing-accounts}), and this section measures that
dependence directly. The logit lens has no fitting corpus and is not
exposed to it. The results below are claims about fitted transports
only.

The test takes up the second of the two ambiguities token identity
cannot express, that of different surface tokens supported by the same
readout structure. (The converse case, one token drawing on different structure
in different contexts, is treated in
\S\ref{subsec:same-token-analysis}.) A lens output is a selected readout
score on the transported state, so SRP decomposes it in the shared
weight-only basis (\S\ref{sec:method-comparing-accounts}) and can
therefore ask whether two lenses that report \emph{different} tokens on
the \emph{same} state are reading different structure or the same
structure.

\paragraph{Design.}
The Jacobian lens is a recent, contemporaneous
construction that transports states through an averaged Jacobian before
decoding through the unembedding \citep{gurnee2026workspace}. On
Qwen3.5-9B we fit two, which differ
only in fitting corpus, 100 English against 100 Chinese C4 prompts, with
the same seeds, layers, and procedure. Both lenses then read the same frozen
hidden states from 80 evaluation prompts spanning seven families
(antonym, cloze, exemplar, factual recall, and translation in both
directions), plus 12 control prompts (digits and proper nouns) whose
surface form should not depend on language. The antonym family includes
the prompt \citet{gurnee2026workspace} use to illustrate an
English-language intermediate reading on a Chinese-language prompt, and
that inference is the one this section places under a corpus control. Their
reading is reported on a different model, whose fitting corpus we cannot
inspect, so what we test is the form of the inference rather than their
measurement. For each prompt we compare the dominant SRP feature of the
score each lens reports, across the mid-band layers (the middle layers
of the decoded set, where the two lenses' token readings can diverge).
The design separates two hypotheses: if the reported surface form were a
property of the state, the two lenses would report the same tokens, and
if the feature account tracked the instrument rather than the state, the
dominant feature would flip with the fitting corpus. Two further
manipulations vary the same design along its other axis, the lens
construction, and are reported below. The SRP basis is
fitted to LM-head rows only. No translation pairs, language labels, or
lens-fitting corpora enter it. Appendix~\ref{app:cross-lens} gives the
protocol, per-family tables, and per-case results.

\paragraph{The token readings contradict each other.}
The two lenses differ only in fitting corpus, yet on the same frozen
states their mid-band readings disagree. In
\Cref{fig:cross-lens-butterfly} the English-fitted lens reads
\texttt{London} where the Chinese-fitted lens reads
\begin{CJK*}{UTF8}{gbsn}伦敦\end{CJK*}, and on factual-recall prompts
the two report different scripts on 9 of 10 (39/80 across all families
on the strict mid-band vote, with the lenses converging to the same
tokens only at the final band). Read alone, either lens licenses a confident
conclusion about the model's intermediate working language, and the
two conclusions contradict. Token rankings can show \emph{that} the
lenses disagree, but not whether the same computation underlies both
readings.
On the published antonym prompt, the unfitted logit lens settles which
of the two readings the fitting corpus produced. It reads a Chinese
form at every mid-band layer
(\begin{CJK*}{UTF8}{gbsn}大的\end{CJK*} at layer 24,
\begin{CJK*}{UTF8}{gbsn}大\end{CJK*} at 26 and 29, reaching 97\% at
29), and the English form never leads. The logit lens is not a neutral
arbiter, since applying the readout unchanged biases it toward the model's
own final token, but it is the one reading here that no choice of
fitting corpus could have shaped.

\paragraph{The feature account does not move.}
The same SRP feature is dominant under both lenses' readings on 77 of
80 prompts (96\%, 95\% CI [0.90, 0.99]). In
\Cref{fig:cross-lens-butterfly}, one shared feature carries 75\% and
63\% of the two readings' feature sums while every other feature
contributes at most \(+1.7\). Within a single lens, one
feature carries both surface forms on 61/80 (English lens) and 62/80
(Chinese lens) prompts. Two controls rule out loose matching. The
dominant feature matches an unrelated token's decomposition
in 0/159 within-lens comparisons (the \emph{unrelated-token} control),
and re-pairing lenses across shuffled prompts matches in 0/240 (the
\emph{shuffled-pairing} control). Digit and proper-noun controls, where
no language flip is expected, agree at 5/6 and 5/6. The claim this
supports is invariance rather than discovery. The factorization is not
required to reveal that translation-equivalent rows share structure,
only to name the carrier they share and measure its dominance, and that
coordinate stays fixed while the lens's surface report changes.

\paragraph{Corpus conditionality is not specific to one lens construction.}
Replacing the lens construction while holding the corpora fixed
reproduces both halves of the result. Per-layer ridge translators in the
tuned-lens style \citep{belrose2023tuned}, fitted on the same English
and Chinese prompt sets, again report the language of their fitting
corpus (on 9/10 EN\(\to\)ZH translation prompts) and again share a
dominant feature across the pair, on 67/80
(\Cref{fig:cross-lens-extension}; construction rules and tables in
Appendix~\ref{app:cross-lens}). Removing the script cue leaves the
result intact, since on 90 English--German prompts with cognates
excluded by construction the two Jacobian lenses report different top-1
tokens on 73/90 with no orthographic signal connecting the readings, and
the dominant feature nonetheless agrees on 84/90. Holding the corpus fixed
and varying only the construction leaves the dominant feature in place
on 76/80. The two axes of the design therefore separate. The surface
reading moves with the fitting corpus, while the feature account moves
with neither corpus nor construction, and every cell sits far above its
matched floors.

\paragraph{What this asks of lens practice.}
In this controlled setting the reported surface language follows the
lens's fitting corpus even when both readings are carried by the same
dominant LM-head feature, so an English surface reading is not, by itself,
evidence of an English intermediate computation. That distinction
matters wherever lens token readings feed working-language audits or
monitoring.

The reading under test is a published
one. \citet{gurnee2026workspace} read an English
intermediate representation off a Chinese-language prompt and take it as
evidence about where the computation happens. On that prompt, under
their own lens implementation, the reported language moves with the
fitting corpus, and the unfitted lens never leads with the English form.
Their reading was obtained on a different model whose fitting corpus we
cannot inspect, so this does not show it to be an artifact. It shows
that the token evidence does not settle the question on its own, and
that the control which would settle it, one refit and one re-read, was
not run.

The same inference appears in the latent-language literature
\citep{wendler2024llamas,schut2025english}, which reads through the
unembedding directly. Those lenses have no fitting corpus and are not
exposed to corpus conditionality. A weaker and separate confound applies
to them instead, since which of several realizations surfaces in a
ranking is fixed by the row codes of \(\WU\) alone, and one readout
feature carries both realizations on 61/80 and 62/80 of the within-lens
comparisons here. \citet{dumas2025tongue} establish language-agnostic
concept representations causally, by activation patching, without
relying on a ranking at all. We do not claim that models never privilege
one language, nor that the shared feature is a language-neutral causal
representation, and the lens-only baseline is reported beside the
decomposition throughout (Appendix~\ref{app:cross-lens}). Three changes
to practice follow, each testable. Vary the fitting corpus and report
the per-corpus spread beside the reading, since a claim about a model
should survive reasonable corpus choices. Score a reading against the
structure that carries it rather than its spelling alone, because a
criterion anchored to one surface form counts a sibling form carried by
the same feature as a miss. Attach a residual to every reading, since a
decomposition that closes exactly gives each reading a reliability
signal of its own.
