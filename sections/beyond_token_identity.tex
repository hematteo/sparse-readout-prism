\section{The Reported Token Can Follow the Lens Corpus}
\label{sec:beyond-token}
\label{sec:readout-analyses}
\label{subsec:cross-lens}

We now put the validated feature space to work on the two ambiguities
that token identity cannot express. That one token can draw on
different feature structure in different contexts appears directly in
worked, audited cases on Qwen3.5-2B
(\S\ref{subsec:interpretation-boundary}; labels audited as in
\S\ref{subsec:label-audit}):\label{subsec:same-token-analysis}\label{subsec:feature-competition}\label{subsec:lens-to-sparse-account}
the software-context \texttt{bug}-minus-\texttt{insect} margin of
\Cref{fig:main-lens-prism-comparison} is carried by defect, crash, and
debug rows (\(\tilde\rho=0.003\)); in an insect context, against the
software-sense anchor \texttt{error}, the same token's support comes
from mosquito-, bee-, and beetle-labeled rows (\(\tilde\rho=0.185\));
and \texttt{bark} and \texttt{bass} reassign signed terms across senses
the same way (\(\tilde\rho=0.126\), \(0.097\);
\Cref{fig:selected-readout-prism-examples},
Appendix~\ref{app:additional-margin-case-studies}). With the token held
fixed, the row-side coefficients \(z_{t,i}\) are frozen and only the
projections \(p_i(h)\) vary
(\Cref{sec:method-local-score}):\label{subsec:context-reweighting}
context reweights \texttt{bug}, \texttt{ring}, and \texttt{bridge}
toward features whose row labels match the setting (each score
\(\tilde\rho<0.05\); Appendix Figure~\ref{fig:same-token-polysemy},
Appendix~\ref{app:additional-qwen-display-examples}). These are
illustrative worked analyses, not a systematic sense-disambiguation
evaluation. The converse ambiguity---different surface tokens supported
by the same feature structure---admits a controlled quantitative study,
and that study is this section's subject.

Fitted vocabulary lenses read intermediate hidden states by learning a
corpus-fitted map into the space the LM head reads and reporting the
resulting tokens. The Jacobian lens is a recent,
contemporaneous instance \citep{gurnee2026workspace}, transporting states
through an averaged Jacobian before decoding through the unembedding.
Because the lens output is a selected readout score on the transported
state, SRP decomposes it in the shared weight-only basis
(\S\ref{sec:method-comparing-accounts})---and can therefore ask whether
two lenses that report \emph{different} tokens on the \emph{same} state
are reading different structure or the same structure.

\paragraph{Design.}
On Qwen3.5-9B we fit two Jacobian lenses that differ only in fitting
corpus: 100 English against 100 Chinese C4 prompts, same seeds, layers,
and procedure. Both lenses then read the same frozen hidden states from
80 evaluation prompts in seven families (Chinese antonym, English and
Chinese cloze, Chinese exemplar and factual recall, and translation in
both directions), plus 12 control prompts (digits and proper nouns) whose
surface form should not depend on language. For each prompt we compare,
across the mid-band layers (where the two lenses' token readings can
diverge), the dominant SRP feature of the score each lens reports. The
SRP basis is fitted to LM-head rows only: no translation pairs, language
labels, or lens-fitting corpora enter it. Protocol, per-family tables,
and per-case results: Appendix~\ref{app:cross-lens}.

\paragraph{Result.}
The token readings diverge as expected if each lens follows its
fitting corpus: on factual-recall prompts, for example, the English-fitted lens
reports Latin-script top-1 tokens while the Chinese-fitted lens reports
CJK top-1 tokens on 9 of 10 prompts. There a lens-only analysis
stops: two lenses apparently disagreeing about the model's
intermediate language. The decomposition says otherwise: the same SRP feature is
dominant under both lenses' readings on 77 of 80 prompts (96\%, 95\% CI
[0.90, 0.99]; \Cref{fig:cross-lens-butterfly}). Within a single lens, one
feature carries both surface forms on 61/80 (English lens) and 62/80
(Chinese lens) prompts. The agreement is not an artifact of loose
matching: the dominant feature matches an unrelated token's decomposition
in 0/159 within-lens comparisons (the \emph{unrelated-token} control),
and re-pairing lenses across shuffled prompts matches in 0/240 (the
\emph{shuffled-pairing} control). The control prompts behave as designed: digit and proper-noun
prompts, where no language flip is expected, agree at 5/6 and 5/6.

\paragraph{Second pair, second family.}
Both axes of the design extend (protocol, construction rules, and tables
in Appendix~\ref{app:cross-lens}). On a same-script pair---90
English--German prompts with cognates excluded by construction, so no
script or surface cue connects the two readings---English- and
German-fitted Jacobian lenses agree on the dominant feature for 84/90
prompts (0.93, CI [0.86, 0.97]; floors 1/204 and 0/270). Replacing the
lens construction while keeping the corpora---per-layer ridge
translators in the tuned-lens style \citep{belrose2023tuned}, fitted on
the same English and Chinese prompt sets---reproduces corpus
conditionality with shared dominant features on 67/80 (floors 3/159,
1/240). And holding the corpus fixed while varying only the
construction, the Jacobian and ridge lenses read the same dominant
feature on 76/80 prompts (floors 0/159, 0/240). The feature account,
unlike the surface reading, is invariant to both the fitting corpus and
the lens construction.

\paragraph{Reading.}
We call this \emph{corpus conditionality}: in this controlled setting,
the reported surface language follows the lens's fitting corpus even
when both readings ride the same dominant LM-head feature. An English surface reading from a fitted lens
is therefore not, by itself, evidence of an English intermediate
computation---a distinction that matters wherever lens token readings
feed working-language audits or monitoring. We do not claim that models
never privilege one language, nor that the shared feature is a
language-neutral causal representation; the lens-only baseline is
reported beside the decomposition throughout
(Appendix~\ref{app:cross-lens}).
