\section*{Limitations}

\paragraph{Scope of explanation.}
SRP targets linear scalar readout questions at the final LM head: which sparse
readout directions support or oppose a chosen token logit, contrast, or margin
for a given hidden state. The feature-resolved DLA analysis composes this
readout account with additive residual-stream attribution, yielding candidates
for later intervention studies rather than a standalone causal circuit claim.

\paragraph{Fidelity and labels.}
As in other sparse dictionary settings, the learned basis can depend on recipe
choices
\citep{elhage2022superposition,karvonen2025saebench,makelov2024principled}. We
therefore report reconstruction error, sign agreement, replacement fidelity, and
stability checks with the decompositions themselves. Feature labels are
row-level summaries of high-scoring unembedding rows; the measured objects are
feature ids, signed terms, and residual errors.

\paragraph{Readout-specific caveats.}
Because SRP factorizes vocabulary rows, some labels and groupings can reflect
tokenizer geometry, token frequency, row norms, or surface-form artifacts
\citep{press2017outputembedding,inan2017tying,yang2018softmaxbottleneck,bostrom2020bpe}.
Group contrasts reduce dependence on any single row, while benchmark-format
targets measure reconstruction of task-shaped readout scores rather than
end-to-end task solving. The readout-edit experiment is likewise a constrained
test of lexical score movement, with broader behavioral effects left to direct
intervention and generation-level evaluation.

\section*{Ethics Statement}

This work is an interpretability study of pretrained language-model readouts. We
do not collect new human-subject data or evaluate deployed systems. The
experiments use public model checkpoints and held-out text-derived hidden states,
including C4 continuations; as a web-crawl-derived resource, C4 may contain
offensive, private, copyrighted, or otherwise sensitive text
\citep{raffel2020exploring,dodge2021documenting}. We use these data only to
measure readout reconstruction and do not make claims about the social validity
of individual tokens, labels, or generated outputs.

SRP can make lexical and semantic readout directions easier to inspect and, in
the readout-edit stress test, easier to move. This has dual-use implications:
the same diagnostic tools that support auditing could also inform attempts to
manipulate model outputs. We therefore frame the edit experiment narrowly, report
distribution shift and off-target probes, and do not present the method as a
safety filter, moderation system, or deployment-ready control mechanism.

\section*{Reproducibility Statement}

The main experiments report exact-versus-reconstructed scores, sign agreement,
relative reconstruction error, and null/reference baselines for the scalar
targets being interpreted. Appendices~\ref{app:model-suite-selection}--%
\ref{app:robustness-controls} specify the model suite, dictionary widths,
TopK budgets, seed convention, row sampling, selection order, tokenization
filters, target construction, and denominator controls. Dictionary selection
uses row reconstruction, replacement fidelity, and sparse code usage; held-out
logit-difference targets are reserved for later fidelity checks.

The main remaining reproducibility risks are computational cost, checkpoint and
tokenizer versioning, and tokenizer-specific filtering decisions for
single-token targets. For this reason, local score accounts include explicit
reconstruction errors, and qualitative interpretations are restricted to
operating regimes where the selected target is reconstructed with sufficient
fidelity.
