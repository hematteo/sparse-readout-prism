\section*{Limitations}

\paragraph{Scope of score decompositions.}
The fixed SRP basis gives local score decompositions only after selecting a
token logit, logit difference, or other fixed linear combination of LM-head
rows. Features are learned from LM-head rows; hidden states enter only through
projections onto those feature directions, so the selected direction fixes
signed support and opposition, while residuals govern whether term-level
interpretation is trustworthy. Feature-resolved DLA attributes the selected readout score additively to
residual-stream components and to SRP features within those components; it
nominates candidate directions but does not establish that any component is
causally necessary. The readout-side edit is a constrained intervention on
selected decoder directions in the final hidden state; it nominates candidate
edit directions and reports the induced score shift and distributional
side-effects, but does not establish causal sufficiency or downstream
generation effects.

\paragraph{Fidelity and labels.}
SRP separates the measured coordinate objects --- feature ids, signed terms,
and residual errors --- from label summaries of high-scoring unembedding rows.
As in other sparse dictionary settings, the learned basis can depend on recipe
choices
\citep{elhage2022superposition,karvonen2025saebench,makelov2024principled};
reconstruction error, sign agreement, replacement fidelity, and stability
checks are reported so that interpretation tracks the measured objects rather
than the labels.

\paragraph{Readout-specific caveats.}
The selected-score interface admits token-level, group-contrast, and
benchmark-derived readout decompositions, and inherits vocabulary-geometry
caveats: labels and groupings can reflect tokenizer geometry, token frequency,
row norms, or surface-form artifacts
\citep{press2017outputembedding,inan2017tying,yang2018softmaxbottleneck,bostrom2020bpe}.
Group contrasts reduce single-row dependence; benchmark-derived contrasts
measure linear readout reconstruction, not task solving; labels remain
summaries of row geometry rather than ground-truth semantics.
Additional scope and future-work discussion appears in
Appendix~\ref{app:extended-discussion-limitations}.

\section*{Ethical Considerations}

This work studies pretrained language-model readouts, collects no new
human-subject data, and evaluates no deployed systems. Experiments use public
checkpoints and held-out text-derived hidden states, including C4 continuations,
which may contain offensive, private, copyrighted, or sensitive text
\citep{raffel2020exploring,dodge2021documenting}. We use C4 only to measure
readout reconstruction and make no social-validity claims about individual
tokens, labels, or generated outputs. We use public model checkpoints and
C4-derived hidden states under the licenses or terms published with their
release pages and model or data cards. We do not redistribute the original
checkpoints or raw C4 text; any released SRP artifacts will include
artifact-specific license and usage notes.

SRP makes lexical readout directions and row-level labels easier to inspect,
and in the readout-edit stress test moves specified lexical scores --- a
dual-use property of auditing tools. Claims rest on measured coordinate
objects rather than on label semantics; the edit is reported with distribution shift and
off-target probes. Safety-filter, moderation, and deployment-control claims
require direct system-level evaluation, which we do not undertake here.
