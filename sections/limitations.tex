\section*{Limitations}

\paragraph{Scope of claims.}
SRP's claim object is local: a selected readout score (a token logit, logit
difference, group contrast, or other fixed linear combination of LM-head rows).
Feature-resolved DLA and readout-side edits use this object to nominate
directions and measure induced score shifts plus distributional side-effects.
Causal necessity, sufficiency, and downstream generation effects are separate
evaluation targets.

\paragraph{Training cost.}
Logit lens and standard direct logit attribution require no fitting; SRP
requires training a sparse autoencoder over the rows of \(\WU\). The fit is
one-time per model and amortizes across downstream readout analyses, and
because the target is a fixed \(|V|\)-row matrix rather than a hidden-state
distribution, the cost is well below that of activation SAEs. Selected-score
decompositions on new hidden states are forward-pass only.

\paragraph{Hyperparameter dependence.}
As in other sparse dictionary settings, the learned basis depends on
dictionary size, sparsity \(k\), and recipe choices
\citep{elhage2022superposition,karvonen2025saebench,makelov2024principled}.
Operating points reported in the main text are selected from sweeps in
Appendices~\ref{app:readout-factorizer-sweeps}
and~\ref{app:readout-factorizer-qwen-sweeps}; the reported basis should be
read as an audited operating point rather than a unique factorization.

\paragraph{Fidelity and labels.}
SRP keeps the measured coordinate objects --- feature ids, signed terms, and
residual errors --- separate from label summaries of high-scoring unembedding
rows. Reconstruction error, sign agreement, replacement fidelity, and stability
checks accompany contribution terms, so interpretation follows the measured
objects.

\paragraph{Vocabulary-geometry artifacts.}
The selected-score interface inherits vocabulary geometry: labels and groupings
can reflect tokenizer geometry, token frequency, row norms, or surface-form
artifacts
\citep{press2017outputembedding,inan2017tying,yang2018softmaxbottleneck,bostrom2020bpe}.
Group contrasts reduce single-row dependence; benchmark-derived contrasts
are used for linear readout reconstruction rather than task solving; labels
summarize row geometry. Additional scope and future-work discussion appears in
Appendix~\ref{app:extended-discussion-limitations}.

\section*{Ethical Considerations}

This work studies pretrained language-model readouts, collects no new
human-subject data, and evaluates no deployed systems. Experiments use public
checkpoints and held-out text-derived hidden states, including C4 continuations,
which may contain offensive, private, copyrighted, or sensitive text
\citep{raffel2020exploring,dodge2021documenting}. We use C4 only to measure
readout reconstruction and make no social-validity claims about individual
tokens, labels, or generated outputs. We use public model checkpoints and
C4-derived hidden states under the licenses or terms published with their
release pages and model or data cards. We do not redistribute the original
checkpoints or raw C4 text; any released SRP artifacts will include
artifact-specific license and usage notes.

SRP makes lexical readout directions and row-level labels easier to inspect,
and in the readout-edit stress test moves specified lexical scores --- a
dual-use property of auditing tools. We report the edit with
distribution-shift and off-target probes, and keep claims tied to measured
coordinate objects. Safety-filter, moderation, and deployment-control
evaluation are system-level questions beyond this experiment.
