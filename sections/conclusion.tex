\section{Conclusion}

Sparse Readout Prism decomposes the readout side of lens scores. Fitting a
sparse dictionary to LM-head rows exposes three factors behind each selected
score: row-side coefficients on shared readout directions, hidden-state
projections supplied by context, and the reconstruction residual---all in
original logit units. Five calibration checks
(replacement fidelity, score reconstruction, sign agreement, relative error,
baseline comparisons) identify when the factorized readout is accurate enough to
interpret; outside that regime, feature labels alone are not evidence.

Within this validated regime, the fixed SRP basis supports readout-level
comparisons across score choices, prompt contexts, residual-stream components,
and constrained readout-side lexical edits. These analyses show how a single
lens score can be separated into row-side coefficients, context-dependent
projections, and explicit residual error, without turning feature labels into
evidence outside the calibrated regime.
