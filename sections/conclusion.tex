\section{Conclusion}

Sparse Readout Prism separates what is fixed by a sparse readout basis from
what is chosen by the score and what is supplied by context. Replacement
fidelity, score reconstruction, sign agreement, and baseline comparisons decide
when the factorized readout is accurate enough to interpret. Under those gates,
SRP supports readout-level comparisons across score choices, contexts,
residual-stream components, and candidate lexical edits; outside them, feature
labels alone are not evidence.

SRP also decomposes a selected lens score into coordinate terms that support
or oppose it, together with the residual left by the approximation. The same
template extends to other linear readouts --- reward-model heads, classifier
heads, retrieval projections; to tuned-lens and logit-prism readouts across
depth, locating where in the residual stream a feature becomes
readout-accessible; to activation-level interventions on decoder directions
that probe causal sufficiency and necessity; to cross-checkpoint comparisons
of basis stability through pretraining and fine-tuning; and to a
residual-stream census that splits projection mass between the selected
score, competitors, and directions active but locally unused. In each case
the same gates decide when feature labels count as evidence.
