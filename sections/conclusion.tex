\section{Conclusion}

Lens-style readouts often invite semantic explanations of individual scores:
a token is high because a context evokes a topic, sense, or residual-stream
component. The quantity being explained, however, is a specific readout score.
Without an explicit score decomposition, token-row geometry, prompt context,
contrast choice, and reconstruction error can be conflated.

Sparse Readout Prism makes that decomposition a readout-side object. A sparse
factorization of the LM head provides shared feature directions and token-row
coefficients; a selected score then supplies the direction coefficients that
turn hidden-state projections into signed terms plus an explicit residual. This
separates what is fixed by the readout basis from what is chosen by the score
and what is supplied by the current context.

The empirical role of the diagnostics is therefore central. Replacement
fidelity, score reconstruction, sign agreement, relative error, and baseline
comparisons decide when the factorized readout is accurate enough to interpret.
Under those gates, SRP supports readout-level comparisons across score choices,
contexts, residual-stream components, and candidate lexical edit directions;
outside them, feature labels alone are not evidence.

This framing makes the broader target concrete: lens explanations should specify
the score being explained, the coordinate terms that support or oppose it, and
the residual left by the approximation. Extending SRP to tuned lenses,
logit-prism variants, and future-token lenses would test whether the same
discipline can support layer-by-layer readout explanations.
