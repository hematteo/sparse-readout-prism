\section{Conclusion}

Sparse Readout Prism separates the target-independent and target-conditioned
parts of readout decomposition. It factorizes LM-head rows into shared readout
features and token-row coefficients; after a scalar readout target fixes the
linear score and sign convention, hidden states enter through feature
projections that yield signed terms plus an explicit residual in raw logit
coordinates. Replacement, target-reconstruction, sign, and baseline diagnostics
identify settings where these terms preserve readout behavior. In those
settings, the fixed SRP basis supports comparisons across targets and contexts,
composes with residual-stream attribution, and supplies candidate directions for
constrained readout-side edits.

\paragraph{Future work.}
SRP applies to linear readout maps, so a natural next step is to compose it with
tuned lenses, logit-prism variants, and future-token lenses, factorizing the
effective readout used at each layer rather than only the final LM head. This
would allow lens methods to be compared by decoded tokens and by their signed
sparse feature terms and residuals. A complementary direction is a
layerwise residual-stream census: track readout-feature projections before target
selection to study when evidence becomes readout-readable, when it becomes
target-specific, and which residual components supply the corresponding feature
terms.
