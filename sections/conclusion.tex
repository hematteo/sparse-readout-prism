\section{Conclusion}

Sparse Readout Prism decomposes the readout side of lens scores. Fitting a
sparse dictionary to LM-head rows yields a basis that separates three factors
any score collapses into a single scalar: the row-side coefficients on shared
readout directions, the hidden-state projections supplied by context, and the
reconstruction residual---all in original logit units. Five calibration checks
(replacement fidelity, score reconstruction, sign agreement, relative error,
baseline comparisons) decide when the factorized readout is accurate enough to
interpret; outside that regime, feature labels alone are not evidence.

Within this validated regime, the fixed SRP basis supports readout-level
comparisons across score choices, prompt contexts, residual-stream components,
and constrained lexical edits. These analyses show how a single lens score can be
separated into row-side coefficients, context-dependent projections, and explicit
residual error, without turning feature labels into evidence outside the
calibrated regime.
