\section{Conclusion}

Sparse Readout Prism contributes the target-independent part: it factorizes the
LM head into shared readout features and token-row coefficients. The selected
scalar readout-target interface contributes the target-conditioned part: it
chooses the linear score and sign convention that turn SRP projections into
signed feature terms with an explicit residual in raw logit coordinates.
Replacement, target-reconstruction, sign, and baseline diagnostics identify
settings where those terms preserve readout behavior. In those settings, the
fixed SRP basis supports comparisons across targets and contexts, composes with
residual-stream attribution, and supplies directions for constrained readout-side
edits. Causal intervention and generation-level evaluation remain separate
tests; the fixed basis provides residual-checked readout-linear candidate terms
for choosing where they should focus.

\paragraph{Future work.}
SRP applies to linear readout maps, so a natural next step is to compose it with
tuned lenses, logit-prism variants, and future-token lenses, factorizing the
effective readout used at each layer rather than only the final LM head. This
would allow lens methods to be compared by decoded tokens and by their signed
sparse feature terms and residuals. A complementary direction is a
layerwise residual-stream census: track readout-feature projections before target
selection to study when evidence becomes readout-readable, when it becomes
target-specific, and which residual components supply the corresponding feature
terms.
