\section{Conclusion}

Sparse Readout Prism decomposes the readout side of lens scores. Fitting a
sparse dictionary to LM-head rows yields a basis that separates three factors
any score collapses into a single scalar: the row-side coefficients on shared
readout directions, the hidden-state projections supplied by context, and the
reconstruction residual---all in original logit units. Five calibration checks
(replacement fidelity, score reconstruction, sign agreement, relative error,
baseline comparisons) decide when the factorized readout is accurate enough to
interpret; outside that regime, feature labels alone are not evidence.

Within the regime, the fixed basis supports readout-level comparisons across
score choices, prompt contexts, residual-stream components, and candidate
lexical edits. The same template extends to other linear readouts (reward-model
heads, classifier heads, retrieval projections); to depth-resolved lenses
(tuned lens, logit prism) that apply the readout at every layer, locating where
a feature becomes readout-accessible; to activation-level interventions on
decoder directions that probe causal sufficiency and necessity; to
cross-checkpoint comparisons of basis stability through pretraining and
fine-tuning; and to surveys of how each hidden state distributes projection
mass between the selected score, its competitors, and active-but-unused
directions. In each case the same gates decide when feature labels count as
evidence.
