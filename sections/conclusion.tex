\section{Conclusion}

Lens-style methods score hidden states against LM-head rows but say nothing
about which readout directions produce the score. Sparse Readout Prism
factorizes the rows into a shared overcomplete basis: each row becomes sparse
coefficients over \emph{readout features}---features of LM-head rows, not of
hidden-state activations---plus a residual. Once a token logit, logit
difference, or other fixed row combination is selected, the basis returns
signed per-feature contributions, an offset, and a residual that sum to the
original score in logit units.

Across eight models, SRP reconstructs centered LM-head rows at explained
variance \(0.83\)--\(0.89\), tracks the dense LM head on six of eight readouts,
and preserves contrast sign on at least \(91\%\) of held-out logit differences.
These diagnostics define a low-error regime (\(\rho<0.5\)) for term-level
reading. Inside that regime, SRP turns each lens score into a sparse
account with an explicit residual: polysemous logit differences expose
competing readout features, prompt context reweights a fixed token-row
decomposition, and decoder directions selected from a small discovery set
drive constrained lexical edits that transfer to held-out tokens.

Readout-side decomposition is complementary to hidden-state decomposition;
together they close the additive loop on lens scores.

\paragraph{Future work.}
Future work can extend SRP from the final LM head to the effective linear
readouts used by tuned lenses, logit-prism variants, and future-token lenses,
so lens methods can be compared by sparse signed feature terms as well as
decoded tokens. The same basis also supports a layerwise census of
readout-feature projections before score selection: when features become
readout-readable, which residual components supply them, and when they become
specific to a token, contrast, or competitor set. SRP-nominated terms then give
targets for causal patching, ablation, and generation-level evaluation, the
next tests for moving beyond additive readout decomposition.
