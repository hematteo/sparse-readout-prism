\section{Conclusion}

Sparse Readout Prism separates what is fixed by a sparse readout basis from
what is chosen by the score and what is supplied by context. Replacement
fidelity, score reconstruction, sign agreement, relative error, and baseline
comparisons decide when the factorized readout is accurate enough to interpret.
Under those gates, SRP supports readout-level comparisons across score choices,
contexts, residual-stream components, and candidate lexical edit directions;
outside them, feature labels alone are not evidence.

More broadly, researchers can use SRP to decompose a selected lens score into
coordinate terms that support or oppose it, together with the residual left by
the approximation. The same template extends naturally to other linear
readouts --- reward-model heads, classifier heads, retrieval projections ---
which currently lack feature-level decompositions; to tuned-lens and
logit-prism readouts, which would track when features become loaded across
depth; to activation-level interventions on the nominated edit directions,
which would test whether feature support implies causal responsibility; and
to cross-checkpoint comparisons of learned bases, which would indicate which
readout features survive fine-tuning.
