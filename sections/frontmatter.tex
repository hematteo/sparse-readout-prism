\begin{abstract}
Lens-style methods are often used to interpret what hidden states favor in
vocabulary space, but each inspected token logit or contrast remains a single
dense score against an LM-head row or row combination. This limits such
interpretations: the score does not identify which readout-side directions
support it, which oppose it, or how much reconstruction error a sparse account
would leave. Direct logit attribution and activation SAEs decompose the
hidden-state side of this score; they do not decompose the readout direction
being scored. Sparse Readout Prism (SRP) fills this gap by fitting a sparse
dictionary directly to LM-head rows. Each token row becomes a sparse combination
of shared \emph{readout features} plus a residual, and hidden states project
onto the same feature directions. For a selected readout score---a token logit,
logit difference, or other fixed LM-head row combination---SRP returns signed
per-feature contributions in original logit units with an explicit residual.

Across an eight-model suite, SRP reconstructs unembedding rows with
row-centered explained variance \(0.83\)--\(0.89\), matches held-out dense-head
top-1 predictions at \(\geq 0.75\) on six models, and preserves held-out
contrast signs on at least \(91\%\) of logit differences; \(69\%\)--\(89\%\)
of contrasts are also sign-preserved with relative reconstruction error below
0.5. On Qwen3.5-2B, SRP attains lower held-out score-reconstruction error than
nearest-row ridge, matched-rank PCA, and shuffled/random sparse-code controls,
supporting learned sparse row-code structure rather than lexical proximity,
dense low-rank geometry, or sparse capacity alone. We report local feature
readings only for sign-preserved, low-error cases, where the fixed basis
supports readout-level analyses of score choice, prompt context,
feature-resolved residual-stream attribution, and constrained readout-side
edits.
\end{abstract}
