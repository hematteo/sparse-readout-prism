\begin{abstract}
Vocabulary lenses read hidden states through the LM head and report
tokens. One token can be supported by different structure in different
contexts, and different tokens by the same structure, so a lens reading
can change while the computation behind it does not. The token is
therefore an ambiguous unit for that report. We present Sparse Readout Prism (SRP), a
sparse factorization of the LM head that reads any selected token logit
or logit difference as signed contributions over shared \emph{readout
features}, plus an explicit residual, in logit units. The basis is fit
from the weights alone, so, unlike activation dictionaries, it exists
before any prompt, and one dictionary covers every layer and lens. Six of
eight models pass a head-replacement check. On those six, SRP
reconstructs selected scores faithfully on \textbf{0.72--0.81} of
contrasts, better than any baseline built on LM-head row geometry, and
its per-feature contributions track the measured effect of removing
those features from the hidden state (\(r^2=0.83\)--\(0.93\)). The
reading also holds up under surface change: English- and
Chinese-fitted Jacobian lenses report different languages on identical
hidden states, yet the same SRP feature stays dominant on \textbf{77 of
80} prompts, and the pattern holds for a same-script language pair and for a
second lens construction. What a fitted lens reports thus follows the corpus
it was fitted on, a dependence we call \emph{corpus conditionality}. On
its own, a lens reading does not establish a matching intermediate
computation.

\end{abstract}
