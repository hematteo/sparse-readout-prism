\begin{abstract}
Lens-style analyses map language-model hidden states through the final
unembedding into vocabulary logits, but a selected logit or contrast still
compresses the readout into one dense score, obscuring supporting and opposing
directions and residual error. We introduce Sparse Readout Prism (SRP), a sparse
feature decomposition for logit-lens readouts that factorizes the LM head into
sparse token-row coefficients and shared readout directions. We pair this
target-independent basis with selected scalar readout targets, which specify the
linear score and sign convention. Given a hidden state and selected target, the
fixed SRP basis and target coefficients decompose the score into signed feature
contributions plus an explicit residual in original logit coordinates.

We test both pieces across multiple model families and readout geometries,
measuring LM-head replacement, target reconstruction, sign preservation, and
baselines. In representative high-fidelity settings, the fixed SRP basis
preserves held-out
contrast signs at \(0.949\)--\(0.958\) with median relative reconstruction error
below \(0.10\), and learned sparse structure outperforms null and reference
baselines. In sign-preserved, low-error cases, the same basis reveals how target
choice, prompt context, residual-stream components, and constrained readout-side
edits reshape the signed terms.
\end{abstract}
