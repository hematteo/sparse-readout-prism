\begin{abstract}
Lens-style analyses expose language-model hidden states through the final
unembedding into vocabulary logits. This gives a vocabulary-level view, but a
selected logit or contrast is still a single score: it identifies an outcome
without showing which readout directions support it, which oppose it, or what
residual remains outside the account. We introduce Sparse Readout Prism (SRP), a
sparse feature decomposition for logit-lens readouts that factorizes the LM head
into sparse token-row coefficients and shared readout directions. Given a hidden
state and scalar readout target, SRP decomposes the selected score into signed
feature contributions plus an explicit residual in original logit coordinates.

We use fidelity tests to identify when these feature-level accounts preserve the
original LM-head score. Across the Qwen settings selected by these diagnostics,
SRP preserves held-out contrast signs at \(0.949\)--\(0.958\) with median
relative reconstruction error below \(0.10\), and learned sparse structure
outperforms null and reference baselines. In sign-preserved, low-error cases,
local score accounts show how target choice and prompt context reweight a shared
readout-feature basis, compose with residual-stream attribution, and supply
directions for constrained readout-side edits. SRP extends logit-lens analysis
from vocabulary outcomes to auditable feature-level accounts of readout evidence.
\end{abstract}
