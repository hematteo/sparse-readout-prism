\begin{abstract}
Logit-lens-style methods inspect hidden states by projecting them through the
LM head and reading vocabulary logits, rankings, or logit differences. After a
token or contrast is selected, the inspected quantity is still a dense
dot product with an LM-head row or row combination, hiding which readout
directions support the score, which oppose it, and what remains unexplained.
We introduce Sparse Readout Prism (SRP), a sparse feature decomposition of
logit-lens readouts.
SRP factorizes the LM head into shared \emph{readout features} and sparse
output-token row coefficients. It projects hidden states onto this
basis once; for any selected token logit, logit difference, or fixed row
combination, SRP decomposes the score into signed feature contributions in
original logit units plus an explicit residual.

The same basis composes with residual-stream attribution and supplies
candidate directions for constrained readout-side edits. Across eight models,
SRP preserves held-out contrast signs on at least \(91\%\) of logit differences.
In the matched baseline comparison, it improves over nearest-row ridge,
matched-rank PCA, and shuffled/random sparse-code controls in median
reconstruction error and sign-preserved low-error coverage. With explicit
reconstruction diagnostics,
SRP lets researchers compare token preferences, component attributions, and
readout-side edits in a shared sparse basis.
\end{abstract}
