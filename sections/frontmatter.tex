\begin{abstract}
Lens-style methods inspect a hidden state \(h\) through the LM head, but the
inspected quantity is still a dense dot product \(h^\top q\) with an LM-head
row or row combination. Direct logit attribution and activation SAEs sparsify
\(h\); neither sparsifies \(q\). Sparse Readout Prism (SRP) fits a sparse
dictionary directly to the rows of the LM head: each token row becomes a
sparse combination of shared \emph{readout features} plus a residual, and
hidden states project onto the same directions. For a selected readout
score---a token logit \(h^\top w_t\), logit difference \(h^\top(w_A-w_B)\), or
other fixed LM-head row combination---SRP returns signed per-feature
contributions in original logit units with an explicit residual.

Across Qwen, Gemma, Ministral, and distilled Qwen and Llama models, SRP
preserves held-out contrast signs and, on Qwen3.5-2B, attains lower held-out
score-reconstruction error than nearest-row ridge, dense PCA, and null sparse
controls---evidence that the gains reflect learned sparse row-code structure,
not lexical proximity, dense low-rank geometry, or sparse capacity alone.
Calibration checks identify the regime where local feature bars are reliable;
within it, the fixed basis supports readout-level analyses of score choice,
prompt context, residual-stream attribution, and constrained lexical-score
edits.
\end{abstract}
