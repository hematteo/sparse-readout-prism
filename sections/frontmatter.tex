\begin{abstract}
Logit-lens-style methods inspect hidden states by projecting them through the
LM head and reading vocabulary logits, rankings, or logit differences. After a
token or contrast is selected, the inspected quantity remains a single dense
dot product with an LM-head row or row combination, concealing which readout
directions support the score, which oppose it, and what remains unexplained.
We introduce Sparse Readout Prism (SRP), a sparse feature decomposition of
logit-lens readouts.
SRP factorizes the LM head into shared \emph{readout features} and sparse
coefficients for output-token rows. It projects hidden states onto this shared
basis once; for any selected token logit, logit difference, or fixed row
combination, it decomposes the score into signed feature contributions in
original logit units plus an explicit residual.

The same fixed basis composes with residual-stream attribution and supplies
candidate directions for constrained edits to selected readout scores. Across
an eight-model suite, SRP preserves held-out contrast signs on at least
\(91\%\) of logit differences; on Qwen3.5-2B it improves on nearest-row ridge,
matched-rank PCA, and shuffled/random sparse-code controls in both median
reconstruction error and sign-preserved low-error coverage. With explicit
reconstruction diagnostics, SRP makes lens-style readouts auditable, letting
researchers compare token preferences, component attributions, and readout-side
edits in a shared sparse basis rather than as isolated dense scores.
\end{abstract}
