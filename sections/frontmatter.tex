\begin{abstract}
Logit-lens-style methods inspect hidden states by projecting them through
the LM head and reading vocabulary logits, rankings, or logit differences.
Once a token or contrast is selected, the inspected quantity is a dense dot
product with an LM-head row or row combination, so the lens reports which
token a context prefers but not which readout directions carry that
preference. We introduce Sparse Readout Prism (SRP), which factorizes the
LM head into shared \emph{readout features} with sparse output-token row
coefficients. Hidden states are projected onto this basis once; any
selected token logit, logit difference, or fixed row combination then
decomposes into signed per-feature contributions plus an explicit
residual, in original logit units.

Across eight models, SRP reconstructs centered unembedding rows at
explained variance \(0.83\)--\(0.89\) and preserves held-out contrast
signs on at least \(91\%\) of logit differences; on Qwen3.5-2B it improves
over nearest-row ridge, dense PCA references including a
compactness-matched point, and shuffled/random sparse-code
controls in median reconstruction error and sign-preserved low-error
coverage. Fidelity varies by score family and falls as a contrast
approaches a near-tie, so the reported diagnostics gate which feature
terms are read. Within that regime, the basis turns lens scores into
readout-level analyses: polysemous contrasts decompose into competing
sense features reweighted by prompt context, direct logit attribution
refines into component-by-feature terms, and discovery-selected decoder
directions drive constrained lexical edits that transfer to held-out
tokens at a measured distribution-shift cost.
\end{abstract}
