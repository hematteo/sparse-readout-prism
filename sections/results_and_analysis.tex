\section{Evidence for Reliable Sparse Readout Accounts}
\label{sec:results}
\label{subsec:calibration}

The results proceed in two stages. We first calibrate when SRP accounts are
faithful enough to interpret: the factorized readout must preserve LM-head
behavior, reconstruct selected readout scores with low error and preserved sign, beat
generic baselines, and admit score-family regimes where local readings hold.
Under those gates, \Cref{sec:readout-analyses} uses sign-preserved,
low-residual cases to analyze score choice, token preferences, context shifts,
attribution, and edits. Aggregate low-error counts use the threshold
\(\rho<0.5\) defined in \Cref{sec:method-reading-local-accounts}.

The sparse factorization preserves unembedding rows across the suite (rowEV
\(0.83\)--\(0.89\)) and tracks the dense readout on six of eight models (top-1
agreement \(\geq 0.75\) and KL \(\leq 0.5\);
\Cref{tab:readout-score-fidelity-summary}). Two exceptions stand out. Gemma-E2B fails at
the decision level (top-1 \(0.333\), KL \(6.37\)), and the reasoning-distilled readouts show that high rowEV can still mis-rank the argmax when a few unembedding rows carry very large residual norms: those rows then dominate the softmax even though row variance overall is well captured (Appendix~\ref{app:readout-norm-tails}). Row
reconstruction alone therefore does not certify a sparse account, and we
report replacement fidelity before reading local decompositions.

\paragraph{Selected-Score Reconstruction}\label{subsec:selected-score-reconstruction}
The logit-difference columns test whether selected readout scores survive the
factorization. Sign agreement is at least \(0.91\) across the suite, and most
contrasts fall in the sign-preserved, low-error subset (\(\rho<0.5\), with
\(\rho=|s_{\mathrm{exact}}-s_{\mathrm{recon}}|/(|s_{\mathrm{exact}}|+0.5)\)).
Errors and flips concentrate near small exact margins
(\Cref{fig:readout-score-fidelity-result}). Calibration therefore splits the score set in two: a sign-preserved low-error subset, where local readings are admitted, and a small-margin failure regime, where we report only aggregate statistics. Case studies come from the former.
RowEV is a capacity metric, not validation of any feature bar.

\paragraph{Reliable Regimes for Local Interpretation}\label{subsec:operating-regimes}
With the basis fixed, reference-style contrasts and separated margins reconstruct
more reliably than heterogeneous row combinations or near ties
(\Cref{tab:benchmark-derived-readout-contrasts,tab:app-general-readout-score-families};
Appendix~\ref{app:selected-readout-score-family-display-example}). Later displays stay
in that regime and are read only when sign is preserved and residual is small.

\section{Readout Analyses}
\label{sec:readout-analyses}

Analyses below stay in the sign-preserved, low-residual regime established in
\Cref{sec:results}.

\paragraph{Lens Preferences Become Sparse Readout Accounts [RQ1]}\label{subsec:lens-to-sparse-account}
Lens-style readouts identify a favored token or contrast but leave it as a dense
LM-head dot product. RQ1 asks whether anything is gained by going further: starting from the lens-selected score, can SRP expose structure that the scalar hides? In \Cref{fig:main-lens-prism-comparison}, SRP holds the
\texttt{verify}-minus-\texttt{assume} score fixed and decomposes that same
preference into signed readout terms plus an explicit residual. The object
becomes a residual-checked sparse account in logit units rather than an
atomic lens score: positive and negative terms support or oppose the selected
side. This is
still a readout-level account; behavioral or causal claims require separate
tests.

\paragraph{Score Choice Sets Support and Opposition [RQ2]}\label{subsec:score-choice}
A large projection onto a readout feature is neither intrinsically favorable
nor unfavorable: its sign depends on whether the selected readout score uses that
feature more on the positive side or the comparison side. Selected-direction
coefficients turn projections into signed contributions, so a feature may
support a raw logit, drop out after mean subtraction, or become negative when
the comparison token uses it more. Each display therefore names its selected
readout score: changing \(q\) changes \(\beta_i(q)\), and the same projection vector
can yield different support and opposition terms.
\Cref{subsec:calibration} bounds which choices of \(q\) admit such comparisons.

\paragraph{Logit Differences Expose Competing Features [RQ3]}\label{subsec:feature-competition}
Sign-preserved, low-error local decompositions identify the readout features
that raise or lower token-vs-token scores within a ranking. Using the fixed SRP
basis, we decompose low-error Qwen3.5-2B logit differences over polysemous
tokens---surface tokens such as \texttt{bug} or \texttt{bark} whose intended
sense depends on context (\Cref{fig:selected-readout-prism-examples}). In an insect context,
\texttt{bug} draws positive mosquito-, bee-, and beetle-labeled terms against
error-token rows; in software, positives shift toward defect, crash, and debug
rows. Relative reconstruction errors are \(0.185\) and \(0.003\). Additional
\texttt{bark}/\texttt{bass} cases, plotting conventions, and label audits
appear in
\cref{fig:app-selected-readout-prism-extra-examples,app:display-taxonomy,tab:app-main-case-study-feature-audit}.
The same surface token draws support from different row-labeled feature groups
as the selected readout score and context change.

We audit displayed labels separately from the measured feature ids and signed
terms (Appendix~\ref{app:main-case-study-feature-audit}). Across 99
claim-bearing displayed labels, 67 are coherent lexical labels, 18 are
ambiguous, and 14 are token-form labels; all 67 coherent labels point to the
signed side claimed in the prose. We therefore use labels as readability
summaries, not as ground-truth semantic features.

\paragraph{Context Reweights Fixed Token-Row Features [RQ4]}\label{subsec:context-reweighting}
Fixing the selected token isolates context effects: the token-row coefficients
\(\beta_i\) remain fixed while the hidden-state projections \(h^\top d_i\)
vary. In \Cref{fig:same-token-polysemy}, prompt context reweights
\texttt{bug}, \texttt{ring}, and \texttt{bridge} toward features whose row
labels match the setting; each token score (after subtracting the shared offset $h^\top\mu$) has \(\rho<0.05\). \texttt{bug} shifts from mosquito/bee to defect/crash/debug
labels; \texttt{bridge} shifts from river/crossing to networking labels. These
cases separate the context-independent component of a local account, carried
by the token-row coefficients, from the context-dependent component, carried
by hidden-state projections onto the readout feature directions. Additional
views are in Appendix~\ref{app:additional-qwen-display-examples}.

\paragraph{Readout Features Compose with Component Attribution [RQ5]}\label{sec:feature-resolved-component-decomposition}\label{subsec:feature-resolved-component-attribution}
Signed readout terms are linear in the final hidden state, so additive
residual-stream decompositions pass through the fixed SRP basis. We compose
SRP with direct logit attribution (DLA), which writes the final hidden state
as a sum of contributions from earlier residual-stream components---the
embedding, attention heads, and MLP layers---and reads off how much each
component adds to a chosen logit
\citep{elhage2021framework,wang2022ioi,nguyen2024logitprisms}: if
\(h\approx\sum_c h_c\), a feature term for selected direction \(q\) splits into
\[
    M_{c,i}(q)=\beta_i(q)\,h_c^\top d_i .
\]
Rather than a single scalar margin contribution per component, this yields
component-by-readout-feature terms for the selected readout score.
This is component-by-feature accounting for a realized forward pass, not causal
necessity without interventions \citep{geiger2023causalabstraction}.

\paragraph{Readout Features Support Constrained Lexical Edits [RQ5]}\label{subsec:constrained-readout-edits}
The learned decoder directions also serve as candidate edit directions at
the readout: subtracting a fixed multiple of a decoder direction from the
final hidden state lowers the logits of tokens that load on that direction.
We test this in a constrained lexical-score setting. Five discovery terms
select ten Qwen3.5-2B decoder directions; the edit is then applied to a
disjoint set of evaluation tokens that were not used during selection. A
``flip'' counts when the top-1 next token moves away from the target word
under the edit. Two logit-bias baselines bracket the SAE-direction edit:
the \emph{discovery token bias} subtracts a fixed logit penalty from the
five discovery tokens only, so by construction it cannot transfer beyond
them, while the \emph{oracle token bias} applies the same penalty directly
to the held-out evaluation tokens, an upper bound that uses the evaluation
labels at intervention time.
