\section{Evidence for Reliable Sparse Readout Accounts}
\label{sec:results}
\label{subsec:calibration}

We first test whether the factorized readout preserves the dense LM head and
selected readout scores. Across the evaluated models, SRP preserves contrast
signs and yields low reconstruction error except near small exact margins. We
therefore interpret feature decompositions only when the reconstructed and
exact scores agree in sign and satisfy \(\rho<0.5\) (defined in
\Cref{sec:method-reading-local-accounts}); \Cref{sec:readout-analyses} works
through score choice, token preferences, context shifts, attribution, and
edits within this subset.

The sparse factorization preserves unembedding rows across the suite (rowEV
\(0.83\)--\(0.89\)) and tracks the dense readout on six of eight models (top-1
agreement \(\geq 0.75\) and KL \(\leq 0.5\);
\Cref{tab:readout-score-fidelity-summary}). Two exceptions stand out.
Gemma-E2B fails at the decision level (top-1 \(0.333\), KL \(6.37\))
despite a respectable rowEV of \(0.83\): row reconstruction alone does not
certify argmax behavior. The reasoning-distilled readouts add a second failure
mode: high rowEV can still mis-rank the argmax when a few unembedding rows
carry very large residual norms, since those rows then dominate the softmax
even though row variance overall is well captured
(Appendix~\ref{app:readout-norm-tails}). Both cases motivate reporting
replacement fidelity before any local decomposition.

\paragraph{Selected-Score Reconstruction}\label{subsec:selected-score-reconstruction}
The logit-difference columns test whether selected readout scores survive the
factorization. Sign agreement is at least \(0.91\) across the suite, and most
contrasts fall in the sign-preserved, low-error subset (\(\rho<0.5\), with
\(\rho=|s_{\mathrm{exact}}-s_{\mathrm{recon}}|/(|s_{\mathrm{exact}}|+0.5)\)).
Errors and flips concentrate near small exact margins
(\Cref{fig:readout-score-fidelity-result}). We therefore restrict local
decompositions to the sign-preserved low-error subset; small-margin scores
enter only the aggregate statistics. Within this subset, reference-style
contrasts and separated margins reconstruct more reliably than heterogeneous
row combinations or near ties
(\Cref{tab:benchmark-derived-readout-contrasts,tab:app-general-readout-score-families};
Appendix~\ref{app:selected-readout-score-family-display-example}). The case
studies that follow are drawn from this subset. RowEV remains a capacity
metric, not validation of any local feature bar.

On the full Qwen3.5-2B logit-difference set, SRP attains lower median error
and a larger sign-preserved low-error subset than nearest-row ridge
\citep{hoerl1970ridge}, PCA \citep{jolliffe2002principal}, shuffled sparse
codes, and random sparse patterns (\Cref{tab:main-baseline-null-comparison}).
Each baseline tests a different alternative explanation: ridge isolates
lexical proximity between scored tokens; PCA at matched rank isolates dense
low-rank geometry; shuffling sparse codes preserves the sparsity pattern but
breaks the learned row--code assignment; random sparse patterns hold sparse
capacity constant. The gap therefore supports learned sparse row-code structure
for these reconstructions specifically; it does not validate every label as
semantic.

\section{Readout Analyses}
\label{sec:readout-analyses}

\paragraph{Lens Preferences Become Sparse Readout Accounts [RQ1]}\label{subsec:lens-to-sparse-account}
Lens-style readouts identify a favored token or contrast but leave it as a
dense LM-head dot product. In \Cref{fig:main-lens-prism-comparison}, SRP holds
the \texttt{verify}-minus-\texttt{assume} score fixed and decomposes it into
signed readout terms plus an explicit residual. Positive and negative terms
support or oppose the selected side, giving a residual-checked sparse account
in logit units rather than a single dense score. The object remains a
readout-level account; behavioral or causal claims require separate tests.

\paragraph{Logit Differences Expose Competing Features [RQ2]}\label{subsec:feature-competition}
Selected-direction coefficients \(\beta_i(q)\) turn projections into signed
contributions. As the selected score \(q\) changes, the same feature may support
a token, oppose it, or drop out after mean subtraction. Sign-preserved,
low-error local decompositions then identify the readout features that raise or
lower token-vs-token scores within a ranking. Using the fixed SRP
basis, we decompose low-error Qwen3.5-2B logit differences over polysemous
tokens---surface tokens such as \texttt{bug} or \texttt{bark} whose intended
sense depends on context (\Cref{fig:selected-readout-prism-examples}). In an
insect context,
\texttt{bug} draws positive mosquito-, bee-, and beetle-labeled terms against
error-token rows; in software, positives shift toward defect, crash, and debug
rows. Relative reconstruction errors are \(0.185\) and \(0.003\). Additional
\texttt{bark}/\texttt{bass} cases, plotting conventions, and label audits
appear in
\cref{fig:app-selected-readout-prism-extra-examples,app:display-taxonomy,tab:app-main-case-study-feature-audit}.
The same surface token draws support from different row-labeled feature groups
as the selected readout score and context change.

We audit displayed labels separately from the measured feature ids and signed
terms (Appendix~\ref{app:main-case-study-feature-audit}). Across 99
claim-bearing displayed labels, 67 are coherent lexical labels, 18 are
ambiguous, and 14 are token-form labels; all 67 coherent labels point to the
signed side claimed in the prose. Labels remain readability summaries, not
ground-truth features.

\paragraph{Context Reweights Fixed Token-Row Features [RQ3]}\label{subsec:context-reweighting}
Fixing the selected token isolates context effects: the token-row coefficients
\(\beta_i\) remain fixed while the hidden-state projections \(h^\top d_i\)
vary. In \Cref{fig:same-token-polysemy}, prompt context reweights
\texttt{bug}, \texttt{ring}, and \texttt{bridge} toward features whose row
labels match the setting; each token score (after subtracting the shared
offset \(h^\top\mu\)) has \(\rho<0.05\). \texttt{bug} shifts from
mosquito/bee to defect/crash/debug
labels; \texttt{bridge} shifts from river/crossing to networking labels. These
cases separate the context-independent component of a local account, carried
by the token-row coefficients, from the context-dependent component, carried
by hidden-state projections onto the readout feature directions. Additional
views are in Appendix~\ref{app:additional-qwen-display-examples}.

\paragraph{Readout Features Compose with Component Attribution [RQ4]}\label{sec:feature-resolved-component-decomposition}\label{subsec:feature-resolved-component-attribution}
Signed readout terms are linear in the final hidden state, so additive
residual-stream decompositions pass through the fixed SRP basis. We compose
SRP with direct logit attribution (DLA), which writes the final hidden state
as a sum of contributions from earlier residual-stream components---the
embedding, attention heads, and MLP layers---and reads off how much each
component adds to a chosen logit
\citep{elhage2021framework,wang2022ioi,nguyen2024logitprisms}: if
\(h\approx\sum_c h_c\), a feature term for selected direction \(q\) splits into
\[
    M_{c,i}(q)=\beta_i(q)\,h_c^\top d_i .
\]

    \caption{\textbf{Feature-resolved component attribution.}
    Qwen3.5-2B \texttt{verify}-\texttt{assume} margin. Rows are embedding and

    assume features, remaining sparse terms, the LM-head residual, and ordinary
    DLA. Red supports \texttt{verify}; blue supports \texttt{assume}.}
    \label{fig:qwen-prism-dla-verify-assume-main}
\end{figure}

Rather than a single scalar margin contribution per component, this yields
component-by-readout-feature terms for the selected readout score.
This is component-by-feature accounting for a realized forward pass, not causal
necessity without interventions \citep{geiger2023causalabstraction}.

\Cref{fig:qwen-prism-dla-verify-assume-main} decomposes a Qwen3.5-2B
\texttt{verify}-minus-\texttt{assume} logit difference. The exact margin is
\(+4.31\), the DLA sum \(+4.36\) (component additivity error \(+0.05\)), and
the sparse feature sum \(+4.28\) (\(\rho=0.009\)), separating
verification/checking support from assumption-family opposition. A row-centered
companion appears in
Appendix~\ref{app:feature-resolved-dla}.

\paragraph{Readout Features Support Constrained Lexical Edits [RQ5]}\label{subsec:constrained-readout-edits}
The learned decoder directions also serve as candidate edit directions at
the readout: subtracting a fixed multiple of a decoder direction from the
final hidden state lowers the logits of tokens that load positively on that
direction.
We test this in a constrained lexical-score setting. Five discovery terms
select ten Qwen3.5-2B decoder directions; the edit is then applied to a
disjoint set of evaluation tokens that were not used during selection. A
``flip'' counts when the top-1 next token moves away from the target word
under the edit. Two logit-bias baselines bracket the SAE-direction edit:
the \emph{discovery token bias} subtracts a fixed logit penalty from the
five discovery tokens only, so by construction it cannot transfer beyond
them, while the \emph{oracle token bias} applies the same penalty directly
to the held-out evaluation tokens, an upper bound that uses the evaluation
labels at intervention time.

\Cref{tab:main-readout-edit} shows that readout SAE directions selected from
discovery tokens transfer to evaluation tokens (flip rate \(0.359\)), matching
the oracle's eval flip rate even though selection never saw the evaluation
tokens; a discovery-token-only bias does not transfer at all (eval flip
\(0.000\)). The trade-off is off-target distribution shift: the oracle uses
the evaluation labels at intervention time and so achieves the same flips at
median KL \(0.004\), while SRP pays median KL \(0.679\) for acting through a
shared basis rather than a token-specific penalty. Transfer to held-out tokens
from a fixed basis is the gain; broader distributional shift is the
corresponding cost. The R1-Distill-Qwen-7B replication appears in
Appendix~\ref{app:lexical-control-stress-test}.
