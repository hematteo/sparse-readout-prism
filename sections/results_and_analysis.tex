\section{The Factorized Readout Is Faithful, Reproducible, and Predictive}
\label{sec:results}
\label{subsec:calibration}
\label{sec:experimental-setup}

This section builds one cumulative argument: selected scores are
reconstructed accurately; simpler
geometry recovers neither the same score fidelity nor the same
groupings; a decomposition's claims reproduce across independent
training runs; and feature contributions predict the measured effects
of local interventions. Each stage tests one claim about the
factorization---\emph{faithfulness}, \emph{distinctness},
\emph{reproducibility}, and \emph{intervention relevance}---and the
utility of the validated space is the subject of
\Cref{sec:beyond-token}. Decompositions are read under the reading rule of
\Cref{sec:method-reading-local-accounts}.

\paragraph{Models and held-out sets.}
The suite spans Qwen3.5~\citep{qwen35collection}, Gemma-4
\citep{gemma4official,gemma4collection}, Ministral~\citep{liu2026ministral3},
and reasoning-distilled Qwen/Llama readouts \citep{deepseekai2025deepseekr1};
identifiers and selection criteria:
Appendix~\ref{app:model-suite-selection}. Because SRP needs only the final weights, any open-weights model
qualifies; we choose eight recent models spanning base, instruction-tuned,
and reasoning-distilled variants at 0.8B--9B scale.
We fit one readout SAE per model to the rows of its final LM head and
evaluate it on 10{,}000 held-out C4 continuations
\citep{raffel2020exploring,dodge2021documenting} plus a fixed
prompt-and-score set. The model split is fixed once: all eight models enter
the reconstruction analysis, and the six whose readouts pass the
interpretation gate (held-out replacement top-1 agreement \(\ge 0.75\);
both Gemma-4 variants fall below it) enter the baseline, reproducibility,
and intervention comparisons.

\paragraph{Contrast banks.}
Selected scores come from fixed, model-agnostic banks
(Appendix~\ref{app:query-bank-provenance}). A hand-written set of 301
prompts spanning factual QA, instruction, paragraph, code, dialogue,
retrieval-update, and safety/tool settings supplies model-native contrasts
built from each model's own ranking: top-1 against the vocabulary mean, a
row-norm-matched distractor, a sampled competitor, and the rank-5 and
rank-2 tokens. A curated A/B bank of 320 template-expanded records,
plus 40 case-candidate records of the same types,
supplies the current-vs-stale, source-vs-prior, action/tool, and
abstention families; per-tokenizer single-token filters
(Appendix~\ref{app:query-bank-provenance})
leave 82--96 cases per family. The banks are held out by construction:
the readout SAEs are trained on LM-head rows and selected on
row-reconstruction, replacement, and code-usage metrics, with the
contrast set used only after selection.
Aggregates cluster items by base case (one prompt--score template with
its variants), which is the resampling unit for bootstrap intervals; baseline
comparisons use the full \(\sim\)1{,}350-item set per model over
\(\sim\)850 base cases. A separate benchmark-derived bank contributes 300
fixed row combinations for each of two Qwen models
(\Cref{subsec:selected-score-reconstruction}).

\label{subsec:selected-score-reconstruction}%
The factorization preserves both the readout and the selected scores
(\Cref{tab:readout-score-fidelity-summary}). What licenses interpretation
is replacement fidelity, not aggregate row reconstruction: we check each
readout by top-token agreement and KL on held-out hidden states before any
local decomposition, since rows with large residual norms can dominate the
softmax even when reconstruction is accurate in aggregate
(Appendix~\ref{app:readout-norm-tails}). At the score level, sign
agreement is at least \(0.91\) across the suite and the low-error fraction
(\(\rho<0.5\)) covers \(0.71\)--\(0.89\) of contrasts per model, with
residual error and the few sign flips confined to near-tie margins
(Appendix Figure~\ref{fig:readout-score-fidelity-result}); case
studies below are drawn from the passing subset, and
small-margin scores remain in the
aggregates.\label{subsec:score-family-fidelity} Fidelity varies
predictably by score family---near-tie margins are hardest, since
the contrast residual does not scale with the margin (Appendix
Table~\ref{tab:readout-score-families})---and extends beyond
hand-picked pairs to 300 benchmark-derived contrasts
(Appendix~\ref{app:benchmark-derived-display-examples},
Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-derived-readout-contrasts}).

If SRP's codes merely repackaged \(\WU\) geometry, methods built directly
from that geometry should reconstruct selected scores equally well. They
do not (\Cref{tab:main-baseline-null-comparison}): across all six
models, SRP reconstructs 0.72--0.81 of contrasts with the correct sign
and error below half the score's magnitude (sign-preserved low-error
coverage), leading the strongest of six alternatives by 8.9--17.3
points with non-overlapping intervals. The comparison controls for capacity: the k-means
centroid dictionary at \(D{=}65{,}536\), \(k{=}256\) matches SRP's width
and sparsity exactly and still trails by 18 points on the display model
(Qwen3.5-2B),
and shuffled-code and random-pattern nulls collapse entirely
(\Cref{tab:app-cross-model-nulls}). The gap holds at the grouping level
as well---a \emph{grouping} being the set of vocabulary rows that one
feature ties together. Of the groupings that reproduce across training
seeds (\S\ref{subsec:stability}), a second SRP dictionary trained with the
same recipe (same width, sparsity, and preprocessing) recovers
72--75\%, against 43--49\% for row-kNN and 21--25\% for hard
clustering, with ratio CIs excluding parity
(Appendix~\ref{app:basis-stability}). Learned sparse row--code structure,
not generic row proximity, carries the reconstruction.

\paragraph{Explanations Reproduce Across Independent Runs}\label{subsec:stability}
Individual decoder directions are not canonical: across three
independently trained dictionaries per width (equal held-out
reconstruction verified), matched decoder cosines for used features
(those active on the evaluation set)
average only \(\sim\)0.33 at the 32\(\times\) width, consistent with
reported TopK seed-sensitivity \citep{paulo2025stability}. What a
decomposition says, however, does reproduce.
Over 63 curated contrasts, the side token-sets (the tokens each seed's
decomposition places on each side) reproduce across every
seed pair at \(\sim\)15\(\times\) the cross-contrast null: 100\% of
contrasts sit above the null's 90th percentile at both widths, and
cross-side leakage, the overlap with the opposing side's set, stays at
or below 0.005. Further, 87--91\% of used feature groups (groups active
in these decompositions) have an above-chance counterpart in an
independently trained dictionary, with the median group 83--90\%
covered by a union of at most three features. Structure reproduces, indexing does not, and the
residual instability is feature splitting---one seed's feature reappearing
as several in another---not noise: seed-sensitivity
of individual directions does not preclude reproducible
explanations. These
statements are within-recipe (fixed width, sparsity, preprocessing);
cross-recipe variation is larger and disclosed in
Appendix~\ref{app:basis-stability}.

The decomposition is more than bookkeeping: on all six
models, predicted feature contributions match the margin change measured
when the feature's direction is deleted from the hidden state
(readout-side ablation) at \(r^2=0.83\)--\(0.93\), while random
directions explain at most 0.06
(\Cref{tab:causal-validation-summary}). Sparse-code misattribution,
decoder non-orthogonality, and the row residual would each break this
agreement; none does. Slopes are near 1 on the 7--8B readouts and
overstate magnitudes on the three Qwen readouts, with sign and rank
preserved throughout (Appendix~\ref{app:causal-validation}). These are
local, readout-side predictions on realized forward passes, not
circuit-level necessity or generation-level effects.

\paragraph{Feature Labels Survive a Blinded Audit}\label{subsec:label-audit}
Feature labels are readability summaries of top unembedding rows, audited
separately from the measured feature ids and signed terms. Under three blinded runs of a frontier language model acting as rater,
76 of the 99 claim-bearing display labels name a coherent lexical
family, 4 are mixed, and 19 are token-form artifacts; rater agreement,
the protocol, and an independent author audit are reported in
Appendix~\ref{app:main-case-study-feature-audit}. This is a same-model
reproducibility audit, not human annotation; claims are tied to feature
ids, signed contributions, and residuals, with labels used only where
audited \citep{karvonen2025saebench,makelov2024principled}.

\paragraph{Further Uses of the Basis}\label{sec:feature-resolved-component-decomposition}\label{subsec:constrained-readout-edits}
Two further uses are developed in the appendices: the basis composes with
residual-stream attribution, splitting each component's scalar
contribution into per-feature terms in the same logit units
(Appendix~\ref{app:feature-resolved-dla}), and its decoder directions
serve as constrained readout-side edit directions, with an advantage
over mean-row and PCA controls on the display model but not on the 0.8B
and 9B variants (Appendix~\ref{app:lexical-control-stress-test}).
