\section{Results and Analysis}
\label{sec:results}

The results follow the dependency chain established in
\Cref{sec:experimental-setup}: first readout replacement and target
reconstruction, then support baselines, then local analyses. For signed feature
accounts, we report whether the sparse reconstruction preserves the original
sign and its local relative error \(\rho\); unless otherwise stated, aggregate
low-error counts use \(\rho<0.5\).

\subsection{The Sparse Readout Factorization Preserves LM-Head Behavior}

\paragraph{Calibration before interpretation.}
Before interpreting any feature bars, we ask two pragmatic questions. If
\(\widehat{\WU}\) replaces the original LM head on held-out hidden states, does it
preserve the readout's top-token behavior and distribution? And for the pairwise
logit differences inspected below, do the reconstructed margins keep the original
size and sign? These checks determine which examples can be read as feature-level
accounts rather than artifacts of a poor sparse substitute.

\paragraph{Qwen gives the cleanest operating point.}
\Cref{tab:readout-query-fidelity-summary} reports row reconstruction, LM-head
replacement, and held-out logit-difference reconstruction for the \(k=256\)
settings used below. We measure margins by sign agreement, median relative error
\(\rho=|s_{\mathrm{exact}}-s_{\mathrm{recon}}|/(|s_{\mathrm{exact}}|+0.5)\),
and the fraction with \(\rho<0.5\). Qwen is strongest: replacement top-1
\(0.887\)--\(0.900\), sign agreement \(0.949\)--\(0.958\), and median
\(\rho<0.10\). Gemma usually keeps the sign but with larger errors. The
reasoning-distilled checkpoints have weaker replacement top-1/KL, yet still
preserve contrastive signs well (sign \(0.921\)--\(0.926\), median
\(\rho<0.15\)); Appendix~\ref{app:readout-factorizer-transfer-comparisons}
expands this pattern. Across families, errors and sign flips concentrate near
small exact margins (\Cref{fig:readout-query-fidelity-result}). Thus the most
reliable setting for the case studies is signed contrasts with nontrivial
margins; RowEV alone is only a capacity metric, not a guarantee that a local
display is trustworthy.

\subsection{Learned Token-Row Support Drives Target Reconstruction}

High-dimensional sparse bases can recover some output-embedding structure by
capacity alone. We therefore test whether the learned token-to-feature support,
rather than just the size of the basis, is responsible for the margin
reconstruction above.

\paragraph{Support controls.}
On the full Qwen3.5-2B logit-difference set, SRP has the lowest median error and
largest sign-preserved low-error subset
(\Cref{tab:main-baseline-null-comparison}).
Nearest-row ridge \citep{hoerl1970ridge} and PCA \citep{jolliffe2002principal}
references retain some sign structure, while shuffled codes and random supports
approach chance, indicating dependence on learned token-to-feature support. The
full error tail appears in
Appendix~\ref{app:robustness-key-quantitative}.
Thus the sparse code support learned from vocabulary rows, not merely the
availability of a large basis, drives the reconstruction used by the local
accounts.

\subsection{Scalar Readout Targets Extend Beyond Token Pairs}

The same accounting identity also covers targets that look more like benchmark
decisions than curated token pairs. We instantiate task-shaped readout contrasts
for QA
\citep{rajpurkar2018squad2,yang2018hotpotqa}, abstention, ContractNLI labels
through LegalBench \citep{koreeda2021contractnli,guha2023legalbench}, and
SecurityEval safe/unsafe actions \citep{siddiq2022securityeval}. These rows ask
whether the sparse reconstruction carries the intended readout contrast while
keeping the evaluation at the readout level.
For example, a QA item is converted into a linear contrast between the average
row for the gold answer token(s) and the average row for fixed distractor
token(s); the table evaluates reconstruction of that contrast, not the model's
end-to-end answer accuracy.

\Cref{tab:benchmark-derived-task-targets} shows sign reconstruction on
Qwen3.5-0.8B and Qwen3.5-2B. Qwen3.5-2B is strongest on answerable QA,
multi-hop distractors, and legal labels; SecurityEval is harder because its
safe/unsafe-action contrast is spread across many lexical forms, so no small
token group captures the target as cleanly. These rows measure whether the
chosen readout contrast is reconstructed and should not be read as benchmark
task accuracy.
\Cref{fig:benchmark-task-group-examples} shows representative low-error QA
accounts.

\subsection{Local Accounts Expose Feature Competition Within Token Preferences}

With the low-error regime identified, we turn to the content of the bars. The
first case studies keep the scoring question simple: one token against another,
with shared readout directions carrying the positive and negative evidence.

\paragraph{From token preferences to feature terms.}
Top-token lens summaries compress a preference into one score. In
\Cref{fig:main-lens-prism-comparison}, the same
\texttt{verify}-versus-\texttt{assume} margin is broken into supporting and
opposing readout features, with the leftover reconstruction error shown next to
the bars.

\paragraph{Polysemous-token case studies.}
For the main case studies, we apply SRP to low-error Qwen3.5-2B polysemous-token
logit differences in \Cref{fig:selected-readout-prism-examples}. In an insect
context, \texttt{bug} receives positive terms from features labeled by mosquito-,
bee-, and beetle-associated rows against error-token rows; in software, the
positive terms shift toward defect, crash, and debug rows. The two \(32\times\),
\(k=256\) panels have relative reconstruction errors \(0.185\) and \(0.003\).
Additional \texttt{bark} and \texttt{bass} cases, plotting conventions, and
label audits appear in
\cref{fig:app-selected-readout-prism-extra-examples,app:display-taxonomy,tab:app-main-case-study-feature-audit}.
These sign-preserved, low-\(\rho\) examples show target-local support: the same
surface token can be supported by different feature families as the competing
alternative and prompt context change. They do not by themselves isolate whether
the change comes from the target, the prompt context, or both; the next two
sections separate those effects. Aggregate frequencies are reported in the
appendix tables.

\subsection{Target Choice Selects Support from a Shared Projection Profile}

We use the scalar-target interface to compare several scoring questions on the
same hidden states. The empirical question is whether the sparse account remains
reliable as targets move from reference contrasts to sharper pair and group
margins.

\paragraph{Target-family reliability.}
Large hidden-state projections become signed evidence only through the target
coefficients, so fidelity must be interpreted per target family. For instance, a
feature with a large projection may support a raw token logit, drop out after
subtracting the vocabulary-mean row, or become negative evidence when the
comparison token uses that feature more strongly.

The aggregate metrics follow this pattern. In the five-model Qwen/Gemma suite,
reference contrasts are easiest: 301 top-token-versus-vocabulary-mean cases give
1,505 model--case rows with median \(\rho=0.021\) and perfect sign agreement.
Pairwise and group contrasts are harder: top-1 versus top-5 margins have median
\(\rho=0.253\), while top-1 versus top-2 margins fall to \(0.824\) sign
agreement. We therefore interpret each display only at its stated score and
report the local sign and error; full counts and examples are in
\Cref{tab:app-general-readout-query-families} and
Appendix~\ref{app:query-family-display-example}.

\subsection{Context Reweights Fixed Token-Row Coefficients}

The converse experiment keeps the token row fixed and changes the prompt. Any
change in the displayed bars must then come from the hidden state's projections,
not from a different set of token-row coefficients.

\paragraph{Context reweighting.}
In \Cref{fig:same-token-polysemy}, Qwen3.5-2B reweights
\texttt{bug}, \texttt{ring}, and \texttt{bridge} toward context-appropriate
feature families.
Each displayed centered-token account has relative reconstruction error below
\(0.05\), using the same \(\rho\) convention as the fidelity tables. For example,
the \texttt{bug} row shifts from mosquito- and bee-like features to defect-,
crash-, and debug-like features; \texttt{bridge} shifts from river/crossing
features to networking features. Additional prompt-level views are in
Appendix~\ref{app:additional-qwen-display-examples}. Because the fitted
token-row coefficients are fixed after training, these changes isolate the
context-dependent projection term.

\subsection{Readout Feature Terms Compose with Direct Logit Attribution}
\label{sec:feature-resolved-component-decomposition}

Because the bars are linear in the final hidden state, we can split them across
standard additive residual-stream decompositions. This asks where the supporting
and opposing readout evidence is written in the realized forward pass.

\paragraph{Component-level readout terms.}
SRP therefore composes with residual-stream direct logit attribution (DLA)
\citep{elhage2021framework,wang2022ioi,nguyen2024logitprisms}. If
\(h\approx\sum_c h_c\), an SAE feature term for target \(q\) splits into
\[
    M_{c,i}(q)=\beta_i(q)\,h_c^\top d_i .
\]
For \(q=w_A-w_B\), \(\beta_i(q)=z_{A,i}-z_{B,i}\); for
\(q=w_A-\bar{w}_{\mathcal{V}}\),
\(\beta_i(q)=z_{A,i}-\bar{z}_{\mathcal{V},i}\). This yields additive
component-by-feature attribution from one forward pass. The same terms can serve
as inputs to later causal interventions
\citep{geiger2023causalabstraction} that test component or feature necessity.

    \caption{\textbf{Feature-resolved component attribution for a
    \texttt{verify}-\texttt{assume} margin.}
    Rows are residual components; columns group feature families, unembedding
    residual, and ordinary DLA. Red supports \texttt{verify}; blue supports

    \label{fig:qwen-prism-dla-verify-assume-main}

\Cref{fig:qwen-prism-dla-verify-assume-main} applies this decomposition to a
Qwen3.5-2B
\texttt{verify}-minus-\texttt{assume} target. The exact margin is \(+4.31\), the
residual-stream DLA sum is \(+4.36\), and the sparse feature sum is \(+4.28\)
(\(\rho=0.009\)), separating verification/checking support from
assumption-family opposition. A row-centered \texttt{verify} companion appears in
Appendix~\ref{app:feature-resolved-dla}.

\subsection{Selected Readout Features Move Held-Out Lexical Scores}
\label{sec:readout-feature-edits}

Finally, we test whether directions selected from local SRP accounts act as
narrow readout-side controls for the corresponding lexical target.

\paragraph{Constrained readout edit.}
We edit logits along ten Qwen3.5-2B decoder directions selected from five
discovery terms, then measure candidate-constrained lexical logit differences on
discovery and disjoint held-out tokens.
The edit adds a fixed logit-space vector derived from the selected decoder
directions; discovery terms choose the directions, while held-out terms test
whether the same readout-side directions move related lexical scores that were
not directly selected.

\Cref{tab:main-readout-edit} shows that SRP features selected from discovery

constrained movement of the specified readout scores, rather than broad
behavioral control.
