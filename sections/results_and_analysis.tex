\section{Calibrating Sparse Readout Accounts}
\label{sec:results}
\label{subsec:calibration}

We first calibrate the factorized readout against the dense LM head and
selected readout scores. Across the evaluated models, SRP preserves contrast
signs and most contrasts satisfy the low-error threshold, with errors and
flips concentrated near small exact margins. This calibration sets the
operating regime for the rest of the section: we read feature decompositions
when reconstructed and exact signed scores agree and satisfy \(\rho<0.5\)
(defined in \Cref{sec:method-reading-local-accounts}). The subsequent analyses
use this regime to examine score choice, token preferences, context shifts,
attribution, and edits.

The sparse factorization preserves unembedding rows across the suite (rowEV

\paragraph{Selected-Score Reconstruction}\label{subsec:selected-score-reconstruction}
The logit-difference columns test whether selected readout scores survive the
factorization. Sign agreement is at least \(0.91\) across the suite, and the
low-error fraction (\(\rho<0.5\))
covers \(0.71\)--\(0.89\) of contrasts per model.
Errors and flips concentrate near small exact margins
(\Cref{fig:readout-score-fidelity-result}). This score-level evidence supplies
the subset used for the case studies; small-margin scores remain in the
aggregate statistics. In this regime, reference-style contrasts and separated
margins reconstruct more reliably than heterogeneous row combinations or
small exact margins
(\Cref{tab:benchmark-derived-readout-contrasts,tab:readout-score-families};
Appendix~\ref{app:selected-readout-score-family-display-example}). The case
studies that follow are drawn from this subset.

Beyond hand-picked token pairs, we instantiate benchmark-derived readout
contrasts as fixed LM-head row combinations: answer-vs-distractor,
abstention-vs-entity, label, and safe-vs-action contrasts drawn from
SQuAD2, HotpotQA, LegalBench (ContractNLI), and SecurityEval
\citep{rajpurkar2018squad2,yang2018hotpotqa,koreeda2021contractnli,guha2023legalbench,siddiq2022securityeval}.
We use these rows as readout-contrast reconstruction tests rather than
task-accuracy measurements. Across
300 such contrasts, Qwen3.5-2B reaches median \(\rho=0.129\), sign agreement
\(0.943\), and sign-preserved low-error coverage \(0.840\); Qwen3.5-0.8B
reaches \(0.180\), \(0.983\), and \(0.933\). Per-family results span perfect
low-error coverage on ContractNLI labels to weaker SecurityEval safe/action
contrasts: broader row combinations remain reconstructable but heterogeneous
(\Cref{tab:benchmark-derived-readout-contrasts}).

Across these checks, rowEV functions as a capacity metric; term-level
interpretation comes from score-level residual and sign checks.

For the full logit-difference set used in this baseline comparison, SRP attains
lower median error and a larger sign-preserved low-error subset than nearest-row ridge
\citep{hoerl1970ridge}, PCA \citep{jolliffe2002principal}, shuffled sparse

lexical proximity between scored tokens; shuffling sparse codes preserves
the sparsity pattern but breaks the learned row--code assignment; random
sparse patterns hold sparse capacity constant. PCA at 1024 components is
the generous dense reference; at SRP's realized per-score compactness (a
median of 76 features carries \(80\%\) of absolute contribution mass,
against 78 for PCA-256) the dense reference drops to coverage \(0.272\).

\paragraph{Fidelity Varies by Score Family}\label{subsec:score-family-fidelity}
The factorization reconstructs some score families more faithfully than
others. \Cref{tab:readout-score-families} reports sign
agreement and median relative residual by family at the \(k=256\)
comparison point. Reconstruction difficulty tracks how close the
competitor sits to the selected token: vocabulary-mean and reference-row
contrasts are easiest, with perfect sign agreement and median
\(\rho\le0.048\), and broad top-competitor margins stay nearly as
faithful (\(\rho=0.113\) and \(0.215\) for a sampled or top-5
competitor). Difficulty rises as the competitor nears a tie: the
current-vs-stale and top1-vs-top2 margins reach median \(\rho=0.369\) and
\(0.342\), sign agreement falls to \(0.861\) and \(0.824\), and only
\(0.61\) and \(0.58\) of their rows pass the joint sign-and-residual
gate, against \(0.996\) for the vocabulary-mean family, so their
feature terms are read only where the checks pass.
The pattern has a structural cause: the contrast residual
\(h^\top(r_A-r_B)\) is set by the unembedding-row residuals and contains
no term that scales with the margin, so relative error grows as a
contrast approaches a tie, where the floored \(\rho\) reduces to an
absolute-error bound (\S\ref{sec:method-reading-local-accounts}).
Near-tie margins are also the contrasts that decide model behavior, so
the gate excludes some of the scores a user may care most about; a
near-tie account that passes carries an error bound comparable to the
margin itself, and we read its terms as evidence about composition,
with the decision left to the exact margin.
Because reconstruction difficulty depends on which family is selected,
SRP keeps the choice of readout direction explicit and reports the
residual and sign behavior that determine when feature terms can be
interpreted.

\section{Readout Analyses}
\label{sec:readout-analyses}

The readout analyses use Qwen3.5-2B as the main low-error display setting,
because its readout passes the interpretation checks and its decompositions
are small enough to display legibly, and report selected R1-Distill-Qwen-7B
replications in the appendix. The
cross-model results above support fidelity claims across the evaluated
readouts; the examples below show how the fixed SRP basis is used for local
readout interpretation in this Qwen3.5-2B setting. The section is a set of
readout-side analyses over realized forward passes.

\begin{description}[leftmargin=!,labelwidth=2.7em]
    \item[RQ1:] Do the features SRP places on each side of a
    token-vs-token contrast correspond to interpretable lexical groups?
    \item[RQ2:] Does prompt context reweight the features contributing to
    a fixed token's logit?
    \item[RQ3:] Do SRP feature terms compose with residual-stream component
    attribution?
    \item[RQ4:] Do the learned decoder directions support constrained
    lexical edits on held-out tokens?
\end{description}

\paragraph{Logit Differences Expose Competing Features [RQ1]}\label{subsec:feature-competition}\label{subsec:lens-to-sparse-account}
Lens-style readouts identify a favored token or contrast but leave it as a
dense LM-head dot product; in \Cref{fig:main-lens-prism-comparison}, SRP
holds the \texttt{bug}-minus-\texttt{insect} score fixed and decomposes it
into signed readout terms plus an explicit residual, in logit units. The
signed features such a decomposition places on each side of the contrast
often cohere as lexical groups; the label audit below quantifies how
often. As the score
\(q\) changes, a given feature may support a token, oppose it, or drop out
after mean subtraction, and the decomposition reads off this competition
directly from the signed terms. The opening figure showed the
software-context \texttt{bug}-vs-\texttt{insect} decomposition;
\Cref{fig:selected-readout-prism-examples} sets it beside its
insect-context counterpart, where the competitor becomes the
software-sense anchor (\texttt{bug}-vs-\texttt{error}), and two
further polysemous-token differences (\texttt{bark}-vs-\texttt{dog},
\texttt{bass}-vs-\texttt{trout}), where a faithful account must reassign
signed terms across senses. In the insect context, \texttt{bug} draws
positive contributions from mosquito-, bee-, and beetle-labeled rows and
negative contributions from error-token rows (\(\rho=0.185\)); in the
software context, positive contributions shift to defect, crash, and
debug rows (\(\rho=0.003\)). The \texttt{bark} and \texttt{bass} cases
show the same reassignment (\(\rho=0.126\) and \(0.097\); all four
unfloored); the
residuals are reported because they bound the interpretation. The same
surface token recruits different row-labeled feature groups as score and
context change. Plotting conventions and the label audit are in
Appendix~\ref{app:display-taxonomy} and
Table~\ref{tab:app-main-case-study-feature-audit}.

We treat feature labels as readability summaries of top rows and audit them
separately from the measured feature ids and signed terms
(Appendix~\ref{app:main-case-study-feature-audit}). The audit is an
author-conducted comparison of each claim-bearing label against its
feature's top unembedding rows. Of 99 claim-bearing
labels, 85 name a coherent lexical family and 14 are token-form surface
artifacts; 66 of the 85 coherent families are on-target, with the sense
matching the panel's named family and the signed term pointing to the
claimed side. Panel-level claims use only that on-target subset, and the
one third of labels that fail the audit bound how far label-level
readings generalize beyond it.

\paragraph{Context Reweights Fixed Token-Row Features [RQ2]}\label{subsec:context-reweighting}
Holding the token fixed isolates context effects: the row-side coefficients
\(z_{t,i}\) are frozen by the token, and only the projections \(p_i(h)\) vary
across prompts (\Cref{sec:method-local-score}). In
\Cref{fig:same-token-polysemy}, prompt context reweights \texttt{bug},
\texttt{ring}, and \texttt{bridge} toward features whose row labels match the
setting; each token score (after subtracting the shared offset
\(h^\top\mu\)) has unfloored \(\rho<0.05\). \texttt{bug} shifts from mosquito/bee to
defect/crash/debug labels; \texttt{bridge} shifts from river/crossing to
networking labels. Context redistributes mass across a fixed token-row
decomposition without changing its row-side coefficients. Additional views
are in Appendix~\ref{app:additional-qwen-display-examples}.

\paragraph{Where Term-Level Reading Stops}\label{subsec:interpretation-boundary}
Before composing feature terms with component attribution (RQ3) and editing
along fitted directions (RQ4), we state where term-level reading stops.
A faithful decomposition is a local reading of the readout side: a
different factorizer or active budget could split the same contribution
across other features, so the fidelity checks license the reading only for
this factorizer and active budget. Appendix~\ref{app:robustness-operating-regime}
sets out what can still be read when a check fails, routing each
failing condition (residual-dominated, sign-flipped, model-unsupported,
diffuse, or token-confounded scores) to the part that still holds. Where the
residual dominates a decomposition or its terms repeatedly change sign, the
audit has failed and the score is reported as uninterpretable rather than
silently dropped. These conditions bind most often at the lower-fidelity Gemma
and R1-distilled rows of \Cref{tab:readout-score-fidelity-summary}.

\paragraph{Readout Features Compose with Component Attribution [RQ3]}\label{sec:feature-resolved-component-decomposition}\label{subsec:feature-resolved-component-attribution}
Signed readout terms are linear in \(h\), so direct logit attribution (DLA)
composes with the fixed SRP basis. DLA decomposes \(h\) into embedding,
attention-head, and MLP-layer contributions and reads each component's logit
contribution \citep{elhage2021framework,wang2022ioi,nguyen2024logitprisms};
under \(h\approx\sum_c h_c\), each feature term for direction \(q\)
decomposes as \(M_{c,i}(q)=\beta_i(q)\,h_c^\top d_i\).

Each component thus yields a feature-resolved breakdown of the realized score,
extending the scalar DLA contribution into SRP coordinates.

\Cref{fig:qwen-prism-dla-verify-assume-main} applies this to a Qwen3.5-2B
source-verification prompt, asking whether the readout favors the
source-checking token \texttt{verify} over the assumption token
\texttt{assume}, a resolved single-sense contrast whose interest lies in
which components carry it; the feature-resolved view separates
verification/checking support from assumption-family opposition. The exact
margin is \(+4.31\); DLA sums to \(+4.36\) (component additivity error
\(+0.05\)) and the sparse-feature sum is \(+4.28\) (unfloored
\(\rho=0.009\); \S\ref{sec:method-reading-local-accounts}). A
row-centered companion appears in Appendix~\ref{app:feature-resolved-dla}.

\paragraph{Readout Features Support Constrained Lexical Edits [RQ4]}\label{subsec:constrained-readout-edits}
The learned decoder directions also serve as edit directions: subtracting a
fixed multiple of a decoder direction from the final hidden state lowers the
logits of tokens that load positively on that direction. We test this as a
readout-side stress test in a constrained lexical-score setting. Five discovery terms select ten
Qwen3.5-2B decoder directions; the edit is then evaluated on candidate
pairs built from held-out terms disjoint from the discovery set. A
``flip'' counts when the top-1 next token moves
away from the target term under the edit. Two logit-bias baselines bracket
the readout-feature direction edit. The \emph{discovery token bias} subtracts a fixed
logit penalty from the five discovery terms' token ids only and so by
construction
cannot transfer beyond them. The \emph{oracle token bias} applies the same
penalty directly to the held-out terms, an upper bound that
uses the evaluation labels at intervention time.

\Cref{tab:main-readout-edit} shows that sparse readout feature directions
selected from discovery terms transfer to held-out evaluation candidates
(flip rate \(0.359\), \(23/64\); cluster-bootstrap 95\% CI
\(0.250\)--\(0.469\)), while the equally informed discovery token bias does
not transfer at all (eval flip \(0.000\)). A candidate can flip only when
the unedited model already prefers the profane side, which holds for 23 of
the 64 held-out candidates, so \(0.359\) is the ceiling of this evaluation;
at scale 16 SRP and the oracle each flip exactly that set, and the
identical rates follow from saturation. The dose-response sweep separates
the methods below the ceiling: at scale 4 the oracle flips 20 of the 23
against SRP's 11, and the oracle saturates by scale 6 while the SRP edit
reaches the ceiling at scale 12
(Appendix~\ref{app:lexical-control-stress-test}). The comparison
also separates transfer from distributional specificity: the oracle
reaches the ceiling at median KL \(0.004\) because it uses the evaluation
labels at intervention time, while SRP obtains its transfer from
discovery-selected basis directions at a broader distribution shift
(median KL \(0.679\)). The R1-Distill-Qwen-7B replication is in
Appendix~\ref{app:lexical-control-stress-test}.
