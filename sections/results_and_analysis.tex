\section{Results and Analysis}
\label{sec:results}

We first identify when SRP accounts are reliable enough to read: LM-head
replacement, target reconstruction, sign preservation, relative error \(\rho\),
and sparse-structure baselines define the operating regime. We then use
sign-preserved, low-error accounts to analyze benchmark-format targets, target
choice, prompt context, residual-stream components, and constrained readout-side
edits. Unless stated otherwise, aggregate low-error counts use \(\rho<0.5\).

distributions, and pairwise-margin size and sign to mark examples whose residuals

On the full Qwen3.5-2B logit-difference set, SRP has the lowest median error and

\subsection{Scalar Readout Targets Extend Beyond Token Pairs}

To test whether SRP handles task-shaped readout questions, we construct
benchmark-format readout targets from dataset-derived linear contrasts. We
instantiate QA \citep{rajpurkar2018squad2,yang2018hotpotqa}, abstention,
ContractNLI labels via LegalBench
\citep{koreeda2021contractnli,guha2023legalbench}, and SecurityEval safe/unsafe
actions \citep{siddiq2022securityeval}; QA contrasts, for example, average
gold-answer rows against fixed distractor rows. These targets evaluate
readout-target reconstruction rather than end-to-end task accuracy.

\Cref{tab:benchmark-derived-task-targets} shows that answerable-QA,
multi-hop-distractor, and legal-label rows have lower error than SecurityEval,
whose safe/unsafe-action contrast spans many lexical forms. SRP therefore
reconstructs several dataset-derived linear contrasts beyond curated token pairs
while remaining a readout-level fidelity test. Representative low-error QA
accounts appear in Appendix~\ref{app:benchmark-format-display-examples}.

\subsection{Local Score Accounts Expose Feature Competition Within Token Rankings}

With the low-error regime identified, we inspect token-vs-token accounts. Labels
summarize high-coefficient rows; feature ids, signed contributions, and residuals
are measured.

\paragraph{Polysemous-token case studies.}
SRP decomposes low-error Qwen3.5-2B polysemous-token logit differences
(\Cref{fig:selected-readout-prism-examples}). In insect context,
\texttt{bug} draws positive mosquito-, bee-, and beetle-labeled terms against
error-token rows; in software, positives shift toward defect, crash, and debug
rows. The panels have relative errors \(0.185\) and \(0.003\). Additional
\texttt{bark}/\texttt{bass} cases, plotting conventions, and label audits appear in
\cref{fig:app-selected-readout-prism-extra-examples,app:display-taxonomy,tab:app-main-case-study-feature-audit}.
These sign-preserved, low-\(\rho\) examples show that the same surface token can
be supported by different row-labeled feature groups as target and context
change. The next sections separate those factors; aggregate frequencies are in
the appendix.

\subsection{Target Choice Selects Signed Terms from Shared Readout Feature Projections}

Having established a low-error operating regime, we next ask how the selected
target changes the account. We compare scoring questions on the same hidden
states, from reference contrasts to sharper pair and group margins.

\paragraph{Target-family reliability.}
Target coefficients turn projections into signed evidence: a feature may support
a raw logit, drop out after mean subtraction, or become negative evidence when
the comparison token uses it more.

In the five-model Qwen/Gemma subset, 301 top-token-versus-vocabulary-mean cases
give 1,505 model--case rows with median \(\rho=0.021\) and perfect sign
agreement. Pairwise/group contrasts are harder: top-1 versus top-5 margins have
median \(\rho=0.253\), while top-1 versus top-2 margins fall to \(0.824\) sign
agreement. This pattern shows why local accounts must name the target they
decompose: reference contrasts are easy to reconstruct, while sharper competitor
margins are the stress test. We interpret each display at its stated score and
report local sign/error; full counts/examples are in
\Cref{tab:app-general-readout-query-families} and
Appendix~\ref{app:readout-target-family-display-example}.

\subsection{Readout Feature Terms Compose with Direct Logit Attribution}
\label{sec:feature-resolved-component-decomposition}

Because the bars are linear in the final hidden state, additive residual-stream
decompositions can split them by component.

\paragraph{Component-level readout terms.}
SRP composes with residual-stream direct logit attribution (DLA)
\citep{elhage2021framework,wang2022ioi,nguyen2024logitprisms}. If
\(h\approx\sum_c h_c\), an SAE feature term for target \(q\) splits into
\[
    M_{c,i}(q)=\beta_i(q)\,h_c^\top d_i .
\]
For \(q=w_A-w_B\), \(\beta_i(q)=z_{A,i}-z_{B,i}\); for
\(q=w_A-\bar{w}_{\mathcal{V}}\),
\(\beta_i(q)=z_{A,i}-\bar{z}_{\mathcal{V},i}\). This yields additive
component-by-feature attribution; later interventions can test necessity
\citep{geiger2023causalabstraction}.

    \caption{\textbf{Feature-resolved component attribution for a

    Rows are residual components; columns group feature-label families,

\Cref{fig:qwen-prism-dla-verify-assume-main} decomposes a Qwen3.5-2B

verification/checking support from assumption-family opposition. A row-centered
\texttt{verify} companion appears in Appendix~\ref{app:feature-resolved-dla}.

\subsection{Selected Readout Features Move Held-Out Lexical Scores}
\label{sec:readout-feature-edits}

Finally, we ask whether directions selected from local accounts transfer to
held-out lexical readout scores.

\paragraph{Constrained readout edit.}
The test edits logits along ten Qwen3.5-2B decoder directions selected from five
discovery terms, then measures specified lexical logit differences on discovery
and held-out tokens. It is candidate-constrained rather than an open-ended
generation evaluation.
