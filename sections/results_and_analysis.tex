\section{Validating the Readout Basis}
\label{sec:results}
\label{subsec:calibration}
\label{sec:experimental-setup}

The stages below test \emph{faithfulness}, \emph{distinctness},
\emph{reproducibility}, and \emph{ablation consistency} in turn, and
each could fail on its own evidence. Decompositions
are read under the reading rule of
\Cref{sec:method-reading-local-accounts}.

\paragraph{Setup.}
The suite spans Qwen3.5~\citep{qwen35collection}, Gemma-4
\citep{gemma4official,gemma4collection}, Ministral~\citep{liu2026ministral3},
and reasoning-distilled Qwen/Llama readouts \citep{deepseekai2025deepseekr1}
at 0.8B--9B scale. We fit one readout SAE per model to the rows of its
final LM head (selected on row-reconstruction, replacement, and
code-usage metrics, before any contrast is seen) and evaluate it on
10{,}000 held-out C4 continuations
\citep{raffel2020exploring,dodge2021documenting} and on fixed,
model-agnostic banks of selected scores (\(\sim\)1{,}350 items per model
over \(\sim\)850 base cases, the resampling unit for bootstrap
intervals). A positive
margin says the readout favors \(A\) over \(B\), not that \(A\) is
right. Only the separate benchmark-derived bank takes \(A\) from a gold
label. The model split is fixed once. All eight models enter the
reconstruction analysis, and the six whose readouts pass the replacement
check (top-1 agreement between reconstructed and original argmax tokens
on held-out states, \(\ge 0.75\); both Gemma-4 variants fall below it)
enter the baseline, reproducibility, and intervention comparisons.
Appendices~\ref{app:model-suite-selection}
and~\ref{app:query-bank-provenance} describe the suite, bank families,
provenance, and filters.

\paragraph{The Factorization Preserves the Readout and the Scores}\label{subsec:readout-replacement-fidelity}
\label{subsec:selected-score-reconstruction}%
\Cref{tab:readout-score-fidelity-summary} reports fidelity at two levels,
the readout as a whole and the individual selected scores. Interpretation rests on
replacement fidelity rather than on aggregate row reconstruction. We check each
readout by top-token agreement and KL on held-out hidden states before any
local decomposition, since rows with large residual norms can dominate the
softmax even when reconstruction is accurate in aggregate
(Appendix~\ref{app:readout-norm-tails}). Row-centered explained variance
is 0.83--0.89 across the suite, but replacement separates the models.
The six passing readouts reach top-1 agreement 0.75--0.90 at KL
\(\le 0.49\), while the two Gemma-4 variants reach 0.33 and 0.74 at KL
6.37 and 1.22. At the score level, sign agreement is at least \(0.91\)
across the suite, with residual error and the few sign flips confined to
near-tie margins, which are hardest because the contrast residual does
not scale with the margin (Appendix
Figure~\ref{fig:readout-score-fidelity-result}, Appendix
Table~\ref{tab:readout-score-families}).\label{subsec:score-family-fidelity}
Case studies below are drawn from the passing subset, small-margin
scores remain in the aggregates, and fidelity extends beyond curated
pairs to 300 benchmark-derived contrasts
(Appendix~\ref{app:benchmark-derived-display-examples},
Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-derived-readout-contrasts}).

If SRP's codes merely repackaged \(\WU\) geometry, methods built directly
from that geometry should reconstruct selected scores equally well. They
do not (\Cref{tab:main-baseline-null-comparison}). Across all six
models, SRP reconstructs 0.72--0.81 of contrasts with the correct sign
and error below half the score's magnitude (sign-preserved low-error
coverage), leading the strongest of six alternatives by 8.9--17.3
points with non-overlapping intervals. The lead does not depend on the
choice of summary statistic, since SRP also has the lowest mean,
95th-percentile, and maximum absolute error of the seven methods, and
what error remains concentrates in the near-tie contrasts the reading
rule already excludes (Appendix~\ref{app:error-tails}).
The comparison controls for capacity. The k-means
centroid dictionary at \(D{=}65{,}536\), \(k{=}256\) matches SRP's width
and sparsity exactly and still trails by 18 points on the display model
(Qwen3.5-2B), and shuffled-code and random-pattern nulls collapse
entirely (\Cref{tab:app-cross-model-nulls}). Learned sparse row--code
structure, not generic row proximity, carries the reconstruction. The
lead persists one level up, at the scale of token \emph{groupings}, the
sets of vocabulary rows one feature ties together. A held-out
same-recipe dictionary recovers 72--75\% of the seed-stable grouping
core against 43--49\% for row-kNN and 21--25\% for hard clustering, with
cross-recipe agreement weaker and disclosed
(Appendix~\ref{app:basis-stability}).

\paragraph{Explanations Reproduce Across Independent Runs}\label{subsec:stability}
Individual decoder directions are not canonical. Across three
independently trained dictionaries per width, with equal held-out
reconstruction verified, matched decoder cosines for used features
average only \(\sim\)0.33 at the 32\(\times\) width, consistent with
reported TopK seed-sensitivity \citep{paulo2025stability}. What a
decomposition says, however, does reproduce. Over 63 curated contrasts,
the tokens each seed's decomposition places on each side reproduce
across every seed pair at \(\sim\)15\(\times\) the cross-contrast null,
with 100\% of contrasts above the null's 90th percentile at both widths
and cross-side leakage at or below 0.005. Further, 87--91\% of used
feature groups have an above-chance counterpart in an independently
trained dictionary, the median group 83--90\% covered by a union of at
most three features. Structure reproduces even though indexing does not. The
residual instability is feature splitting rather than noise, with
one seed's feature reappearing as several in another. These
statements are within-recipe (fixed width, sparsity, preprocessing), and
cross-recipe variation is larger and disclosed in
Appendix~\ref{app:basis-stability}.

\paragraph{Predicted Contributions Versus Measured Ablation}\label{subsec:causal-validation}
The decomposition is consistent with direct ablation. On all six
models, predicted feature contributions match the margin change measured
when the feature's direction is deleted from the hidden state
(readout-side ablation) at \(r^2=0.83\)--\(0.93\), while random
directions explain at most 0.06
(\Cref{tab:causal-validation-summary}). Sparse-code misattribution,
decoder non-orthogonality, and the row residual would each degrade this
agreement, and the slope inflation bounds their combined effect. Slopes
are near 1 on the 7--8B readouts and overstate magnitudes on the three
Qwen readouts, with sign and rank preserved
(Appendix~\ref{app:causal-validation}). These are
local, readout-side predictions on realized forward passes, not
circuit-level necessity or generation-level effects.

\paragraph{Context Decides Which Features Carry a Token}\label{subsec:label-audit}\label{subsec:same-token-analysis}\label{subsec:feature-competition}\label{subsec:lens-to-sparse-account}\label{subsec:context-reweighting}
Holding a token fixed freezes its row coefficients \(z_{t,i}\) and
leaves only the projections \(p_i(h)\) free
(\S\ref{sec:method-local-score}), so context alone decides which
readout features carry its score. Worked cases on Qwen3.5-2B are read
under the operating-regime conditions of
Appendix~\ref{subsec:interpretation-boundary}. The software-context
\texttt{bug}-minus-\texttt{insect} margin of
\Cref{fig:main-lens-prism-comparison} is carried by defect, crash, and
debug rows (\(\tilde\rho=0.003\)). In an insect context, against the
software-sense anchor \texttt{error}, the same token's support comes
from mosquito-, bee-, and beetle-labeled rows (\(\tilde\rho=0.185\)),
and \texttt{bark}, \texttt{bass}, \texttt{ring}, and \texttt{bridge}
reassign signed terms across senses the same way. Labels are
readability summaries of top unembedding rows, kept separate from the
measured feature ids and signed terms and used only where audited. On
the stricter of two audits, 66 of the 99 claim-bearing display labels
name a coherent family that matches the sense their panel claims
\citep{karvonen2025saebench,makelov2024principled}. These are worked
analyses of individual scores, not a systematic sense-disambiguation
evaluation. Appendices~\ref{app:additional-margin-case-studies}
and~\ref{app:main-case-study-feature-audit} give displays, per-case
terms, and the audit protocol.\label{sec:feature-resolved-component-decomposition}\label{subsec:constrained-readout-edits}
The basis also splits each residual-stream component's direct logit
attribution into per-feature terms
(Appendix~\ref{app:feature-resolved-dla}), and its decoder directions
support constrained readout-side edits
(Appendix~\ref{app:lexical-control-stress-test}). With the basis
validated on its own evidence, the next section applies it to a question
token readings cannot settle.
