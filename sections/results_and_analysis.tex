\section{Calibrating Sparse Readout Accounts}
\label{sec:results}
\label{subsec:calibration}

We first calibrate the factorized readout against the dense LM head and
selected readout scores. Across the evaluated models, SRP preserves contrast
signs and most contrasts satisfy the low-error threshold, with errors and
flips concentrated near small exact margins. This calibration sets the
operating regime for the rest of the section: we read feature decompositions
when reconstructed and exact signed scores agree and satisfy \(\rho<0.5\)
(defined in \Cref{sec:method-reading-local-accounts}). The subsequent analyses
use this regime to examine score choice, token preferences, context shifts,
attribution, and edits.

The sparse factorization preserves unembedding rows across the suite (rowEV
\(0.83\)--\(0.89\)) and tracks the dense readout on six of eight models (top-1
agreement \(\geq 0.75\) and KL \(\leq 0.5\);
\Cref{tab:readout-score-fidelity-summary}). The two deviations show that high
rowEV does not guarantee argmax behavior: row reconstruction can be accurate
in aggregate while a few unembedding rows with large residual norms dominate
the softmax (Appendix~\ref{app:readout-norm-tails}). This argues for reporting
replacement fidelity before any local decomposition.

\paragraph{Selected-Score Reconstruction}\label{subsec:selected-score-reconstruction}
The logit-difference columns test whether selected readout scores survive the
factorization. Sign agreement is at least \(0.91\) across the suite, and the
sign-preserved, low-error subset (\(\rho<0.5\), with
\(\rho=|s_{\mathrm{exact}}-s_{\mathrm{recon}}|/(|s_{\mathrm{exact}}|+0.5)\))
covers \(0.69\)--\(0.89\) of contrasts per model.
Errors and flips concentrate near small exact margins
(\Cref{fig:readout-score-fidelity-result}). This score-level evidence supplies
the subset used for the case studies; small-margin scores remain in the
aggregate statistics. In this regime, reference-style contrasts and separated
margins reconstruct more reliably than heterogeneous row combinations or
small exact margins
(\Cref{tab:benchmark-derived-readout-contrasts,tab:app-general-readout-score-families};
Appendix~\ref{app:selected-readout-score-family-display-example}). The case
studies that follow are drawn from this subset.

Beyond hand-picked token pairs, we instantiate benchmark-derived readout
contrasts as fixed LM-head row combinations: answer-vs-distractor,
abstention-vs-entity, label, and safe-vs-unsafe contrasts drawn from
SQuAD2, HotpotQA, ContractNLI, LegalBench, and SecurityEval
\citep{rajpurkar2018squad2,yang2018hotpotqa,koreeda2021contractnli,guha2023legalbench,siddiq2022securityeval}.
We use these rows as readout-contrast reconstruction tests rather than
task-accuracy measurements. Across
300 such contrasts, Qwen3.5-2B reaches median \(\rho=0.129\), sign agreement
\(0.943\), and sign-preserved low-error coverage \(0.840\); Qwen3.5-0.8B
reaches \(0.180\), \(0.983\), and \(0.933\). Per-family results span perfect
low-error coverage on ContractNLI labels to weaker SecurityEval safe/action
contrasts: broader row combinations remain reconstructable but heterogeneous
(\Cref{tab:benchmark-derived-readout-contrasts}).

RowEV is therefore reported as a capacity metric; term-level interpretation
comes from score-level residual and sign checks.

For the full logit-difference set used in this baseline comparison, SRP attains
lower median error and a larger sign-preserved low-error subset than nearest-row ridge
\citep{hoerl1970ridge}, PCA \citep{jolliffe2002principal}, shuffled sparse
codes, and random sparse patterns (\Cref{tab:main-baseline-null-comparison}).
Each baseline tests a different alternative explanation: ridge isolates
lexical proximity between scored tokens; PCA at matched rank isolates dense
low-rank geometry; shuffling sparse codes preserves the sparsity pattern but
breaks the learned row--code assignment; random sparse patterns hold sparse
capacity constant. The gap therefore supports learned sparse row-code structure
in SRP's reconstructions; label interpretation is handled by the separate audit
below.

\section{Readout Analyses}
\label{sec:readout-analyses}

The readout analyses use Qwen3.5-2B as the main low-error display setting and
report selected R1-Distill-Qwen-7B replications in the appendix. The
cross-model results above support fidelity claims across the evaluated
readouts; the examples below show how the fixed SRP basis is used for local
readout interpretation in this Qwen3.5-2B setting. The section is a set of
readout-side analyses over realized forward passes.

\begin{description}[leftmargin=!,labelwidth=2.7em]
    \item[RQ1:] Does an SRP decomposition reveal which features drive a
    logit-lens score on a single contrast?
    \item[RQ2:] Do the features SRP places on each side of a
    token-vs-token contrast correspond to interpretable lexical groups?
    \item[RQ3:] Does prompt context reweight the features contributing to
    a fixed token's logit?
    \item[RQ4:] Do SRP feature terms compose with residual-stream component
    attribution?
    \item[RQ5:] Do the learned decoder directions support constrained
    lexical edits on held-out tokens?
\end{description}

\paragraph{Lens Preferences Become Sparse Readout Accounts [RQ1]}\label{subsec:lens-to-sparse-account}
Lens-style readouts identify a favored token or contrast but leave it as a
dense LM-head dot product. In \Cref{fig:main-lens-prism-comparison}, SRP holds
the \texttt{verify}-minus-\texttt{assume} score fixed and decomposes it into
signed readout terms plus an explicit residual. Positive and negative terms
support or oppose the chosen side, replacing the single dense score with a
signed, sparse account in logit units.

\paragraph{Logit Differences Expose Competing Features [RQ2]}\label{subsec:feature-competition}
Beyond a single contrast, the signed features SRP places on each side of a
token-vs-token contrast typically cohere as lexical groups. As the score
\(q\) changes, a given feature may support a token, oppose it, or drop out
after mean subtraction, and the decomposition reads off this competition
directly from the signed terms. Using the fixed SRP basis, we decompose
Qwen3.5-2B logit differences over polysemous surface tokens such as
\texttt{bug} or \texttt{bark}, whose intended sense depends on context
(\Cref{fig:selected-readout-prism-examples}). In an insect context,
\texttt{bug} draws positive contributions from mosquito-, bee-, and
beetle-labeled rows and negative contributions from error-token rows
(\(\rho=0.185\)); in a software context, positive contributions shift to
defect, crash, and debug rows (\(\rho=0.003\)). The same surface token
recruits different row-labeled feature groups as score and context change.
Additional \texttt{bark}/\texttt{bass} cases are in
Figure~\ref{fig:app-selected-readout-prism-extra-examples}; plotting
conventions and the label audit are in
Appendix~\ref{app:display-taxonomy} and
Table~\ref{tab:app-main-case-study-feature-audit}.

We treat feature labels as readability summaries of top rows and audit them
separately from the measured feature ids and signed terms
(Appendix~\ref{app:main-case-study-feature-audit}). Of 99 claim-bearing labels,
67 are coherent lexical, 18 ambiguous, and 14 token-form; all 67 coherent labels
point to the signed side reported in the text.

\paragraph{Context Reweights Fixed Token-Row Features [RQ3]}\label{subsec:context-reweighting}
Holding the token fixed isolates context effects: the row-side coefficients
\(z_{t,i}\) are frozen by the token, and only the projections \(p_i(h)\) vary
across prompts (\Cref{sec:method-local-score}). In
\Cref{fig:same-token-polysemy}, prompt context reweights \texttt{bug},
\texttt{ring}, and \texttt{bridge} toward features whose row labels match the
setting; each token score (after subtracting the shared offset
\(h^\top\mu\)) has \(\rho<0.05\). \texttt{bug} shifts from mosquito/bee to
defect/crash/debug labels; \texttt{bridge} shifts from river/crossing to
networking labels. Context redistributes mass across a fixed token-row
decomposition without changing its row-side coefficients. Additional views
are in Appendix~\ref{app:additional-qwen-display-examples}.

\paragraph{Readout Features Compose with Component Attribution [RQ4]}\label{sec:feature-resolved-component-decomposition}\label{subsec:feature-resolved-component-attribution}
Signed readout terms are linear in \(h\), so direct logit attribution (DLA)
composes with the fixed SRP basis. DLA decomposes \(h\) into embedding,
attention-head, and MLP-layer contributions and reads each component's logit
contribution \citep{elhage2021framework,wang2022ioi,nguyen2024logitprisms};
under \(h\approx\sum_c h_c\), each feature term for direction \(q\)
decomposes as \(M_{c,i}(q)=\beta_i(q)\,h_c^\top d_i\).

    \centering

    \caption{\textbf{Feature-resolved component attribution.}
    Qwen3.5-2B \texttt{verify}-\texttt{assume} margin. Rows are embedding and
    per-layer post-norm contributions; columns group verify/check, assume,
    other sparse, LM-head residual, and ordinary DLA terms. Red supports
    \texttt{verify}; blue supports \texttt{assume}.}
    \label{fig:qwen-prism-dla-verify-assume-main}
\end{figure}

Each component thus yields a feature-resolved breakdown of the realized score,
extending the scalar DLA contribution into SRP coordinates.

\Cref{fig:qwen-prism-dla-verify-assume-main} applies this to a Qwen3.5-2B
\texttt{verify}-minus-\texttt{assume} logit difference, separating
verification/checking support from assumption-family opposition. The exact
margin is \(+4.31\); DLA sums to \(+4.36\) (component additivity error
\(+0.05\)) and the sparse feature sum to \(+4.28\) (\(\rho=0.009\)). A
row-centered companion appears in Appendix~\ref{app:feature-resolved-dla}.

\paragraph{Readout Features Support Constrained Lexical Edits [RQ5]}\label{subsec:constrained-readout-edits}
The learned decoder directions also serve as edit directions: subtracting a
fixed multiple of a decoder direction from the final hidden state lowers the
logits of tokens that load positively on that direction. We test this in a
constrained lexical-score setting. Five discovery terms select ten
Qwen3.5-2B decoder directions; the edit is then applied to a disjoint set
of evaluation tokens. A ``flip'' counts when the top-1 next token moves
away from the target word under the edit. Two logit-bias baselines bracket
the SAE-direction edit. The \emph{discovery token bias} subtracts a fixed
logit penalty from the five discovery tokens only and so by construction
cannot transfer beyond them. The \emph{oracle token bias} applies the same
penalty directly to the held-out evaluation tokens---an upper bound that
uses the evaluation labels at intervention time.
