\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Lens-style readout interfaces.}
The logit lens maps hidden states through the model's unembedding
\citep{nostalgebraist2020}; the tuned lens learns layerwise translators to
calibrate those predictions \citep{belrose2023tuned}. Future Lens asks whether
a single hidden state predicts later tokens \citep{pal2023future},
Patchscopes elicits continuations or descriptions by patching representations
into prompted contexts \citep{ghandeharioun2024patchscopes}, and the
contemporaneous Jacobian lens transports states through an averaged Jacobian
before decoding \citep{gurnee2026workspace}. These methods vary how the
hidden state is read out, but each reports tokens, and the fitted variants
inherit a fitting corpus; SRP keeps the readout interface, decomposes the
selected LM-head direction into sparse readout features, and thereby
compares what differently fitted lenses read on the same state
(\Cref{subsec:cross-lens}).

\paragraph{Output geometry and sparse row dictionaries.}
Output-embedding work studies how weight tying
\citep{press2017outputembedding,inan2017tying,lopardo2026weighttying},
softmax rank and next-token target geometry
\citep{yang2018softmaxbottleneck,zhao2024implicit}, and tokenizer
segmentation \citep{bostrom2020bpe} shape the rows used for vocabulary
scoring. Sparse coding of type-level embedding matrices is an established
NLP tool: non-negative sparse embeddings \citep{murphy2012nnse}, sparse
overcomplete word vectors \citep{faruqui2015sparse}, SPINE
\citep{subramanian2018spine}, and sparse sense-atom decompositions of
polysemous vectors \citep{arora2018polysemy} all recover interpretable
sparse codes from static word-embedding rows. SRP carries this row-side
approach to the LM head of a trained transformer and ties it to the
lens-score interface, where the dictionary is judged by replacement and
selected-score reconstruction. A
parallel sparse-dictionary line targets the hidden-state factor \(h\) of a
lens score \(h^\top q\): activation-SAEs learn overcomplete bases for \(h\)
\citep{bricken2023monosemanticity,cunningham2023sparse,gao2024scaling,templeton2024scaling},
with standardized diagnostics
\citep{karvonen2025saebench,makelov2024principled} and known caveats about
unit canonicity \citep{leask2025canonical}. SRP applies the same
machinery to the complementary factor \(q\), fitting it directly to LM-head
rows. The rationale transfers: a logit is a dot product of two superposed
vectors, so the structure that warrants decomposing \(h\) warrants
examining \(q\), and the readout rows in fact carry the same shared
structure (\S\ref{sec:results}). Parameter-space decompositions share the
weight-side target \citep{braun2025parameter,gao2025weightsparse}; SRP
keeps the trained model fixed and factorizes only its readout rows, so
the dictionary remains tied to the lens-score interface.

\paragraph{Additive attribution and mechanisms.}
Direct logit attribution splits a token logit or logit-difference direction
into contributions from residual-stream components
\citep{elhage2021framework,wang2022ioi}, and logit-prism methods extend this
across transformer components \citep{nguyen2024logitprisms}. Composing the
row-side basis with DLA yields feature-resolved attributions on a realized
forward pass, refining each component's scalar contribution into signed
per-feature terms that lens-style methods leave as a single dense score.
The combined view remains additive; interventions \citep{conmy2023acdc}, causal
abstraction \citep{geiger2023causalabstraction}, and transcoder or
attribution-graph approaches
\citep{dunefsky2024transcoders,ameisen2025circuittracing} address the
complementary circuit- and mechanism-level questions. Broader positioning
appears in Appendix~\ref{app:extended-related-work}.
