\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Lens-style readout interfaces.}
SRP keeps the lens-style readout interface but factorizes the model's own LM
head behind it: a selected token logit or contrast remains a dot product with
an unembedding row or row combination, and SRP decomposes that readout
direction into sparse feature terms with residual checks. The original logit
lens maps hidden states through the model's unembedding
\citep{nostalgebraist2020}; the tuned lens learns layerwise translators to
calibrate those predictions \citep{belrose2023tuned}. Future Lens asks whether
a single hidden state predicts later tokens \citep{pal2023future}, and
Patchscopes elicits continuations or descriptions by patching representations
into prompted contexts \citep{ghandeharioun2024patchscopes}. These methods
vary the hidden-state inspection interface; SRP instead leaves the selected
readout direction in place and factorizes that direction into sparse readout
features.

\paragraph{Output geometry and sparse row dictionaries.}
SRP applies sparse-dictionary machinery to a different object from prior
activation-SAE work: unembedding rows. Its sparse codes are token-row
coefficients, its decoder directions are readout feature directions, and
\(h^\top d_i\) is a hidden-state projection onto that readout basis, not an
activation-SAE code. Output embeddings have been studied as learned
representations and prediction-shaping geometry: output/input weight tying
changes the classifier geometry \citep{press2017outputembedding,inan2017tying},
softmax rank constraints limit distribution families
\citep{yang2018softmaxbottleneck}, and vocabulary segmentation changes the
rows being scored \citep{bostrom2020bpe}. Activation-SAE work has established
sparse-feature dictionaries over hidden-state distributions
\citep{bricken2023monosemanticity,cunningham2023sparse}. Scaling work studies
capacity and fidelity tradeoffs \citep{gao2024scaling}, while benchmark and
principled-evaluation work stresses standardized diagnostics
\citep{karvonen2025saebench,makelov2024principled}. SRP uses the same
sparse-dictionary vocabulary but fits the dictionary to LM-head rows.

\paragraph{Additive attribution and mechanisms.}
SRP asks the complementary readout-side question to component attribution:
which sparse readout directions raise or lower a selected score. The
selected-direction terms are linear in the hidden state and so compose with
residual-stream attribution to yield feature-resolved DLA for a realized
forward pass. Direct logit attribution decomposes residual-stream components
against token logits or logit-difference directions
\citep{elhage2021framework,wang2022ioi}, and logit-prism methods extend this
additive accounting across transformer components \citep{nguyen2024logitprisms}.
SRP further decomposes the selected readout direction into sparse readout
features. This remains additive accounting for readout scores; intervention
methods address the stronger question of circuit discovery
\citep{conmy2023acdc}, causal abstraction formalizes mechanism-level
validation \citep{geiger2023causalabstraction}, and transcoders or
attribution-graph tracing target feature-level computational graphs
\citep{dunefsky2024transcoders,ameisen2025circuittracing}.
Broader positioning appears in Appendix~\ref{app:extended-related-work}.
