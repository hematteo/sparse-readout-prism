\section{Method: Sparse Readout Prism}
\label{sec:method}

We take \(h\in\R^d\) to be the residual-stream vector after the model's final
normalization (LayerNorm or RMSNorm), the vector the unembedding multiplies.
The logit for token \(t\) is then exactly \(\ell_t(h)=h^\top w_t\),
with normalization folded into \(h\), so the readout under analysis is \(\WU\)
alone. Stacking rows, the dense LM head produces \(\ell(h)=\WU h\). A hidden state
captured at this point reproduces the model readout with no post-readout transform
(Gemma's logit softcap is the one exception, handled in
Appendix~\ref{app:readout-factorizer-diagnostics-reproducibility}), so every
additive account below sums to the model's own score. SRP supplies four operations (\Cref{fig:method-schematic}): factorize the LM head into
a sparse readout basis (\S\ref{sec:method-factorization}); project hidden
states onto its directions (\S\ref{sec:method-feature-projections});
decompose any selected readout score into per-feature contributions plus
residual (\S\ref{sec:method-local-score}), a list we call the
score's \emph{decomposition}; and compare decompositions across contexts,
tokens, or fitted lenses under a fixed reading rule
(\S\ref{sec:method-comparing-accounts}). The projection step does not
depend on which score is selected, but the decomposition step does.

\subsection{Factorizing the LM Head into Sparse Readout Features}
\label{sec:method-factorization}

Following
sparse-autoencoder practice~\citep{bricken2023monosemanticity,cunningham2023sparse,gao2024scaling},
SRP fits a sparse dictionary to the rows of \(\WU\). Each row then
decomposes as
\(w_t = \widehat{w}_t + r_t\) with
\(\widehat{w}_t = \mu + \textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{D} z_{t,i} d_i\),
where \(\mu\) is a shared offset, \(d_i\in\R^d\) is a readout feature direction
(an SAE decoder direction), \(z_{t,i}\) is token row \(t\)'s sparse coefficient,
\(\widehat{w}_t\) is the fitted row, and \(r_t\) is the residual.
We call this dictionary the \emph{readout SAE}. Its codes describe how each
vocabulary row decomposes into shared directions. An activation SAE's codes
do the same for hidden states.

We fit TopK sparse autoencoders~\citep{makhzani2013ksparse,gao2024scaling},
whose encoder retains only the \(k\) largest pre-activations per row. Row
sparsity is therefore fixed at \(k\), and rows remain comparable across
tokens. Any centering or
row-normalization applied during training is inverted after fitting, so
\(\widehat{w}_t\), \(d_i\), \(z_{t,i}\), and \(r_t\) all lie in the raw row
space of \(w_t\), in original logit units, and the pipeline applies no
hidden dense correction.
Appendices~\ref{app:readout-factorizer-sweeps}--%
\ref{app:readout-factorizer-diagnostics-reproducibility} and
\ref{app:score-decomposition} give dictionary width, sparsity,
preprocessing, and score-decomposition details.

\subsection{Readout Reconstruction and Feature Projections}
\label{sec:method-feature-projections}

Once the readout feature directions \(d_i\) are fixed, any hidden state can be
projected onto them before a token logit, contrast, or margin is selected. Let
\(Z\in\R^{|\mathcal{V}|\times D}\) collect the sparse row codes and let
\(D_{\mathrm{feat}}\in\R^{D\times d}\) have rows \(d_i^\top\), with
\(\mathbf{1}\) denoting the all-ones column vector. Stacking fitted rows yields
the reconstructed LM head
\(\widehat{\WU} = \mathbf{1}\mu^\top + ZD_{\mathrm{feat}}\)
and reconstructed logit vector
\(\widehat{\ell}(h) = \mathbf{1}(\mu^\top h) + Z(D_{\mathrm{feat}}h)\).
For a hidden state \(h\), the readout feature projection
\(p_i(h)=h^\top d_i\) measures how strongly \(h\) aligns with readout
feature \(i\). The projection vector \(p(h)=D_{\mathrm{feat}}h\)
collects these coordinates and is the inner term of the reconstructed
logit \(\widehat{\ell}(h)\) above. The
projections are reusable across prompts, layers, and residual-stream
components, while the row codes \(Z\) weight them into per-token logits
only once a score is selected.

\subsection{Decomposing Selected Readout Scores}
\label{sec:method-local-score}

A selected readout score is a fixed linear combination of LM-head rows.
Selecting one fixes where the hidden state is taken (source and
position), what defines the score (token ids, coefficient vector, score
family), and the tokenization filters and competitor sets that frame the
contrast (\Cref{sec:experimental-setup}). Let
\(\alpha\in\R^{|\mathcal{V}|}\) be vocabulary-row coefficients and define
the selected readout direction and score
\(q_\alpha = \textstyle\sum_{t\in\mathcal{V}} \alpha_t w_t\) and
\(s_\alpha(h)=h^\top q_\alpha\). A selected direction is assembled from
LM-head rows and fixes which score is read, whereas the feature
directions \(d_i\) are the basis that score is then expanded over.
Setting \(\alpha_A=1\) and all other
coefficients to zero recovers the familiar logit-lens coordinate
\(\ell_A(h)=h^\top w_A\) for vocabulary token \(A\in\mathcal{V}\).

Contrasts cancel score components shared across the compared rows, including
output-embedding and tokenizer-row structure
\citep{press2017outputembedding,inan2017tying,yang2018softmaxbottleneck,bostrom2020bpe}.
For two tokens \(A,B\in\mathcal{V}\), the pairwise logit difference
\(m_{A,B}(h)=h^\top(w_A-w_B)\) is positive when the readout favors token
\(A\) over token \(B\). For token groups, write
\(\bar{w}_{\mathcal{A}}=\tfrac{1}{|\mathcal{A}|}\textstyle\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}w_a\).
For top-competitor margins, the target row, competitor set, and nonnegative
weights (summing to one) are taken from the original LM-head ranking computed
before SRP is applied, so the reconstructed readout cannot choose the
comparison set it is later evaluated against.

Fix a hidden state \(h\) and any selected readout direction
\(q_\alpha=\sum_{t\in\mathcal{V}}\alpha_t w_t\). Substituting the fitted
reconstruction row-by-row and exchanging the sums gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
    s_\alpha(h)
    =
    \big(\textstyle\sum_t \alpha_t\big) h^\top\mu
    &+ \textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{D}
        \big(\textstyle\sum_t \alpha_t z_{t,i}\big) h^\top d_i \\
    &+ h^\top \textstyle\sum_t \alpha_t r_t
\end{aligned}
\]
(full expansion in Appendix~\ref{app:score-decomposition}).
The shared-offset term \(\left(\sum_t\alpha_t\right) h^\top\mu\) vanishes
whenever the coefficients sum to zero, which covers logit differences, group
and vocabulary-mean contrasts, and top-competitor margins. For these scores,
the decomposition reduces to feature contributions plus residual.
For readout feature \(i\), define its direction coefficient
\(\beta_i(\alpha)=\textstyle\sum_{t\in\mathcal{V}} \alpha_t z_{t,i}\)
and signed feature contribution
\(c_i(h,\alpha)=\beta_i(\alpha)\,p_i(h)\).
Here \(\beta_i(\alpha)\) is fixed by the selected direction and the readout
SAE, while \(p_i(h)\) varies with context. When \(q=q_\alpha\) is clear, we
write \(\beta_i(q)\). A feature contributes locally only when both factors are
large in magnitude (Appendix~\ref{app:global-readout-feature-profiles}).

We label the components
\(s_{\mu}=(\textstyle\sum_t\alpha_t)h^\top\mu\),
\(s_{\mathrm{feat}}=\textstyle\sum_i c_i(h,\alpha)\),
\(s_{\mathrm{recon}}=s_{\mu}+s_{\mathrm{feat}}\), and
\(s_{\mathrm{exact}}=s_\alpha(h)\), with residual
\(\epsilon=s_{\mathrm{exact}}-s_{\mathrm{recon}}
=h^\top\sum_{t\in\mathcal{V}}\alpha_t r_t\).
Table~\ref{tab:method-special-cases}
(Appendix~\ref{app:score-decomposition}) instantiates \(q_\alpha\) and
\(\beta_i(\alpha)\) for each score family we use.

The residual is not orthogonal to the feature directions, and with an
overcomplete dictionary it cannot be: a remainder orthogonal to every
\(d_i\) would be zero. The separation is additive and exact rather than
geometric, so we report \(\epsilon\) beside
the contributions instead of discarding it. Little of it is recoverable on the selected
support, since refitting coefficients by least squares there raises
row-centered explained variance (rowEV) by
\(0.004\)--\(0.006\) (Appendix~\ref{app:readout-factorizer-diagnostics}).

\subsection{Comparing and Reading Decompositions}
\label{sec:method-comparing-accounts}

Because the basis is fixed by the weights, decompositions taken at
different contexts, layers, tokens, or selected scores land in one
coordinate system and compare term by term. Operationally, two readings
agree at the feature level when the same feature id dominates both
signed contribution profiles (or when their profiles correlate, where
stated). The same operation extends to fitted vocabulary lenses. Write a
lens as a transport \(T_\ell\) applied to a hidden state before the
readout, so the score it reports for a selected direction \(q\) is
\((T_\ell h)^\top q\), a selected readout score on the transported
state, which SRP decomposes in the shared basis. A lens is \emph{fitted}
when \(T_\ell\) carries parameters estimated from data: the tuned lens
learns a per-layer affine translator \citep{belrose2023tuned}, the
Jacobian lens averages a Jacobian over a corpus
\citep{gurnee2026workspace}. The logit lens is the unfitted case,
\(T_\ell=I\) \citep{nostalgebraist2020}. Fitting enters the transport,
never the basis: \(D_{\mathrm{feat}}\) and \(Z\) come from \(\WU\) alone.
We claim this comparison only for the lens construction
tested in \Cref{sec:beyond-token}, whose implementation feeds the
unembedding directly, and it may not extend to arbitrary lenses.

\paragraph{Reading rule.}
\label{sec:method-reading-local-accounts}
Because the residual closes the account exactly, the decomposition always
balances arithmetically. Whether it can be interpreted is a separate
question, settled by four checks. The \emph{replacement check} is global,
asking whether substituting \(\widehat{\WU}\) for \(\WU\) preserves
model-facing behavior (\S\ref{subsec:readout-replacement-fidelity}),
since a dictionary can reconstruct rows accurately in aggregate and
still miss the logits under interpretation. The \emph{score} and \emph{residual}
checks are per-score and are jointly enforced by a relative
reconstruction error
\(\rho(h,\alpha)=|s_{\mathrm{exact}}-s_{\mathrm{recon}}|\,/\,
\big(|s_{\mathrm{exact}}|+0.5\big)<0.5\),
whose additive constant makes the criterion absolute rather than relative
at near-tie scores. The \emph{sign check} additionally requires
\(s_{\mathrm{exact}}\) and \(s_{\mathrm{recon}}\) to agree in sign before
support or opposition is read, since \(\rho\) bounds the magnitude of the
reconstruction error but not its direction.
Case-study displays and baseline comparisons instead report the
unfloored variant
\(\tilde\rho(h,\alpha)=|\epsilon|\,/\,|s_{\mathrm{exact}}|\), which drops
the additive constant and so upper-bounds \(\rho\). The two are never
mixed within one table.
Appendix~\ref{app:score-decomposition} records which results use which
and states the display conventions.
