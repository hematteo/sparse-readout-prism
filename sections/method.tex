\section{Method: Sparse Readout Prism}
\label{sec:method}

Throughout, ``readout'' means the final unembedding or LM head
\(\WU\in\R^{|\mathcal{V}|\times d}\). For hidden state \(h\in\R^d\), row \(t\)
is \(w_t^\top\), and the full logit vector is \(\ell(h)=\WU h\); its
SRP-reconstructed counterpart is \(\widehat{\ell}(h)\), introduced below.

SRP has three steps. First, it factorizes the final
unembedding into a sparse readout basis: token-row coefficients over shared
readout feature directions, plus residuals. Second, for a hidden state, it
computes coordinates \(p_i(h)=h^\top d_i\) along those directions. These
coordinates do not depend on which token logit, contrast, or other readout
score will later be inspected; the same basis can also reconstruct or replace
the full LM head. Third, after the analyst or evaluation selects a token logit,
contrast, or margin, SRP multiplies that score's row-side coefficients by the
hidden-state projections to obtain signed feature contributions plus an explicit
residual.
We define the supported score families below.

\subsection{Factorizing the LM Head into Sparse Readout Features}
\label{sec:method-factorization}
\label{sec:method-fitting-readout-sae}

SRP learns a sparse dictionary for rows of \(\WU\). We call the SAE trained on unembedding rows the \emph{readout SAE}. The more
common variant, an activation SAE, is trained on per-layer hidden-state
activations collected from forward passes, so its codes describe hidden
states. A readout SAE instead takes the static rows of the LM head as
its training data, so its codes describe how each vocabulary row decomposes
into shared directions. Following sparse-autoencoder
practice
\citep{bricken2023monosemanticity,cunningham2023sparse,gao2024scaling}, the
readout SAE approximates each unembedding row. In the raw readout coordinates used for all
score decompositions, the fitted row and residual satisfy
\[
    \widehat{w}_t = \mu + \sum_{i=1}^{D} z_{t,i} d_i,
    \qquad
    r_t = w_t-\widehat{w}_t,
\]
where \(\mu\) is a shared offset, \(d_i\in\R^d\) is a readout feature direction
(an SAE decoder direction), \(z_{t,i}\) is token row \(t\)'s sparse coefficient,
and \(r_t\) is the residual. Thus
\[
    w_t=\mu+\sum_i z_{t,i}d_i+r_t .
\]

We fit TopK sparse autoencoders~\citep{makhzani2013ksparse,gao2024scaling}: the
encoder retains only the \(k\) largest pre-activations per row and zeros the
rest, fixing the active-feature budget so row sparsity is comparable across
tokens. When training uses centering or row normalization, we invert those
transformations after fitting so that \(\widehat{w}_t\), \(d_i\), \(z_{t,i}\),
and \(r_t\) live in the same raw row space as \(w_t\). Every displayed term then
remains in original logit units, with no hidden dense correction. Qualitative
case studies use Qwen3.5-2B with \(D=32d_{\mathrm{model}}\) and \(k=256\);
full width, sparsity, and preprocessing sweeps are in
Appendices~\ref{app:readout-factorizer-sweeps}--%
\ref{app:readout-factorizer-diagnostics-reproducibility} and
\ref{app:score-decomposition}.

\subsection{Reconstructing the Readout and Projecting Hidden States}
\label{sec:method-feature-projections}

Once the readout feature directions \(d_i\) are fixed, any hidden state can be
projected onto them before a token logit, contrast, or margin is selected. Let
\(Z\in\R^{|\mathcal{V}|\times D}\) collect the sparse row codes and let
\(D_{\mathrm{feat}}\in\R^{D\times d}\) have rows \(d_i^\top\), with
\(\mathbf{1}\) denoting the all-ones column vector. Stacking fitted rows yields
the reconstructed LM head and reconstructed logit vector,
\[
    \begin{aligned}
    \widehat{\WU}
    &=
    \mathbf{1}\mu^\top
    +
    ZD_{\mathrm{feat}},\\
    \widehat{\ell}(h)
    &=
    \mathbf{1}(\mu^\top h)
    +
    Z(D_{\mathrm{feat}}h).
    \end{aligned}
\]
For a hidden state \(h\), define the readout feature projection coordinate and
projection vector
\[
    p_i(h)=h^\top d_i,
    \qquad
    p(h)=D_{\mathrm{feat}}h .
\]
The vector \(p(h)\) is the readout feature projection vector: it records how
strongly \(h\) aligns with each readout feature direction before choosing a
specific score.

\emph{A projection \(p_i(h)\) is a coordinate, not a contribution.} It
acquires a sign---supporting or opposing the selected side---only once a
selected readout score supplies the coefficient \(\beta_i\) that multiplies
it. The full-readout replacement test \(\widehat{\ell}(h)\) is therefore
score-agnostic, while the local score decomposition derived below is
score-conditioned.

\subsection{Decomposing Selected Readout Scores}
\label{sec:method-selected-readout-scores}
\label{sec:problem-setup}
\label{sec:method-local-score}

For local decompositions, SRP applies to selected readout scores that are fixed
linear combinations of LM-head rows, giving an exact sparse-plus-residual
identity for the score families considered here. Let
\(\alpha\in\R^{|\mathcal{V}|}\) be
vocabulary-row coefficients and define
\[
    q_\alpha = \sum_{t\in\mathcal{V}} \alpha_t w_t,
    \qquad
    s_\alpha(h)=h^\top q_\alpha .
\]
We call \(q_\alpha\) the selected readout direction and \(s_\alpha(h)\) the
selected readout score. For vocabulary token \(A\in\mathcal{V}\), a token logit
recovers the familiar logit-lens
coordinate
\[
    \ell_A(h)=h^\top w_A,
\]
where \(\alpha_A=1\) and all other coefficients are zero.

Contrasts often reduce sensitivity to shared output-embedding and tokenizer row
structure
\citep{press2017outputembedding,inan2017tying,yang2018softmaxbottleneck,bostrom2020bpe};
for two tokens \(A,B\in\mathcal{V}\), a pairwise logit difference
\[
    m_{A,B}(h)=h^\top(w_A-w_B),
\]
is positive when the readout favors token \(A\) over token \(B\). For token
groups, write
\[
    \bar{w}_{\mathcal{A}}
    =
    \frac{1}{|\mathcal{A}|}\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}w_a .
\]
Group contrasts, vocabulary-mean contrasts, and local-ranking alternatives use
the same fixed-row-combination form. For top-competitor margins we use
\[
    w_u-\sum_{r\in\mathcal{R}}\pi_r w_r
\]
where \(u\), competitor set \(\mathcal{R}\), and weights \(\pi_r\ge0\) are
fixed by the original LM-head ranking prior to SRP decomposition, with
\(\sum_{r\in\mathcal{R}}\pi_r=1\). \Cref{tab:method-special-cases} gives the
corresponding sparse-feature coefficients.

Fix a hidden state \(h\) and any selected readout direction
\(q_\alpha=\sum_{t\in\mathcal{V}}\alpha_t w_t\). Substituting the SAE
reconstruction gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
    s_\alpha(h)
    &= h^\top q_\alpha \\
    &=
    \left(\sum_{t\in\mathcal{V}} \alpha_t\right) h^\top\mu \\
    &\quad + \sum_{i=1}^{D}
        \left(\sum_{t\in\mathcal{V}} \alpha_t z_{t,i}\right) h^\top d_i \\
    &\quad + h^\top \sum_{t\in\mathcal{V}} \alpha_t r_t .
\end{aligned}
\]
The shared-offset term \(\left(\sum_t\alpha_t\right) h^\top\mu\) cancels out
whenever the coefficients sum to zero, which covers logit differences,
group and vocabulary-mean contrasts, and top-competitor margins
(\Cref{sec:method-score-coefficients}); for these contrasts the entire decomposition lives in feature contributions plus residual.
For readout feature \(i\), define its direction coefficient and signed feature
contribution:
\[
    \beta_i(\alpha)=\sum_{t\in\mathcal{V}} \alpha_t z_{t,i},
    \qquad
    c_i(h,\alpha)=\beta_i(\alpha)\,p_i(h).
\]
Here \(\beta_i(\alpha)\) is fixed by the selected direction and readout SAE,
while \(p_i(h)\) varies with context; when \(q=q_\alpha\) is clear, we write
\(\beta_i(q)\). A feature matters locally only when both factors are large in
magnitude---reiterating the point of
\Cref{sec:method-feature-projections}, the projection coordinate alone is not
support or opposition until a selected direction supplies its coefficient
(Appendix~\ref{app:global-readout-feature-profiles}).

For a selected readout score, let
\[
\begin{aligned}
    s_{\mu}&=\left(\sum_t\alpha_t\right)h^\top\mu,\\
    s_{\mathrm{feat}}&=\sum_i c_i(h,\alpha),\\
    s_{\mathrm{recon}}&=s_{\mu}+s_{\mathrm{feat}} .
\end{aligned}
\]
The sparse account leaves residual
\[
\begin{aligned}
    s_{\mathrm{exact}}&=s_\alpha(h),\\
    \epsilon
    &=
    s_{\mathrm{exact}}-s_{\mathrm{recon}}\\
    &=
    h^\top\sum_{t\in\mathcal{V}}\alpha_t r_t .
\end{aligned}
\]
Positive terms raise the selected readout score; negative terms lower it. For
signed contrasts, positive values support the first side and negative values
support the comparison side.

\subsection{Residuals, Sign Checks, and Interpretation Rules}
\label{sec:method-reading-local-accounts}

Every local score decomposition is paired with a reconstruction check. A
displayed account reports exact and reconstructed scores, feature sum, offset when
present, residual, relative reconstruction error, and sign status for signed
contrasts. We compute relative reconstruction error as
\[
    \rho(h,\alpha)=
    \frac{|\epsilon|}{|s_{\mathrm{exact}}|+\delta},
    \qquad
    \delta=0.5\ \text{logit}.
\]
The half-logit floor \(\delta\) keeps \(\rho\) bounded when
\(|s_{\mathrm{exact}}|\) is near zero, so the metric is comparable across
score magnitudes. Feature bars are read only when \(s_{\mathrm{recon}}\) is
close to \(s_{\mathrm{exact}}\). We treat \(\rho<0.5\) as the operating
threshold: feature bars are interpreted only when the reconstructed and exact
scores agree within this relative-error bound, and for signed contrasts the
two scores must also share sign before support and opposition are read off.

Figures may truncate small bars, but reconstructions use the full feature sum.
Claims remain tied to feature ids, signed contributions, and residuals; labels
summarize high-coefficient vocabulary rows, following sparse-feature evaluation
practice
\citep{karvonen2025saebench,makelov2024principled}.
