\section{Method: Sparse Readout Prism}
\label{sec:method}

For hidden state \(h\in\R^d\), the dense LM head produces logit vector
\(\ell(h)=\WU h\). SRP has three stages: factorize the LM head into a sparse
readout basis (\S\ref{sec:method-factorization}), project hidden states onto
the resulting directions (\S\ref{sec:method-feature-projections}), and define
selected readout scores whose decomposition splits into per-feature
contributions plus residual (\S\ref{sec:method-local-score}). The projection
step is score-agnostic; the local decomposition is score-conditioned.

\subsection{Factorizing the LM Head into Sparse Readout Features}
\label{sec:method-factorization}

SRP fits a sparse dictionary to the rows of \(\WU\). Following
sparse-autoencoder practice~\citep{bricken2023monosemanticity,cunningham2023sparse,gao2024scaling},
each row decomposes as
\[
    w_t = \widehat{w}_t + r_t,
    \qquad
    \widehat{w}_t = \mu + \sum_{i=1}^{D} z_{t,i} d_i,
\]
where \(\mu\) is a shared offset, \(d_i\in\R^d\) is a readout feature direction
(an SAE decoder direction), \(z_{t,i}\) is token row \(t\)'s sparse coefficient,
\(\widehat{w}_t\) is the fitted row, and \(r_t\) is the residual.
We call this dictionary a \emph{readout SAE}: its codes describe how each
vocabulary row decomposes into shared directions, not how each hidden state
does, as in activation SAEs.

We fit TopK sparse autoencoders~\citep{makhzani2013ksparse,gao2024scaling}: the
encoder retains only the \(k\) largest pre-activations per row, fixing row
sparsity at \(k\) for comparability across tokens. Any centering or
row-normalization applied during training is inverted after fitting, so
\(\widehat{w}_t\), \(d_i\), \(z_{t,i}\), and \(r_t\) all lie in the raw row
space of \(w_t\)---in original logit units, with no hidden dense correction. Dictionary width,
sparsity, preprocessing, and score-decomposition details are in
Appendices~\ref{app:readout-factorizer-sweeps}--%
\ref{app:readout-factorizer-diagnostics-reproducibility} and
\ref{app:score-decomposition}.

\subsection{Readout Reconstruction and Feature Projections}
\label{sec:method-feature-projections}

Once the readout feature directions \(d_i\) are fixed, any hidden state can be
projected onto them before a token logit, contrast, or margin is selected. Let
\(Z\in\R^{|\mathcal{V}|\times D}\) collect the sparse row codes and let
\(D_{\mathrm{feat}}\in\R^{D\times d}\) have rows \(d_i^\top\), with
\(\mathbf{1}\) denoting the all-ones column vector. Stacking fitted rows yields
the reconstructed LM head and reconstructed logit vector,
\[
    \begin{aligned}
    \widehat{\WU}
    &=
    \mathbf{1}\mu^\top
    +
    ZD_{\mathrm{feat}},\\
    \widehat{\ell}(h)
    &=
    \mathbf{1}(\mu^\top h)
    +
    Z(D_{\mathrm{feat}}h).
    \end{aligned}
\]
For a hidden state \(h\), define the readout feature projection coordinate and
projection vector
\[
    p_i(h)=h^\top d_i,
    \qquad
    p(h)=D_{\mathrm{feat}}h .
\]
The coordinate \(p_i(h)\) records the alignment of \(h\) with readout feature
\(i\).

\subsection{Decomposing Selected Readout Scores}
\label{sec:method-local-score}

A selected readout score is a fixed linear combination of LM-head rows. Let
\(\alpha\in\R^{|\mathcal{V}|}\) be vocabulary-row coefficients and define the
selected readout direction and score
\[
    q_\alpha = \sum_{t\in\mathcal{V}} \alpha_t w_t,
    \qquad
    s_\alpha(h)=h^\top q_\alpha .
\]
Setting \(\alpha_A=1\) and all other coefficients to zero recovers the
familiar logit-lens coordinate
\[
    \ell_A(h)=h^\top w_A
\]
for vocabulary token \(A\in\mathcal{V}\).

Contrasts cancel score components shared across the compared rows, including
output-embedding and tokenizer-row structure
\citep{press2017outputembedding,inan2017tying,yang2018softmaxbottleneck,bostrom2020bpe};
for two tokens \(A,B\in\mathcal{V}\), the pairwise logit difference
\[
    m_{A,B}(h)=h^\top(w_A-w_B),
\]
is positive when the readout favors token \(A\) over token \(B\). For token
groups, write
\[
    \bar{w}_{\mathcal{A}}
    =
    \frac{1}{|\mathcal{A}|}\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}w_a .
\]
For top-competitor margins, the target row, competitor set, and nonnegative
weights (summing to one) are taken from the original LM-head ranking computed
before SRP is applied.

Fix a hidden state \(h\) and any selected readout direction
\(q_\alpha=\sum_{t\in\mathcal{V}}\alpha_t w_t\). Substituting the SAE
reconstruction row-by-row and exchanging the sums gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
    s_\alpha(h)
    &= h^\top q_\alpha
     = \sum_{t\in\mathcal{V}} \alpha_t \, h^\top w_t \\
    &= \sum_{t\in\mathcal{V}} \alpha_t \, h^\top
       \!\left(\mu + \sum_{i=1}^{D} z_{t,i} d_i + r_t\right) \\
    &=
    \left(\sum_{t\in\mathcal{V}} \alpha_t\right) h^\top\mu
    + \sum_{i=1}^{D}
        \left(\sum_{t\in\mathcal{V}} \alpha_t z_{t,i}\right) h^\top d_i \\
    &\quad + h^\top \sum_{t\in\mathcal{V}} \alpha_t r_t .
\end{aligned}
\]
The shared-offset term \(\left(\sum_t\alpha_t\right) h^\top\mu\) vanishes
whenever the coefficients sum to zero, which covers logit differences, group
and vocabulary-mean contrasts, and top-competitor margins; for these scores,
the decomposition reduces to feature contributions plus residual.
For readout feature \(i\), define its direction coefficient and signed feature
contribution:
\[
    \beta_i(\alpha)=\sum_{t\in\mathcal{V}} \alpha_t z_{t,i},
    \qquad
    c_i(h,\alpha)=\beta_i(\alpha)\,p_i(h).
\]
Here \(\beta_i(\alpha)\) is fixed by the selected direction and the readout
SAE, while \(p_i(h)\) varies with context; when \(q=q_\alpha\) is clear, we
write \(\beta_i(q)\). A feature contributes locally only when both factors are
large in magnitude (Appendix~\ref{app:global-readout-feature-profiles}).

We label the components:
\[
\begin{aligned}
    s_{\mu}            &= \left(\sum_t\alpha_t\right)h^\top\mu, \\
    s_{\mathrm{feat}}  &= \sum_i c_i(h,\alpha), \\
    s_{\mathrm{recon}} &= s_{\mu}+s_{\mathrm{feat}}, \\
    s_{\mathrm{exact}} &= s_\alpha(h), \\
    \epsilon           &= s_{\mathrm{exact}}-s_{\mathrm{recon}}
                        = h^\top\sum_{t\in\mathcal{V}}\alpha_t r_t .
\end{aligned}
\]
\Cref{tab:method-special-cases} instantiates \(q_\alpha\) and
\(\beta_i(\alpha)\) for the score families used in our analyses.

\paragraph{Reading rule.}
\label{sec:method-reading-local-accounts}
We interpret local feature terms only after a reconstruction check. For each
selected score, we report exact and reconstructed scores, feature sum, offset
when present, residual, and relative reconstruction error
\[
    \rho(h,\alpha)=
    \frac{|s_{\mathrm{exact}}-s_{\mathrm{recon}}|}
         {|s_{\mathrm{exact}}|+0.5}.
\]
Feature bars are interpreted only when \(\rho<0.5\); for signed contrasts,
\(s_{\mathrm{exact}}\) and \(s_{\mathrm{recon}}\) must also share sign before
we read support or opposition. Displayed bars may be truncated, but feature
sums and residuals always use the full decomposition. Claims remain tied to
feature ids, signed contributions, and residuals rather than labels alone
\citep{karvonen2025saebench,makelov2024principled}.
