\section{Method: Sparse Readout Prism}
\label{sec:method}

Throughout, ``readout'' means the final unembedding or LM head
\(\WU\in\R^{|\mathcal{V}|\times d}\). For hidden state \(h\in\R^d\), row \(t\)
is \(w_t^\top\), and the full logit vector is \(\WU h\).

SRP has three steps. First, it factorizes the final
unembedding into a sparse readout basis: token-row coefficients over shared
readout feature directions, plus residuals. Second, it projects a hidden state
onto those directions, giving a score-independent profile in readout-feature
coordinates; the same basis can also reconstruct or replace the full LM head.
Third, once a token logit, logit difference, group contrast, vocabulary-mean
contrast, or top-competitor margin is selected, SRP multiplies the row-side
coefficients by the hidden-state projections to obtain signed feature
contributions plus an explicit residual for that score.

\subsection{Factorizing the LM Head into Sparse Readout Features}
\label{sec:method-factorization}
\label{sec:method-fitting-readout-sae}

SRP learns a sparse dictionary for rows of \(\WU\). We call the sparse
autoencoder (SAE) trained on unembedding rows the \emph{readout SAE}; unlike an
activation SAE, its sparse codes describe token rows rather than hidden states. Following sparse-autoencoder
practice
\citep{bricken2023monosemanticity,cunningham2023sparse,gao2024scaling}, it
approximates each unembedding row. In the raw readout coordinates used for all
score decompositions, the fitted row and residual satisfy
\[
    \widehat{w}_t = \mu + \sum_{i=1}^{D} z_{t,i} d_i,
    \qquad
    r_t = w_t-\widehat{w}_t,
\]
where \(\mu\) is a shared offset, \(d_i\in\R^d\) is a readout feature direction
(an SAE decoder direction), \(z_{t,i}\) is token row \(t\)'s sparse coefficient,
and \(r_t\) is the residual. Thus
\[
    w_t=\mu+\sum_i z_{t,i}d_i+r_t .
\]

We fit TopK sparse autoencoders~\citep{makhzani2013ksparse,gao2024scaling};
TopK keeps a fixed active-feature budget \(k\), making row sparsity comparable
across tokens. When training uses centering or row normalization, fitted
codes, directions, and residuals are mapped back to raw readout coordinates
before any score decomposition. Every displayed term then remains in original logit
units, with no hidden dense correction. Width \(D\), sparsity \(k\), and
preprocessing details are given in
appendices~\ref{app:readout-factorizer-sweeps}--%
\ref{app:readout-factorizer-diagnostics-reproducibility} and
\ref{app:score-decomposition}.

\subsection{Reconstructing the Readout and Projecting Hidden States}
\label{sec:method-feature-projections}

Once the readout feature directions \(d_i\) are fixed, a hidden state supplies
score-independent projections onto them. Let \(Z\in
\R^{|\mathcal{V}|\times D}\) collect the sparse row codes and let
\(D_{\mathrm{feat}}\in\R^{D\times d}\) have rows \(d_i^\top\), with
\(\mathbf{1}\) denoting the all-ones column vector. Stacking fitted rows yields
the reconstructed LM head and reconstructed logit vector,
\[
    \begin{aligned}
    \widehat{\WU}
    &=
    \mathbf{1}\mu^\top
    +
    ZD_{\mathrm{feat}},\\
    \widehat{\ell}(h)
    &=
    \mathbf{1}(\mu^\top h)
    +
    Z(D_{\mathrm{feat}}h).
    \end{aligned}
\]
For a hidden state \(h\), define the readout feature projection coordinate and
projection vector
\[
    p_i(h)=h^\top d_i,
    \qquad
    p(h)=D_{\mathrm{feat}}h .
\]
The vector \(p(h)\) is a score-independent projection profile.
\emph{A readout-feature projection \(p_i(h)\) is not support or opposition for
any token or contrast until a selected readout direction supplies its
coefficient}; it becomes a signed local term only after multiplication by that
coefficient. Full readout replacement tests \(\widehat{\ell}(h)\), while the
local score decomposition below is score-conditioned.

\subsection{Decomposing Selected Readout Scores}
\label{sec:method-selected-readout-scores}
\label{sec:problem-setup}
\label{sec:method-local-score}

For local decompositions, SRP applies to selected scores that are fixed linear
combinations of LM-head rows, giving an exact sparse-plus-residual identity for
the score families considered here. Let \(\alpha\in\R^{|\mathcal{V}|}\) be
vocabulary-row coefficients and define
\[
    q_\alpha = \sum_{t\in\mathcal{V}} \alpha_t w_t,
    \qquad
    s_\alpha(h)=h^\top q_\alpha .
\]
We call \(q_\alpha\) the selected readout direction and \(s_\alpha(h)\) the
selected readout score. A token logit recovers the familiar logit-lens
coordinate
\[
    \ell_A(h)=h^\top w_A,
\]
where \(\alpha_A=1\) and all other coefficients are zero.

Contrasts often reduce sensitivity to shared output-embedding and tokenizer row
structure
\citep{press2017outputembedding,inan2017tying,yang2018softmaxbottleneck,bostrom2020bpe};
for example, a pairwise logit difference
\[
    m_{A,B}(h)=h^\top(w_A-w_B),
\]
is positive when the readout favors token \(A\) over token \(B\). For token
groups, write
\[
    \bar{w}_{\mathcal{A}}
    =
    \frac{1}{|\mathcal{A}|}\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}w_a .
\]
Group contrasts, vocabulary-mean contrasts, and local-ranking alternatives use
the same fixed-row-combination form. For top-competitor margins we use
\[
    w_u-\sum_{r\in\mathcal{R}}\pi_r w_r
\]
where \(u\), competitor set \(\mathcal{R}\), and weights \(\pi_r\ge0\) are
fixed by the original LM-head ranking prior to SRP decomposition, with
\(\sum_{r\in\mathcal{R}}\pi_r=1\). \Cref{tab:method-special-cases} gives the
corresponding sparse-feature coefficients.

Fix a hidden state \(h\) and any selected readout direction
\(q_\alpha=\sum_{t\in\mathcal{V}}\alpha_t w_t\). Substituting the SAE
reconstruction gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
    s_\alpha(h)
    &= h^\top q_\alpha \\
    &=
    \left(\sum_{t\in\mathcal{V}} \alpha_t\right) h^\top\mu \\
    &\quad + \sum_{i=1}^{D}
        \left(\sum_{t\in\mathcal{V}} \alpha_t z_{t,i}\right) h^\top d_i \\
    &\quad + h^\top \sum_{t\in\mathcal{V}} \alpha_t r_t .
\end{aligned}
\]
The shared-offset term \(\left(\sum_t\alpha_t\right) h^\top\mu\) vanishes
whenever the coefficients sum to zero, which covers logit differences,
group and vocabulary-mean contrasts, and top-competitor margins
(\Cref{sec:method-score-coefficients}).
For readout feature \(i\), define its direction coefficient and signed feature
contribution:
\[
    \beta_i(\alpha)=\sum_{t\in\mathcal{V}} \alpha_t z_{t,i},
    \qquad
    c_i(h,\alpha)=\beta_i(\alpha)\,p_i(h).
\]
Here \(\beta_i(\alpha)\) is fixed by the selected direction and readout SAE,
while \(p_i(h)\) varies with context; when \(q=q_\alpha\) is clear, we write
\(\beta_i(q)\). A feature matters locally only when both factors are large in
magnitude---reiterating the point of
\Cref{sec:method-feature-projections}, the projection coordinate alone is not
support or opposition until a selected direction supplies its coefficient
(Appendix~\ref{app:global-readout-feature-profiles}).

For a selected score, let
\[
\begin{aligned}
    s_{\mu}&=\left(\sum_t\alpha_t\right)h^\top\mu,\\
    s_{\mathrm{feat}}&=\sum_i c_i(h,\alpha),\\
    s_{\mathrm{recon}}&=s_{\mu}+s_{\mathrm{feat}} .
\end{aligned}
\]
The sparse account leaves residual
\[
\begin{aligned}
    s_{\mathrm{exact}}&=s_\alpha(h),\\
    \epsilon
    &=
    s_{\mathrm{exact}}-s_{\mathrm{recon}}\\
    &=
    h^\top\sum_{t\in\mathcal{V}}\alpha_t r_t .
\end{aligned}
\]
Positive terms raise the selected score; negative terms lower it. For signed
contrasts, positive values support the first side and negative values support
the comparison side.

\subsection{Residuals, Sign Checks, and Interpretation Rules}
\label{sec:method-reading-local-accounts}

Every local explanation is paired with a reconstruction check. A displayed
account reports exact and reconstructed scores, feature sum, offset when
present, residual, relative reconstruction error, and sign status for signed
contrasts. We compute relative reconstruction error as
\[
    \rho(h,\alpha)=
    \frac{|\epsilon|}{|s_{\mathrm{exact}}|+\delta},
    \qquad
    \delta=0.5\ \text{logit}.
\]
The half-logit floor \(\delta\) keeps \(\rho\) bounded when
\(|s_{\mathrm{exact}}|\) is near zero, so the metric is comparable across
score magnitudes. Feature bars are read only when \(s_{\mathrm{recon}}\) is
close to \(s_{\mathrm{exact}}\); aggregate analyses use the threshold
\(\rho<0.5\), and for signed contrasts the reconstructed and exact scores
must also agree in sign before support and opposition are interpreted.

Figures may truncate small bars, but reconstructions use the full feature sum.
Claims remain tied to feature ids, signed contributions, and residuals; labels
summarize high-coefficient vocabulary rows, following sparse-feature evaluation
practice
\citep{karvonen2025saebench,makelov2024principled}.
