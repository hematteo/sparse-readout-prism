\section{Method: Sparse Readout Prism}
\label{sec:method}

The SRP factorization is learned before any target is chosen. It first
factorizes LM-head rows and projects hidden states onto the learned readout
directions. The selected-target layer then chooses a scalar readout target and
combines target coefficients with hidden-state projections to obtain signed
terms plus a residual. This keeps the logit-lens interface fixed: a hidden state
\(h\) is still read out through the LM head \(\WU\), but selected logits,
contrasts, group scores, and margins are decomposed through the same row
factorization of \(\WU\). The subsections below separate what SRP learns from the
LM head, what is computed from \(h\), what target selection supplies, and what
remains in the residual.

\subsection{Sparse Factorization of the Readout}
\label{sec:method-factorization}

Throughout, ``readout'' means the final unembedding or LM head
\(\WU\in\R^{|\mathcal{V}|\times d}\). For hidden state \(h\in\R^d\), row \(t\)
is \(w_t^\top\), and the full logit vector is \(\WU h\).

SRP first learns a sparse dictionary for rows of \(\WU\). One fitted row
factorization can later be reused for token logits, pairwise contrasts, group
contrasts, and top-competitor margins. We call the SAE trained on unembedding
rows the readout SAE; unlike an activation SAE, its sparse codes describe token
rows rather than hidden states. It follows sparse-autoencoder practice
\citep{bricken2023monosemanticity,cunningham2023sparse,gao2024scaling} and
approximates each unembedding row. In the raw readout coordinates used for all
score decompositions, the fitted row and residual satisfy
\[
    \widehat{w}_t = \mu + \sum_{i=1}^{D} z_{t,i} d_i,
    \qquad
    r_t = w_t-\widehat{w}_t,
\]
where \(\mu\) is a shared offset, \(d_i\in\R^d\) is a readout feature direction
(an SAE decoder direction), \(z_{t,i}\) is token row \(t\)'s sparse coefficient,
and \(r_t\) is the residual.
Thus
\[
    w_t=\mu+\sum_i z_{t,i}d_i+r_t .
\]
Including this residual keeps the later score accounting explicit; the sparse
feature sum alone is the reconstructed part of the score.
Stacking fitted rows yields \(\widehat{\WU}\); Appendix~\ref{app:score-decomposition}
details centering and row normalization.

We fit TopK sparse autoencoders \citep{makhzani2013ksparse,gao2024scaling}; the
fixed active-feature budget makes row sparsity comparable across tokens. Training
uses preprocessed rows
\[
    x_t = \frac{w_t-\mu}{a_t},
\]
where \(a_t=1\) without row normalization. The encoder \(f_\theta\) maps \(x_t\)
to a \(D\)-dimensional pre-code; \(\operatorname{TopK}_k\) keeps the \(k\)
largest entries and decodes with directions \(\widetilde d_i\):
\[
    \widetilde z_t
    =
    \operatorname{TopK}_k(f_\theta(x_t)),
    \qquad
    \widehat{x}_t
    =
    \sum_{i=1}^{D}\widetilde z_{t,i}\widetilde d_i .
\]
The objective is row reconstruction in this preprocessed space,
\[
    \min_{\theta,\{\widetilde d_i\}}
    \frac{1}{|\mathcal{V}|}
    \sum_{t\in\mathcal{V}}
    \left\|x_t-\widehat{x}_t\right\|_2^2 .
\]
After fitting, we convert back to raw readout space,
\[
    z_{t,i}=a_t\widetilde z_{t,i},
    \qquad
    d_i=\widetilde d_i,
    \qquad
    r_t=a_t(x_t-\widehat{x}_t).
\]
This conversion puts all subsequent decompositions in original logit or contrast
units, with no additional dense correction term. Width \(D\) and native sparsity
\(k\) are selected by held-out reconstruction and readout-replacement fidelity;
appendices~\ref{app:readout-factorizer-sweeps}--%
\ref{app:readout-factorizer-diagnostics-reproducibility} give details.

\subsection{Projecting Hidden States onto Readout Features}
\label{sec:method-feature-projections}

Once the readout feature directions \(d_i\) are fixed, a hidden state supplies
only projections onto those directions. No scalar target is used in this step,
so the same projection vector can serve different local decompositions once
targets supply different coefficients. Let \(Z\in
\R^{|\mathcal{V}|\times D}\) collect the sparse row codes and let
\(D_{\mathrm{feat}}\in\R^{D\times d}\) have rows \(d_i^\top\), with
\(\mathbf{1}\) denoting the all-ones column vector. Then the reconstructed LM
head and reconstructed logit vector are
\[
    \begin{aligned}
    \widehat{\WU}
    &=
    \mathbf{1}\mu^\top
    +
    ZD_{\mathrm{feat}},\\
    \widehat{\ell}(h)
    &=
    \mathbf{1}(\mu^\top h)
    +
    Z(D_{\mathrm{feat}}h).
    \end{aligned}
\]
For a hidden state \(h\), define
\[
    p_i(h)=h^\top d_i,
    \qquad
    p(h)=D_{\mathrm{feat}}h .
\]
The vector \(p(h)\) is the readout feature projection vector before target
selection. It is target-independent: a coordinate is not yet support or
opposition for any score. A coordinate becomes a signed term only after the
target supplies a coefficient. Full readout replacement tests
\(\widehat{\ell}(h)\), while local contributions below are target-conditioned.
Quantitative reconstructions use the full feature sum, while plots may truncate
small bars for readability.

\subsection{Scalar Readout Targets}
\label{sec:problem-setup}

A scalar readout target tells SRP which linear score to decompose and which sign
counts as support. In this paper, every target is a linear combination of
unembedding rows. Under this restriction, the same row factorization decomposes
token logits, pairwise contrasts, group scores, and margins, always with an
explicit residual.

Formally, let \(\alpha\in\R^{|\mathcal{V}|}\) be a vector of vocabulary-row
coefficients and define
\[
    q_\alpha = \sum_{t\in\mathcal{V}} \alpha_t w_t,
    \qquad
    s_\alpha(h)=h^\top q_\alpha .
\]
The token-logit target recovers the familiar logit-lens coordinate
\[
    \ell_A(h)=h^\top w_A,
\]
where \(\alpha_A=1\) and all other coefficients are zero. Token logits can be
used directly, but contrasts often reduce sensitivity to shared output-embedding
and tokenizer row structure
\citep{press2017outputembedding,inan2017tying,yang2018softmaxbottleneck,bostrom2020bpe}.
We write
\(\bar{w}_{\mathcal{V}}=|\mathcal{V}|^{-1}\sum_{t\in\mathcal{V}}w_t\) for the
vocabulary-mean row.

Target choice fixes the sign convention: support and opposition are always
relative to the selected token, contrast, group, or margin.

A pairwise logit difference,
\[
    m_{A,B}(h)=h^\top(w_A-w_B),
\]
is positive when the readout favors token \(A\) over token \(B\); shared offsets
cancel and the sign encodes the favored token. For answer, action, or abstention
targets represented by multiple token rows, we use group contrasts. For nonempty
\(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathcal{V}\), write
\[
    \bar{w}_{\mathcal{A}}
    =
    \frac{1}{|\mathcal{A}|}\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}w_a .
\]
The group contrast is
\[
    q_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}}
    =
    \bar{w}_{\mathcal{A}}-\bar{w}_{\mathcal{B}} .
\]
This keeps the target linear while reducing dependence on one tokenizer row; the
vocabulary mean gives a reference contrast. When the relevant alternative is the
model's local ranking, we use top-competitor margins
\(w_u-\sum_{r\in\mathcal{R}}\pi_r w_r\), with selected token \(u\), competitor
set \(\mathcal{R}\) fixed from the original LM-head ranking before SRP
decomposition, and fixed weights \(\sum_{r\in\mathcal{R}}\pi_r=1\).

The scalar \(s_\alpha(h)\) is the unit of a local score decomposition.

\subsection{Local Score Decomposition}
\label{sec:method-local-score}

After both hidden state \(h\) and target \(q_\alpha\) are fixed, the local score
identity turns target-independent projections into target-conditioned signed
terms. All decompositions use raw readout coordinates, and the equality includes
the residual.

    \centering

    positive bars support \(A\)
    over \(B\), negative bars support \(B\) over \(A\). At hidden state \(h\), each

    projection; hatching marks
    reconstruction error. Raw token-logit targets also include a shared offset.}
    \label{fig:readout-query-schematic}
\end{figure}

Fix a hidden state \(h\) and any scalar readout target
\(q_\alpha=\sum_{t\in\mathcal{V}}\alpha_t w_t\). Substituting the SAE
reconstruction gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
    s_\alpha(h)
    &= h^\top q_\alpha \\
    &=
    \left(\sum_{t\in\mathcal{V}} \alpha_t\right) h^\top\mu \\
    &\quad + \sum_{i=1}^{D}
        \left(\sum_{t\in\mathcal{V}} \alpha_t z_{t,i}\right) h^\top d_i \\
    &\quad + h^\top \sum_{t\in\mathcal{V}} \alpha_t r_t .
\end{aligned}
\]
For readout feature \(i\), define its coefficient for the chosen target and its
signed feature contribution as
\[
    \beta_i(\alpha)=\sum_{t\in\mathcal{V}} \alpha_t z_{t,i},
    \qquad
    c_i(h,\alpha)=\beta_i(\alpha)\,p_i(h).
\]
Here \(\beta_i(\alpha)\) is fixed by the target and readout SAE, while
\(p_i(h)=h^\top d_i\) varies with context; when \(q=q_\alpha\) is clear, we write
\(\beta_i(q)\). Only their product is a local contribution. Positive terms raise
the chosen scalar, and negative terms lower it. The projection vector alone is
target-independent (Appendix~\ref{app:global-readout-feature-profiles}).

\subsection{Residual Diagnostics and Target Instantiations}
\label{sec:method-residual-diagnostics}

The residual term answers whether the sparse feature terms preserve the selected
readout score closely enough to interpret. Let
\(s_{\mathrm{exact}}=s_\alpha(h)\),
\(s_\mu=(\sum_t\alpha_t)h^\top\mu\),
\(s_{\mathrm{feat}}=\sum_i c_i(h,\alpha)\), and
\(s_{\mathrm{recon}}=s_\mu+s_{\mathrm{feat}}\). The reconstruction error is
\[
    \epsilon
    =
    s_{\mathrm{exact}}-s_{\mathrm{recon}}
    =
    h^\top\sum_{t\in\mathcal{V}}\alpha_t r_t .
\]
This residual is the target-reconstruction diagnostic: small \(|\epsilon|\)
means the displayed bars reconstruct the chosen score, while large
\(|\epsilon|\) marks incomplete sparse accounting for that target and hidden
state. Figures report the original target score, feature sum, offset when
present, and reconstruction error. For signed contrasts, sign preservation is
also required for support and opposition to be readable.

\Cref{tab:method-special-cases} lists \(\beta_i(\alpha)\) for the target
families. Write
\(\bar{z}_{\mathcal{A},i}=|\mathcal{A}|^{-1}\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}z_{a,i}\)
for the uniform average of feature \(i\)'s sparse coefficient over token group
\(\mathcal{A}\). For top-competitor margins, write
\(\bar{w}_{\pi,\mathcal{R}}=\sum_{r\in\mathcal{R}}\pi_r w_r\) and
\(\bar{z}_{\pi,\mathcal{R},i}=\sum_{r\in\mathcal{R}}\pi_r z_{r,i}\).

For reference contrasts, pairwise differences, group contrasts, and competitor
margins, coefficients sum to zero and the shared offset cancels. Positive values
support the first side; negative values support the comparison side. We use a
local score decomposition for interpretation when the reconstructed score has
small residual or relative error and, for signed targets, preserves the exact
score's sign; experiments compare these diagnostics against baselines.

Feature labels in contribution plots summarize high-coefficient vocabulary rows,
but quantitative claims remain tied to feature id, signed contribution, and
residual.
Because unembedding rows reflect lexical, frequency, tokenizer, and row-geometry
effects, labels are descriptive aids rather than ground-truth properties; appendix
checks follow sparse-feature evaluation practice
\citep{karvonen2025saebench,makelov2024principled}.
