\section{Method: Sparse Readout Prism}
\label{sec:method}

SRP separates two objects that lens-style readouts usually collapse: a
target-independent sparse basis for the LM head and a target-specific signed
account of one selected score. This section defines the row factorization, the
hidden-state projections onto readout features, the scalar readout targets that
select signed terms, and the residual diagnostics used to decide when an account
is readable.

\subsection{Sparse Factorization of the Readout}
\label{sec:method-factorization}

Throughout, ``readout'' means the final unembedding or LM head
\(\WU\in\R^{|\mathcal{V}|\times d}\). For hidden state \(h\in\R^d\), row \(t\)
is \(w_t^\top\), and the full logit vector is \(\WU h\).

We call the SAE trained on unembedding rows the readout SAE. It follows
sparse-autoencoder practice
\citep{bricken2023monosemanticity,cunningham2023sparse,gao2024scaling} and
approximates each unembedding row. In the raw readout coordinates used for all
score accounts, the fitted row and residual satisfy
\[
    \widehat{w}_t = \mu + \sum_{i=1}^{D} z_{t,i} d_i,
    \qquad
    r_t = w_t-\widehat{w}_t,
\]
where \(\mu\) is a shared offset, \(d_i\in\R^d\) is a decoder direction,
\(z_{t,i}\) is token row \(t\)'s sparse coefficient, and \(r_t\) is the residual.
Thus
\[
    w_t=\mu+\sum_i z_{t,i}d_i+r_t .
\]
The residual is the only approximation term in later score decompositions.
Stacking fitted rows yields \(\widehat{\WU}\); Appendix~\ref{app:score-accounting}
details centering and row normalization.

We fit TopK sparse autoencoders
\citep{makhzani2013ksparse,gao2024scaling}; the fixed active-feature budget
simplifies score accounting. Before training, reported models center and
optionally row-normalize readout rows:
\[
    x_t = \frac{w_t-\mu}{a_t},
\]
where \(a_t=1\) without row normalization. The encoder keeps the \(k\) largest
entries of a \(D\)-dimensional pre-code and decodes with directions
\(\widetilde d_i\):
\[
    \widetilde z_t
    =
    \operatorname{TopK}_k(f_\theta(x_t)),
    \qquad
    \widehat{x}_t
    =
    \sum_{i=1}^{D}\widetilde z_{t,i}\widetilde d_i .
\]
The objective is row reconstruction,
\[
    \min_{\theta,\{\widetilde d_i\}}
    \frac{1}{|\mathcal{V}|}
    \sum_{t\in\mathcal{V}}
    \left\|x_t-\widehat{x}_t\right\|_2^2 .
\]
After fitting, we convert the learned representation back to raw readout
space by setting
\[
    z_{t,i}=a_t\widetilde z_{t,i},
    \qquad
    d_i=\widetilde d_i,
    \qquad
    r_t=a_t(x_t-\widehat{x}_t).
\]
All subsequent decompositions are therefore in original logit or contrast units,
with no additional dense correction term. Width \(D\) and native sparsity \(k\)
are selected by held-out reconstruction and readout-replacement fidelity;
appendices~\ref{app:readout-factorizer-sweeps}--%
\ref{app:readout-factorizer-diagnostics-reproducibility} give details.

\subsection{Readout Reconstruction and Feature Projections}
\label{sec:method-feature-projections}

The fitted sparse codes and decoder directions also map hidden states to
target-independent readout-feature coordinates. Let \(Z\in
\R^{|\mathcal{V}|\times D}\) collect the sparse row codes and let
\(D_{\mathrm{feat}}\in\R^{D\times d}\) have rows \(d_i^\top\). Then
\[
    \begin{aligned}
    \widehat{\WU}
    &=
    \mathbf{1}\mu^\top
    +
    ZD_{\mathrm{feat}},\\
    \widehat{\ell}(h)
    &=
    \mathbf{1}(\mu^\top h)
    +
    Z(D_{\mathrm{feat}}h).
    \end{aligned}
\]
For a hidden state \(h\), define
\[
    p_i(h)=h^\top d_i,
    \qquad
    p(h)=D_{\mathrm{feat}}h .
\]
The vector \(p(h)\) is the readout feature projection vector before any token,
contrast, or scalar readout target is selected. A coordinate becomes support or
opposition only after the target supplies a coefficient for that feature. Full
readout replacement tests held-out readout behavior; local contributions below
form target-conditioned accounts. Quantitative reconstructions use the full
feature sum, while plots may truncate small bars for readability.

\subsection{Scalar Readout Targets}
\label{sec:problem-setup}

A scalar readout target is the linear scalar quantity to decompose.

Formally, let \(\alpha\in\R^{|\mathcal{V}|}\) be a vector of vocabulary-row
coefficients and define
\[
    q_\alpha = \sum_{t\in\mathcal{V}} \alpha_t w_t,
    \qquad
    s_\alpha(h)=h^\top q_\alpha .
\]
The familiar logit-lens coordinate is the token-logit special case
\[
    \ell_A(h)=h^\top w_A,
\]
where \(\alpha_A=1\) and all other coefficients are zero. Token logits can be
used directly, but contrasts often reduce sensitivity to shared output-embedding
and tokenizer row structure
\citep{press2017outputembedding,inan2017tying,yang2018softmaxbottleneck,bostrom2020bpe}.
We write
\(\bar{w}_{\mathcal{V}}=|\mathcal{V}|^{-1}\sum_{t\in\mathcal{V}}w_t\) for the
vocabulary-mean row.

Target choice fixes the sign convention: support and opposition are always
relative to the selected token, contrast, group, or margin.

A pairwise logit difference,
\[
    m_{A,B}(h)=h^\top(w_A-w_B),
\]
asks why the readout supports token \(A\) over token \(B\); shared offsets cancel
and the sign encodes the favored token. For answer, action, or abstention
behaviors spread across tokens, we use group contrasts. For nonempty
\(\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{V}\), write
\[
    \bar{w}_{\mathcal{A}}
    =
    \frac{1}{|\mathcal{A}|}\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}w_a .
\]
The group contrast is
\[
    q_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}}
    =
    \bar{w}_{\mathcal{A}}-\bar{w}_{\mathcal{B}} .
\]
This keeps the target linear while reducing dependence on one tokenizer row; the
vocabulary mean gives a reference contrast. We also use top-competitor margins
\(w_u-\sum_{r\in\mathcal{R}}\pi_r w_r\), with competitor set \(\mathcal{R}\)
and weights \(\pi\) fixed before evaluation from the original LM-head ranking.

The scalar \(s_\alpha(h)\) is the unit of a local score account.

\subsection{Local Score Decomposition}
\label{sec:method-local-score}

The local score identity turns the projection vector into a signed account after
fixing both hidden state \(h\) and target \(q_\alpha\); all decompositions use
raw readout coordinates.

    \centering

    \vspace{-0.8\baselineskip}
    \caption{\textbf{Local score account schematic.}
    A centered or contrast readout target is decomposed into readout features learned
    from unembedding rows. For \(w_A-w_B\), \(A\) and \(B\) are the competing
    token or token-set sides: positive bars support \(A\) over \(B\), negative bars
    support \(B\) over \(A\). At hidden state \(h\), each signed term is a target
    coefficient times \(h\)'s readout feature projection; hatching marks
    reconstruction error. Raw token-logit targets also include a shared offset.}
    \label{fig:readout-query-schematic}
\end{figure}

Fix a hidden state \(h\) and any scalar readout target
\(q_\alpha=\sum_{t\in\mathcal{V}}\alpha_t w_t\). Substituting the SAE
reconstruction gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
    s_\alpha(h)
    &= h^\top q_\alpha \\
    &=
    \left(\sum_{t\in\mathcal{V}} \alpha_t\right) h^\top\mu \\
    &\quad + \sum_{i=1}^{D}
        \left(\sum_{t\in\mathcal{V}} \alpha_t z_{t,i}\right) h^\top d_i \\
    &\quad + h^\top \sum_{t\in\mathcal{V}} \alpha_t r_t .
\end{aligned}
\]
For SAE feature \(i\), define its coefficient for the chosen target and its
signed feature contribution as
\[
    \beta_i(\alpha)=\sum_{t\in\mathcal{V}} \alpha_t z_{t,i},
    \qquad
    c_i(h,\alpha)=\beta_i(\alpha)\,p_i(h).
\]
The coefficient \(\beta_i(\alpha)\) is fixed by the target and readout SAE,
whereas the projection \(p_i(h)=h^\top d_i\) changes with context. When
\(q=q_\alpha\) is clear, we write \(\beta_i(q)\). The product separates the
readout/target-side factor \(\beta_i(q)\), the context-side projection
\(p_i(h)\), and the signed local contribution \(c_i(h,\alpha)\). A feature
matters locally when both factors are large. Positive terms raise the chosen
scalar; negative terms lower it. Projection profiles alone are target
independent (Appendix~\ref{app:global-readout-feature-profiles}).

\subsection{Residual Diagnostics and Target Instantiations}
\label{sec:method-faithfulness}

The residual term determines whether a local score account is low-error enough
to interpret. Let \(s_{\mathrm{exact}}=s_\alpha(h)\),
\(s_\mu=(\sum_t\alpha_t)h^\top\mu\),
\(s_{\mathrm{feat}}=\sum_i c_i(h,\alpha)\), and
\(s_{\mathrm{recon}}=s_\mu+s_{\mathrm{feat}}\). The reconstruction error is
\[
    \epsilon
    =
    s_{\mathrm{exact}}-s_{\mathrm{recon}}
    =
    h^\top\sum_{t\in\mathcal{V}}\alpha_t r_t .
\]
This residual is the target-reconstruction diagnostic for a local account: small
\(|\epsilon|\) means the displayed bars account for the chosen score, while large
\(|\epsilon|\) marks an incomplete sparse account. Figures report the original
target score, feature sum, offset when present, and reconstruction error. For
signed contrasts, sign preservation makes support and opposition readable.

\Cref{tab:method-special-cases} lists \(\beta_i(\alpha)\) for the target
families. Write
\(\bar{z}_{\mathcal{A},i}=|\mathcal{A}|^{-1}\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}z_{a,i}\)
for the uniform average of feature \(i\)'s sparse coefficient over token group
\(\mathcal{A}\). For top-competitor margins, write
\(\bar{w}_{\pi,\mathcal{R}}=\sum_{r\in\mathcal{R}}\pi_r w_r\) and
\(\bar{z}_{\pi,\mathcal{R},i}=\sum_{r\in\mathcal{R}}\pi_r z_{r,i}\).

For reference contrasts, pairwise differences, group contrasts, and competitor
margins, coefficients sum to zero and the shared offset cancels. Positive values
support the first side; negative values support the comparison side. Replacement
fidelity, reconstruction error, sign agreement, relative error, and baselines
identify interpretable regimes.

Contribution-plot labels summarize high-coefficient vocabulary rows for a
feature; the measured quantities are feature id and signed contribution. Because
unembedding rows reflect semantic, frequency, tokenizer, and row-geometry
effects, labels are descriptive aids rather than ground-truth semantics; appendix
controls follow sparse-feature evaluation practice
\citep{karvonen2025saebench,makelov2024principled}.
