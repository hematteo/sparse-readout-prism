\section{Method: Sparse Readout Prism}
\label{sec:method}

We take \(h\in\R^d\) to be the residual-stream vector after the model's final
normalization (LayerNorm or RMSNorm), the input the unembedding multiplies; the
logit for token \(t\) is then the exact bilinear identity \(\ell_t(h)=h^\top w_t\),
with normalization folded into \(h\) so the readout under analysis is \(\WU\)
alone. Stacking rows, the dense LM head produces \(\ell(h)=\WU h\). The captured
\(h\) reproduces the model readout with no additional post-readout transform
(Gemma's logit softcap is the one model-specific exception, handled in
Appendix~\ref{app:readout-factorizer-diagnostics-reproducibility}), so every
additive account below sums to the model's own score rather than an
approximation of it. SRP supplies four operations: factorize the LM head into
a sparse readout basis (\S\ref{sec:method-factorization}), project hidden
states onto the resulting directions (\S\ref{sec:method-feature-projections}),
decompose any selected readout score into per-feature contributions plus
residual (\S\ref{sec:method-local-score}), and compare the resulting feature
accounts across contexts, tokens, or fitted lenses under a fixed reading rule
(\S\ref{sec:method-comparing-accounts}). The projection step is
score-agnostic; the local decomposition is score-conditioned.

\subsection{Factorizing the LM Head into Sparse Readout Features}
\label{sec:method-factorization}

SRP fits a sparse dictionary to the rows of \(\WU\). Following
sparse-autoencoder practice~\citep{bricken2023monosemanticity,cunningham2023sparse,gao2024scaling},
each row decomposes as
\(w_t = \widehat{w}_t + r_t\) with
\(\widehat{w}_t = \mu + \textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{D} z_{t,i} d_i\),
where \(\mu\) is a shared offset, \(d_i\in\R^d\) is a readout feature direction
(an SAE decoder direction), \(z_{t,i}\) is token row \(t\)'s sparse coefficient,
\(\widehat{w}_t\) is the fitted row, and \(r_t\) is the residual.
We call this dictionary a \emph{readout factorizer}, or a \emph{readout
SAE} when its training objective matters: its codes describe how each
vocabulary row decomposes into shared directions, not how each hidden state
does, as in activation SAEs.

We fit TopK sparse autoencoders~\citep{makhzani2013ksparse,gao2024scaling}: the
encoder retains only the \(k\) largest pre-activations per row, fixing row
sparsity at \(k\) for comparability across tokens. Any centering or
row-normalization applied during training is inverted after fitting, so
\(\widehat{w}_t\), \(d_i\), \(z_{t,i}\), and \(r_t\) all lie in the raw row
space of \(w_t\)---in original logit units, with no hidden dense correction. Dictionary width,
sparsity, preprocessing, and score-decomposition details are in
Appendices~\ref{app:readout-factorizer-sweeps}--%
\ref{app:readout-factorizer-diagnostics-reproducibility} and
\ref{app:score-decomposition}.

\subsection{Readout Reconstruction and Feature Projections}
\label{sec:method-feature-projections}

Once the readout feature directions \(d_i\) are fixed, any hidden state can be
projected onto them before a token logit, contrast, or margin is selected. Let
\(Z\in\R^{|\mathcal{V}|\times D}\) collect the sparse row codes and let
\(D_{\mathrm{feat}}\in\R^{D\times d}\) have rows \(d_i^\top\), with
\(\mathbf{1}\) denoting the all-ones column vector. Stacking fitted rows yields
the reconstructed LM head and reconstructed logit vector,
\[
    \begin{aligned}
    \widehat{\WU}
    &=
    \mathbf{1}\mu^\top
    +
    ZD_{\mathrm{feat}},\\
    \widehat{\ell}(h)
    &=
    \mathbf{1}(\mu^\top h)
    +
    Z(D_{\mathrm{feat}}h).
    \end{aligned}
\]
For a hidden state \(h\), define the readout feature projection
coordinate \(p_i(h)=h^\top d_i\) and projection vector
\(p(h)=D_{\mathrm{feat}}h\). The coordinate \(p_i(h)\) records the alignment of \(h\) with readout feature
\(i\). The projection vector \(p(h)=D_{\mathrm{feat}}h\) is the inner term of
the reconstructed logit \(\widehat{\ell}(h)\) above: score-agnostic and
reusable across prompts, layers, and residual-stream components, while the
row codes \(Z\) weight it into per-token logits only once a score is
selected.

\subsection{Decomposing Selected Readout Scores}
\label{sec:method-local-score}

A selected readout score is a fixed linear combination of LM-head rows.
Selecting one fixes, before any feature term is read, where the hidden state is
taken (source and position) and what defines the score (token ids, coefficient
vector, and score family), together with the tokenization filters, competitors,
references, and split assignments that frame the contrast. Let
\(\alpha\in\R^{|\mathcal{V}|}\) be vocabulary-row coefficients and define
the selected readout direction and score
\(q_\alpha = \textstyle\sum_{t\in\mathcal{V}} \alpha_t w_t\) and
\(s_\alpha(h)=h^\top q_\alpha\). Setting \(\alpha_A=1\) and all other
coefficients to zero recovers the familiar logit-lens coordinate
\(\ell_A(h)=h^\top w_A\) for vocabulary token \(A\in\mathcal{V}\).

Contrasts cancel score components shared across the compared rows, including
output-embedding and tokenizer-row structure
\citep{press2017outputembedding,inan2017tying,yang2018softmaxbottleneck,bostrom2020bpe};
for two tokens \(A,B\in\mathcal{V}\), the pairwise logit difference
\(m_{A,B}(h)=h^\top(w_A-w_B)\) is positive when the readout favors token
\(A\) over token \(B\). For token groups, write
\(\bar{w}_{\mathcal{A}}=\tfrac{1}{|\mathcal{A}|}\textstyle\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}w_a\).
For top-competitor margins, the target row, competitor set, and nonnegative
weights (summing to one) are taken from the original LM-head ranking computed
before SRP is applied. This stops the reconstructed readout from choosing the
comparison set it is later evaluated against.

Fix a hidden state \(h\) and any selected readout direction
\(q_\alpha=\sum_{t\in\mathcal{V}}\alpha_t w_t\). Substituting the fitted
reconstruction row-by-row and exchanging the sums gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
    s_\alpha(h)
    =
    \Big(\textstyle\sum_t \alpha_t\Big) h^\top\mu
    &+ \sum_{i=1}^{D}
        \Big(\textstyle\sum_t \alpha_t z_{t,i}\Big) h^\top d_i \\
    &+ h^\top \textstyle\sum_t \alpha_t r_t
\end{aligned}
\]
(full expansion in Appendix~\ref{app:score-decomposition}).
The shared-offset term \(\left(\sum_t\alpha_t\right) h^\top\mu\) vanishes
whenever the coefficients sum to zero, which covers logit differences, group
and vocabulary-mean contrasts, and top-competitor margins; for these scores,
the decomposition reduces to feature contributions plus residual.
For readout feature \(i\), define its direction coefficient and signed feature
contribution:
\[
    \beta_i(\alpha)=\sum_{t\in\mathcal{V}} \alpha_t z_{t,i},
    \qquad
    c_i(h,\alpha)=\beta_i(\alpha)\,p_i(h).
\]
Here \(\beta_i(\alpha)\) is fixed by the selected direction and the readout
factorizer, while \(p_i(h)\) varies with context; when \(q=q_\alpha\) is clear, we
write \(\beta_i(q)\). A feature contributes locally only when both factors are
large in magnitude (Appendix~\ref{app:global-readout-feature-profiles}).

We label the components
\(s_{\mu}=(\textstyle\sum_t\alpha_t)h^\top\mu\),
\(s_{\mathrm{feat}}=\textstyle\sum_i c_i(h,\alpha)\),
\(s_{\mathrm{recon}}=s_{\mu}+s_{\mathrm{feat}}\), and
\(s_{\mathrm{exact}}=s_\alpha(h)\), with residual
\(\epsilon=s_{\mathrm{exact}}-s_{\mathrm{recon}}
=h^\top\sum_{t\in\mathcal{V}}\alpha_t r_t\).
Table~\ref{tab:method-special-cases}
(Appendix~\ref{app:score-decomposition}) instantiates \(q_\alpha\) and
\(\beta_i(\alpha)\) for the score families used in our analyses.

\subsection{Comparing and Reading Feature Accounts}
\label{sec:method-comparing-accounts}

Because the basis is fixed by the weights, decompositions taken at
different contexts, layers, tokens, or selected scores land in one
coordinate system and can be compared term by term. We compare accounts
operationally: two readings agree at the feature level when the same
feature id dominates both signed contribution profiles (or when their
profiles correlate, where stated). The same operation extends to fitted
vocabulary lenses: when a lens maps an intermediate state into the space
the LM head reads, the score the lens reports is itself a selected
readout score on the transported state, and SRP decomposes it in the
shared basis. We claim this comparison only for the lens construction
tested in \Cref{sec:beyond-token}, whose implemented form feeds the
unembedding directly; we do not claim a general transport calculus for
arbitrary lens families.

\paragraph{Reading rule.}
\label{sec:method-reading-local-accounts}
Adding the residual back to the reconstructed score recovers the exact score, so
the decomposition always balances arithmetically; whether it can be interpreted
is a separate question that four checks decide. The \emph{replacement check} is
global: it asks whether substituting \(\widehat{\WU}\) for \(\WU\) preserves
model-facing behavior, measured by rowEV together with top-token agreement and
KL on held-out hidden states (\S\ref{subsec:readout-replacement-fidelity}), since
a factorizer can match rowEV yet still miss the specific logits being
interpreted. The remaining three turn to the selected score. For each one we
report exact and reconstructed scores, feature sum, offset when present,
residual, and relative reconstruction error
\[
    \rho(h,\alpha)=
    \frac{|s_{\mathrm{exact}}-s_{\mathrm{recon}}|}
         {|s_{\mathrm{exact}}|+0.5}.
\]
The additive constant keeps \(\rho\) finite at near-tie scores, where the
criterion tightens from a relative to an absolute bound (reconstruction
error below \(0.25\) logit units at the \(\rho<0.5\) gate). Identity
checks, case-study displays, and every baseline comparison instead report
the unfloored ratio
\(\tilde\rho(h,\alpha)=
|s_{\mathrm{exact}}-s_{\mathrm{recon}}|/|s_{\mathrm{exact}}|\);
the two are never mixed in one table, and \(\tilde\rho\) upper-bounds the
floored \(\rho\), so a threshold passed under \(\tilde\rho\) also passes
the gate.
The \emph{score} and \emph{residual} checks read feature bars only when
\(\rho<0.5\); the \emph{sign check} additionally requires \(s_{\mathrm{exact}}\)
and \(s_{\mathrm{recon}}\) to share sign before support or opposition is read,
because a small \(\rho\) bounds the size of the reconstruction error but not its
direction, and direction is what the sign decides. Displayed bars may be
truncated, but feature sums and residuals always use the full decomposition. We
therefore tie claims to feature ids, signed contributions, and residuals, with
labels used as summaries \citep{karvonen2025saebench,makelov2024principled}.
