\section{Readout Features Behind a Lens Score}
\label{sec:worked-example}

We open with the result the rest of the paper calibrates and generalizes.
Lens-style readouts name a favored token or contrast but leave it as a dense
LM-head dot product; SRP holds that score fixed and decomposes it into signed
readout-feature terms plus an explicit residual, in logit units. The opening
\texttt{bug}-vs-\texttt{insect} margin (\Cref{fig:main-lens-prism-comparison})
already shows the mechanic: software-defect features carry the margin and no
insect-sense feature appears among the leading terms (feature sum \(+20.43\)
against the exact \(+20.37\), residual \(-0.06\)).

The account reorganizes with the score and the context.
\Cref{fig:selected-readout-prism-examples} places four polysemous logit
differences side by side. In an insect context, \texttt{bug} draws positive
contributions from mosquito-, bee-, and beetle-labeled rows and negative
contributions from error-token rows (\(\rho=0.185\)); in a software context,
the positive contributions shift to defect, crash, and debug rows
(\(\rho=0.003\)). The \texttt{bark}-vs-\texttt{dog} and
\texttt{bass}-vs-\texttt{trout} cases show the same reassignment
(\(\rho=0.126\) and \(0.097\); all four unfloored), with residuals reported
because they bound the interpretation. The same surface token recruits different
row-labeled feature groups as score and context change---a reorganization
invisible to the scalar margin.

Two questions decide whether such readings can be trusted and what they buy.
Whether the feature terms can be read at all turns on the fidelity of the
factorization on the selected score: \Cref{sec:results} settles this across
eight models and the score families that arise in practice, fixing the
low-error regime (\(\rho<0.5\)) the readings require.
\Cref{sec:readout-analyses} then treats this competition finding
systematically---with a label audit---next to context reweighting, composition
with component attribution, and constrained readout-side edits.
