\section{Introduction}

Transformer language models map a final hidden state \(h\in\R^d\) to next-token logits through an unembedding matrix \(\WU\in\R^{|\mathcal{V}|\times d}\)---the \emph{LM head}, or \emph{readout}. Row \(w_t\) is the learned direction whose dot product with \(h\) scores token \(t\). Lens-style methods~\citep{nostalgebraist2020,belrose2023tuned,pal2023future,ghandeharioun2024patchscopes} reuse this map on intermediate hidden states, exposing vocabulary logits, token rankings, or distributions. Yet once a token or contrast is selected, the inspected object remains a single dense dot product \(h^\top q\), where \(q\) is an LM-head row or row combination.

Consider one hidden state from a source-verification prompt: does the readout
favor the source-checking token \texttt{verify} over the assumption token
\texttt{assume}? The ordinary logit lens reports the signed margin
(Figure~\ref{fig:main-lens-prism-comparison}A) but is silent on its
composition---which readout directions support it, which oppose it, and how
much remains unexplained by any shared basis. This gap is generic to lens
scores: they report what a hidden state reads out, not which readout
directions produce the score.

Decompositions commonly paired with lens-style readouts act on the hidden-state side. Direct logit attribution (DLA) decomposes \(h=\sum_c h_c\) into contributions from earlier residual-stream components---the embedding, attention heads, and MLP layers---and reads off each component's contribution to a chosen logit~\citep{elhage2021framework,wang2022ioi,nguyen2024logitprisms}. Sparse autoencoders (SAEs) and related dictionary-learning methods learn overcomplete bases that reconstruct each input from a few active coordinates~\citep{makhzani2013ksparse,bricken2023monosemanticity,cunningham2023sparse,gao2024scaling}; applied to hidden activations, they yield sparse coordinates for \(h\). Neither decomposes the readout direction \(q\) itself.

Token logits and logit differences are linear in LM-head rows: \(h^\top(w_A - w_B) = h^\top w_A - h^\top w_B\). A per-feature account in logit units therefore requires decomposing the rows themselves; decomposing \(h\) alone cannot produce row-side coordinates.

Sparse Readout Prism (SRP) applies dictionary learning directly to LM-head rows: each row is written as sparse coefficients over a shared overcomplete basis, plus a residual. We call the shared directions \emph{readout features}---features of LM-head rows, in contrast to SAE features trained on hidden-state activations. Once a \emph{readout score} is fixed---a token logit \(h^\top w_t\), a pairwise logit difference \(h^\top(w_A-w_B)\), or another LM-head row combination---the row-side coefficients combine with hidden-state projections \(p_i(h)=h^\top d_i\) to give signed per-feature contributions in original logit units (Figure~\ref{fig:main-lens-prism-comparison}B). Contributions, offset, and residual sum to the original score.

We demonstrate SRP in two different sets of evaluations. First, we establishes that the factorization is faithful
enough to read from (\Cref{sec:results}). Across eight models, SRP reconstructs unembedding
rows with explained variance \(0.83\)--\(0.89\) after row centering. It
tracks the dense LM head on six of eight readouts and preserves
contrast sign on at least \(91\%\) of held-out logit differences; on
Qwen3.5-2B it outperforms baseline methods such as nearest-row ridge, matched-rank PCA, and
shuffle/random sparse baselines. %These checks define a sign-preserved, low-error subset (relative reconstruction error \(\rho<0.5\))---the reading rule for the rest of the paper.

In addition, we applies SRP within that subset (\Cref{sec:readout-analyses}). Each
token preference reported by a lens becomes a sparse account in
logit units with an explicit residual. Polysemous logit differences
expose competing features; prompt context reweights a fixed
token-row decomposition; direct logit attribution composes with the
basis to give component-by-feature terms. Decoder directions
selected from a small discovery set drive constrained lexical edits
that transfer to held-out tokens.

In summary, this paper makes three contributions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \emph{A sparse factorization of the LM head.} We fit a shared
    overcomplete dictionary directly to unembedding rows; the full head can be
    reconstructed or replaced from the resulting sparse codes.
    \item \emph{Score-agnostic feature projections.} Each learned direction
    yields a per-feature projection of any hidden state, reusable across
    downstream score choices.
    \item \emph{Local score decompositions in logit units.} For any token logit,
    logit difference, or fixed row combination, SRP returns signed per-feature
    contributions plus offset and residual, all in original logit units.
\end{itemize}
