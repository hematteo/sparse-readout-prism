\section{Introduction}

Language models map hidden states to vocabulary logits through the final
unembedding matrix, or LM head; we call this interface the \emph{readout}.
Lens-style methods inspect hidden states in vocabulary space, through the LM head
or through a learned lens
\citep{nostalgebraist2020,belrose2023tuned,pal2023future,ghandeharioun2024patchscopes}.
A logit-lens probe asks: what vocabulary evidence does this hidden state expose?
This view is useful, but each selected logit or contrast remains a dense dot
product with an unembedding row or readout direction: it identifies favored
tokens without showing which shared directions support or oppose the score, or
what residual remains. We introduce Sparse Readout Prism (SRP), a sparse feature
decomposition for logit-lens readouts. SRP factorizes the LM head into sparse
token-row coefficients and shared decoder directions, or \emph{readout
features}. Given a hidden state and scalar readout target, SRP decomposes the
score into positive and negative feature terms plus a reconstruction residual;
we call this a \emph{local score decomposition}.

SRP works on the readout side. A scalar readout target can be a token logit
\(h^\top w_t\), pairwise logit difference \(h^\top(w_A-w_B)\), reference
contrast, group contrast, or top-competitor margin. For a fixed hidden state and
target, the terms are local and checkable against the exact score and residual.

SRP applies sparse-autoencoder and dictionary-learning machinery to the LM head
itself \citep{bricken2023monosemanticity,cunningham2023sparse,gao2024scaling},
factorizing the final unembedding matrix into sparse token-row coefficients and
shared readout directions,
\[
    \WU \approx \mathbf{1}\mu^\top + ZD_{\mathrm{feat}} .
\]
This defines a reusable readout-feature basis before any target is chosen.
Projecting a hidden state \(h\) onto these decoder directions gives a readout
feature projection vector over directions learned from unembedding rows. A
scalar readout target then turns those projections into signed feature
contributions plus a residual.

\Cref{fig:main-lens-prism-comparison} illustrates this identity on a
\texttt{verify}-minus-\texttt{assume} margin: SRP keeps the pairwise target fixed,
assigns positive signed contributions to features labeled by
verification/checking tokens, negative contributions to assumption-related
features, and reports the residual.

The paper contributes:
\begin{itemize}
    \item a sparse dictionary factorization of the final unembedding / LM head
    that defines a reusable readout-feature basis;
    \item a scalar readout-target formalism that turns token scores, token
    contrasts, group contrasts, and margins into signed feature contributions;
    \item fidelity diagnostics for LM-head replacement, target reconstruction,
    sign agreement, relative error, and baselines, identifying when the terms
    preserve the original readout score;
    \item analyses showing how target choice, prompt context, residual-stream
    components, and constrained readout-side edits interact with the same
    readout-feature basis.
\end{itemize}

Diagnostics identify settings where the feature terms preserve selected readout
scores closely enough for local analysis. In the Qwen settings selected by these
checks, SRP preserves held-out contrast signs at \(0.949\)--\(0.958\) with median
relative reconstruction error below \(0.10\).
