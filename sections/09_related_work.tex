\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Lens-style readouts: the interface.}
Lens-style methods expose hidden states through vocabulary distributions,
future-token predictions, continuations, or descriptions
\citep{nostalgebraist2020,belrose2023tuned,pal2023future,ghandeharioun2024patchscopes}.
They are closest to SRP in interface: a hidden state is inspected by asking what
the model can read out from it. Lens variants typically change the inspection
map, the layer being decoded, or the representation being queried. SRP keeps the
lens-style question but changes what happens after a score is selected. It
factorizes the model's own final readout, projects hidden states onto the
resulting readout directions, and uses scalar targets to turn those projections
into signed feature terms. Thus a lens can show that a hidden state favors one
token or contrast, while SRP asks which sparse readout features support, oppose,
or fail to reconstruct that scalar.

\paragraph{Output geometry: the object.}
SRP treats the final unembedding, or LM head, as the object to be factorized.
Output embeddings have long been studied as learned representations and as part
of the geometry that shapes language-model predictions
\citep{press2017outputembedding,inan2017tying,yang2018softmaxbottleneck,bostrom2020bpe}.
This work motivates caution about interpreting raw vocabulary logits: token
rows, weight tying, softmax rank constraints, and vocabulary segmentation all
affect the final scores. SRP makes this geometry explicit by learning sparse
token-row coefficients and shared readout directions for \(\WU\). The resulting
basis is readout-side: target coefficients are fixed by unembedding-row
geometry, while the current context enters through hidden-state projections onto
the learned directions.

\paragraph{Sparse dictionaries: the machinery.}
Activation-SAE work learns dictionaries over hidden-state distributions and has
developed evaluation practice for sparse features
\citep{bricken2023monosemanticity,cunningham2023sparse,gao2024scaling,karvonen2025saebench,makelov2024principled}.
SRP uses sparse-dictionary machinery but changes the fitted object: the training
examples are unembedding rows rather than activation vectors. Its sparse codes
are therefore token-row coefficients, and its decoder directions are readout
directions used by the LM head. A hidden state's coordinate \(h^\top d_i\) is a
projection onto such a direction, not an SAE activation in the usual
activation-SAE sense. This distinction matters because SRP's local terms are
formed only after a scalar readout target supplies the corresponding target
coefficient.

\paragraph{Logit attribution and circuits: the composition.}
Component attribution and logit-prism methods ask where logit evidence is
written in the residual stream
\citep{elhage2021framework,wang2022ioi,nguyen2024logitprisms}. Automated circuit
discovery, causal abstraction, transcoders, and circuit-tracing systems pursue
internal mechanisms and intervention-tested structure
\citep{conmy2023acdc,geiger2023causalabstraction,dunefsky2024transcoders,ameisen2025circuittracing}.
SRP addresses a complementary axis: it factorizes the rows against which the
final hidden state is scored. Because its terms are linear in the readout, they
compose with residual-stream attribution: a signed readout-feature contribution
can be split across residual components in a realized forward pass. This gives
an additive feature-resolved attribution account; causal interventions are still
needed to test component or feature necessity. SRP reports replacement fidelity,
target reconstruction, sign agreement, and residual terms to mark when these
readout-level accounts are interpretable. Broader positioning appears in
Appendix~\ref{app:extended-related-work}.
