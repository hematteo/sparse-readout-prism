# Glossary and Terminology Guide

## 1. Purpose

This file controls terminology for the Sparse Readout Prism paper. Use it to keep the paper precise, internally consistent, and grounded in the mechanistic interpretability, logit-lens, output-embedding, sparse-autoencoder, and attribution literature. It is not a list of every noun phrase in the paper. It focuses on terms that affect claims, positioning, mathematical definitions, experimental interpretation, and reviewer understanding.

Updated 2026-08-30 to the post-metareview register. Where this file and the manuscript disagree, the manuscript wins.

## 2. Terminology Policy

- Prefer standard literature terms where they exist: use "logit lens", "unembedding matrix", "LM head", "sparse autoencoder", "decoder direction", "reconstruction error", "direct logit attribution", "baseline", and "ablation" in their usual senses.
- Use paper-specific terms only when they name a real SRP object or distinction: "Sparse Readout Prism", "readout feature", "readout feature projection", "selected readout direction", "selected readout score", "direction coefficient", "signed contribution", and "local score decomposition".
- Define key terms before relying on them, especially "readout", "selected readout direction", "readout feature projection", "local score decomposition", and "feature label".
- Keep "readout" narrowly scoped to the final unembedding / LM head matrix. Do not use it for the whole model, a prompt, a generation, or an internal computation.
- Avoid calling hidden-state projections onto readout SAE decoder directions "feature activations". In SAE literature, "activation" usually refers to the sparse encoder output for the object on which the SAE was trained.
- Treat "faithful", "interpretable", "robust", "general", "causal", and "control" as evidence-sensitive words. Use them only with the diagnostics, scope, and caveats stated in the paper.
- Keep terminology stable across sections. Do not alternate between "query", "target", "score", "decomposition", and "explanation" unless the distinction is intentional.
- Match wording to evidence: the fixed SRP basis reconstructs and decomposes selected token logits, logit differences, and explicit linear combinations of unembedding rows; it does not by itself explain arbitrary behavior, probabilities, full generations, or causal mechanisms.
- The factorized object has exactly three sanctioned names: "unembedding matrix" `W_U` (mathematical prose), "LM head" (implementation and evaluation), and "readout" (the paper-wide alias). The manuscript christens it "We call this matrix the readout, since the model reads out its prediction from the final state alone." Never call it an "interface" (banned 2026-08-28; earlier versions of this file suggested that word) and never add a fourth synonym ("output matrix" was removed).
- The transformed state consumed by the LM head is the **decoded state** (renamed paper-wide from "readout-space state", 2026-08-30). Do not resurrect the old name.
- Unpack coined hyphenated modifiers into prepositional phrases in paper prose: "ablation on the readout side", "contrasts derived from benchmarks", "the terms for each case", "readouts distilled for reasoning". Hyphens stay only in standard literature compounds (held-out, next-token, hidden-state, tuned-lens style) and in protected places (section titles, label/ref keys, tight table cells). Score families are open compounds: "vocabulary mean contrast", "top competitor margin".
- Any sentence in this file that may be pasted into the manuscript must satisfy the paper prose rules: no colons or semicolons, no "X rather than Y" / "X, not Y" contrastives, no very short sentences, and no self-deflating clauses — say what a mechanism delivers.

These are the highest-value cleanup substitutions to run before submission.

| Search for | Prefer | Reason |
|---|---|---|

## 4. Paper-Specific Terms

Each term below names a real SRP object that no standard term covers. Definitions and usage rules are in §3, first-use sentences in §8, and allowed/disallowed variants in §7. This table records only what those do not: why the paper needs the name, and the reviewer risk it carries.

| Term | Why the paper needs it | Risk to manage |
|---|---|---|
| Sparse Readout Prism (SRP) | Names the factorization and projection basis, keeping score selection explicit as a downstream use. | The name can sound broader than the evidence. Keep claims at decomposition of readout-linear scores. |

## 8. First-Use Definitions

The manuscript's current sentences take precedence over these templates. Every template here follows the paper prose rules, so it can be pasted without introducing a colon, semicolon, or contrastive pairing.

> Each output logit is a dot product between the normalized final state and one row of the unembedding matrix `W_U`, also called the LM head. We call this matrix the **readout**, since the model reads out its prediction from the final state alone.

> Following logit-lens work, we use **lens-style readouts** to refer to methods that inspect hidden states through vocabulary logits, distributions, continuations, or descriptions.

> We introduce **Sparse Readout Prism (SRP)**, a sparse feature decomposition for logit-lens readouts that factorizes the final unembedding / LM head and decomposes selected token logits, logit differences, and contrasts built from explicit combinations of rows into signed feature terms plus explicit residuals.

> The **readout SAE** is a sparse autoencoder trained on unembedding rows, so its sparse codes describe token rows (the manuscript's christening is "We call this dictionary the readout SAE.").

> We refer to the learned decoder directions `d_i` as **readout features** because they are features of the final readout matrix.

> For a decoded state `tilde h_l`, the **readout feature projection** `p_i(tilde h_l)=tilde h_l^T d_i` is its coordinate along readout feature `i` before any token, contrast, or margin has been selected.


> Following logit-attribution work, token logits and logit differences can be written as projections onto unembedding rows or differences of rows, and we use `q_alpha` for any such selected readout direction and `s_alpha(tilde h_l)=tilde h_l^T q_alpha` for its selected readout score.

> A **pairwise logit difference** is the signed contrast `tilde h_l^T(w_A-w_B)`, where positive values support token `A` over token `B`.

> A **group contrast** is a linear contrast between average unembedding rows for two token groups, used when the intended contrast is spread across several token rows.

> For each feature `i`, the **direction coefficient** `beta_i(alpha)=sum_v alpha_v z_{v,i}` records how the selected row coefficients use that readout feature.

> The **signed contribution** is `c_i(tilde h_l,alpha)=beta_i(alpha)p_i(tilde h_l)`, where positive terms push the selected readout score up and negative terms pull it down.

> We call the signed, score-specific decomposition a **local score decomposition** because it decomposes one selected readout score at one decoded state.

> The **reconstruction error** `epsilon=s_exact-s_recon` is the part of the selected readout score not captured by the offset and sparse feature terms.

> For signed contrasts, **sign agreement** means that the reconstructed score has the same sign as the exact score, so the reconstructed decomposition supports the same side of the contrast.

> **LM head replacement fidelity** measures whether substituting the reconstructed readout for the original LM head preserves held-out readout distributions or rankings, such as top-token agreement and KL.

> **Score reconstruction** measures exact-versus-reconstructed selected readout scores and is the primary evidence for interpreting local score decompositions.

> A **feature label** is a readability summary of the unembedding rows most associated with an SAE feature, while the measured objects remain the feature id and its signed contribution.

> We use **feature-resolved DLA** for the additive decomposition that splits residual-stream direct logit attribution terms across SRP readout features.

> A **readout contrast derived from benchmarks** is a linear contrast built from benchmark examples, and it evaluates how well the basis reconstructs the contrast each example induces on the readout.

> A **constrained edit on the readout side** changes specified logits along selected readout SAE decoder directions and is evaluated as an intervention on lexical scores at the readout level.
